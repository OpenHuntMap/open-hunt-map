## Land area description

This area includes Lake Superior from the western end of Black Bay Peninsula to the Pic River. It includes the bed of the lake, islands not designated as provincial park or conservation reserve and any private lands within the Lake Superior Archipelago. The area extends from the Lake Superior shoreline southerly to the international boundary.Existing activities include commercial fishing, sport fishing, transportation, boating, Crown land recreation, cottaging and tourism. Portions of this area are within the municipalities of Terrace Bay and Marathon.Private lands on Channel Island, Cobinosh Island, Paradise Island and Bowman Island are Areas of Natural and Scientific Interest. These areas exhibit a variety of significant earth science features. The bed of Lake Superior is withdrawn from staking and is not part of any forest management unit.This area is located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism. Lake Superior is designated as a tourism lake.

## Land use intent

The primary intent of this area will be to preserve its character for non-structured public recreation activities and to maintain the high aesthetic appeal of the Lake Superior north shore area. Commercial fishing and tourism activities are permitted to continue.Fires on the island portions of this general use area that threaten human life, property or other values will receive a full response. All other fires on the islands will be monitored, and if risks are acceptiable, allowed to burn in order to obtain ecological benefits. Consult the Interim Fire Response Plan for the Islands of Lake Superior (2005) for further direction on fire suppression.Lake Superior General Use Area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Lake Superior General Use Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS) at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .
