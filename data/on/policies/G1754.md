## Land area description

This area is largely inaccessible and extends from the Quebec/Ontario boundary westerly to the Little Abitibi - Abitibi - Onakawana River chain. Development in the area includes a hydraulic generating station, a water control dam, commercial outpost camps, a few cottage sites and the Detour Lake Mine site. Associated with this mine site is a powerline and an access road.Nine Mile Rapids on the Abitibi River has hydraulic power generation site potential. The area is known to contain of high mineral potential (e.g. Detour Lake mine site and along a band running to the west and then southwest from Kesagami Lake). Other significant resource values are the remote hunting and fishing opportunities the area provides and a resident caribou population.This area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.”

## Land use intent

The priority in this area will be shared between resource extraction (e.g. mining and forestry) and commercial recreation (i.e. outpost camps). Commercial tourist outfitting will be encouraged on identified tourism lakes. The maintenance of significant fish and wildlife populations habitat will also be a priority in the area. Hydraulic power production will be a priority at Nine Mile Rapids and for areas with existing facilities.This area includes osprey nests and heron colonies on the Thorning River, Audrey Lake, Agassiz Lake and Big Cedar Creek, native brook trout in Corner Lakes and the Bad River and scattered caribou populations. Management of these areas may include seasonal restrictions, selective harvesting or outright protection. Structural development on and near these features will be discouraged. Where a need can be demonstrated, proposals for these areas will be dealt with on a case-by-case basis. A small portion of this area overlaps with the Burntbush, Two Peaks and Straggler Lakes Areas of the Approved Cochrane District Remote (Wilderness) Tourism Strategy. Management of the overlapped area will be subject to the Approved Cochrane District Remote (Wilderness) Tourism Strategy (1997). Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Cochrane District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
