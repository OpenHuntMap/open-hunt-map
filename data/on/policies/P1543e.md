## Land area description

Missinaibi Provincial Park protects a provincially significant waterway, over 500 kilometres long, with many natural and cultural heritage values. The park stretches from approximately 40 kilometres north of Chapleau, though Mattice on Highway 11, to approximately 50 kilometres southwest of Moosonee, and follows the Missinaibi River through Ecodistricts 2E-2, 3E-1, 3E-2 and 3E-5.Lacustrine deposits with mixed and dense conifer, mixed deciduous and spruce forest dominate the site, either side of Thunderhouse Falls.Missinaibi Lake, over 40 kilometres long, provides car camping at the Barclay Bay campground, as well as boating and canoeing opportunities. Backcountry opportunities for canoeing and angling are found in the Little Missinaibi Lake. Part of Missinaibi Provincial Park is located beyond the Ontario's Living Legacy planning area. The total area of this park is 99,090 hectares.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.The Missinaibi River, a Canadian Heritage River, provides excellent backcountry canoeing and whitewater recreation.Missinaibi Provincial Park is a waterway park and was originally regulated in 1970. An addition was made to the park in 2005 as a result of Ontario’s Living Legacy.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

Missinaibi Provincial Park will be managed according to Provincial Park policy. Missinaibi Provincial Park will protect significant natural, cultural and recreational values along an un-dammed waterway, over 500 kilometres long. The River was an important fur trade route, and is a popular canoe route today. A range of recreational opportunities will be provided, from low-intensity backcountry to high-intensity day-use.The Park will protect the natural, cultural and recreational values that contribute to the Canadian Heritage River System.

## Permitted uses — preface

At that time, significant decisions regarding resource stewardship, operations, development and permitted uses are made.
