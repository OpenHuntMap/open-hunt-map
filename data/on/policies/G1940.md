## Land area description

The Nipissing Game Preserve is comprised of parts of eight townships between Highway 11 and the Ontario Northland Railway in the northern part of the District. Bedrock terrain with shallow soils and sandy outwash plains are the dominant land forms. The Marten River Canoe Route is primarily within the area. There are two access points, one of which is designated, both of which receive significant use. The area is almost entirely comprised of Crown land, with a few private cottage lots. The Himsworth Game Preserve is located between Highway 11 and the Canadian National Railway in North and South Himsworth and East Ferris Townships. The area is mostly forested and is a mixture of Crown and private lands. The Preserve provides protection to a significant local deer yard, waterfowl and moose. Timber production in the Himsworth Preserve is not significant compared with the volume produced in the Nipissing Preserve.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Fish and wildlife management, resource production, recreation and cottaging are existing accepted uses and these will continue. Significant aggregate areas should be kept available for road construction and repair. Any uses which would destroy important winter deer habitat in the Himsworth Preserve will be discouraged. Several sites, where preservation is the main theme of management (e.g. heronries, osprey nests, etc.) are found in this area. Their exact location may be seen in the North Bay District Sensitive Areas Report. These and other such sites, which may be found in the future, will be protected during resource management operations and given special consideration in the Ministry's review of the plans of other agencies. Marten, Poplar, Hammell, Wicksteed and North and South Spruce Lakes are the subject of lake management plans and will be managed according to the spirit and intent of this document and the detailed policies of the approved lake management plans. The local tourist industry relies heavily on some of the area's lakes and this will be encouraged through the management of fish and wildlife and other resources in a multiple use sustained yield manner. Wildlife viewing opportunities in the Game Preserves will be actively promoted. Disposition of Crown land will only be considered on a case-by-case basis with careful consideration of potential impacts on Ministry programs and under the general disposition policy contained in this document. No alienation of Crown land will occur in Sisk, McLaren, Kenny, Gladman, Gooderham and Hammell Townships as a result of this document until the Bear Island Indian Band land caution has been resolved.
