## Land area description

A relatively remote area containing old growth red and white pine and several micro watersheds and tributaries associated with the Spanish River. Although timber operations have accessed the southern and northern edges of the area, there remain remote pockets that have seen little or no human activity other than some hunting and trapping and recreational camps used near Acheson Lake. The area is managed under a sustainable forest licnece (SFL).The major issue in this area revolves around the construction of roads. Fly-in tourist operators, some recreationists, and some wildlife managers are opposed to major road network expansions, while other resource users view road development as necessary for proper resource management and use.This area is located within the City of Greater Sudbury Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

This is an important recreational and resource sector (forestry, mining) commercial tourism area. Minor boundary adjustments may be made to this area through further planning exercises.Land use direction and resource management activities will maintain remote quality of this area and be compatible with protecting the natural and recreational values of the Spanish River Provincial Park.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Espanola District Land Use Guidelines (1983)

## Permitted uses — preface

Remote access enhanced management area are intended to maintain the remote character of selected areas. Typically, these are relatively large areas which provide the public and tourism operators with high-quality remote recreational experiences including hunting, fishing, canoeing, and camping. Given the large size, remoteness, and relative absence of roads, these areas will play a significant role in protecting wilderness values outside the parks and protected areas system.
