## Land area description

Windigo Bay Provincial Park Nature Reserve is 8,378 hectares in size and is located between the northwest shore of Lake Nipigon, the CNR tracks, the Whitesand River on the west and Kenna Creek on the east. The area provides representation of an earth science lacustrine theme and biophysical features typical of the Lake Nipigon Basin; glacial Lake Kelvin shoreline, fine lacustrine sand dunes and a shoreline peatland complex. The area also contains part of a caribou wintering range and migration corridor.This area is located within the Lake Nipigon Basin Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.Windigo Bay was regulated as a provincial park in 1989. It represents earth and life science features of Site District 3W-3.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.This area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.”

## Land use intent

The intent is to manage this park as a nature reserve class provincial park, in accordance with MNR and Ontario Parks Policy.Potential conflicts with an Indian burial ground have been avoided by excluding it from the candidate boundary. Status Indians will be allowed to continue their traditional activities within their treaty area. The area initially excluded from the candidate park pending evaluation of peat resource potential has been re-instated. A secondary seasonal (6month), D class road has been built (completion summer 2003) by the Whitesand First Nation to access the healing lodge at Mount St. John. This area known as 'Old Whitesand,' is not included in the park. Vehicle access will be restricted to members of Whitesand First Nation who are using the healing lodge. No other roads will be permitted within the park. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Nipigon District Land Use Guidelines (1983) and Parks Policy.
