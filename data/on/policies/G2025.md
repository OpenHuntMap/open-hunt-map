## Land area description

This area includes most of Manitoulin Island and the lower portion of the Spanish River valley. The majority of the District's rural population is settled here and most land is patented. The major existing uses are related to agriculture and recreation, however significant aggregate extraction and forest management also occurs. Several resource capabilities are high, although the pattern is mixed and complex. The large majority of this area is covered by existing municipal official plans.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The purpose of this area is to recognize and support the existing activities in the settled, rural areas of the District. The primary management efforts of MNR will be directed towards supporting management of privately owned class 2, 3 and 4 agricultural lands, good quality aggregate resources and high capability recreational areas. Assistance in forest management will also be available for private lands not required for food or aggregate production. Wildlife and fisheries management will be a Ministry priority in certain, specific areas (i.e. the Manitou and Blue Jay Rivers). MNR will actively participate in the protection of specific features such as unique vegetation or geological complexes that are sensitive to development The Carter Bay sand dunes and Vidal Bay beach terraces on Manitoulin Island and the pine plantations in Nairn Township on the mainland are three examples. Crown Land Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Espanola District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
