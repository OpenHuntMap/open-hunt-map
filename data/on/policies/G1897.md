## Land area description

This area encompasses that portion of the North Channel of Lake Huron east of Blind River and includes portions of the shoreline south of Highway 17.The area contains some of the best inland boating and yachting waters in the Province. The Land Use Guidelines emphasize the protection and preservation of the area's high quality recreational features.In addition to boating, camping and day-use recreation, this area is also used for cottaging, angling and commercial fishing. Crown lands on offshore islands have been proposed for regulation as additions to the existing La Cloche Provincial Park. Much of the main land area is captured within the proposed boundaries from the North Channel Inshore Waterway Provincial Park.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The intent is to manage the area for recreational uses such as boating, angling and camping, and to maintain the existing commercial fishery. Important yachting harbours, such as John Harbour and Moiles Harbour will be protected from adverse developments.Land disposition in offshore areas will not be permitted, except for water lots for private or public use, subject to official plans, zoning bylaws and zoning orders, where no adverse effects on Ministry programs are anticipated and subject to the protocol for disposition as per the Wikwemikong Land Claim. Land disposition along the shoreline may be permitted for uses which are complementary to the recreational intent of the area. MNR will ensure however, that sites having potential for public recreational use will remain in public ownership. Crown islands are also subject to the interim protection guidelines put forth through Ontario's Living Legacy.Protection of bird nesting sites will be emphasized.Expansion of existing industrial uses will be permitted where such expansions will not jeopardize recreational or fishery resources. New industrial uses will be discouraged.Lands under the waters of Lake Huron and all islands in this area have been withdrawn from exploration and staking out under the appropriate section of the Mining Act. Onshore mining developments may expand underground, outward under the North Channel, provided this is in accord with existing international agreements.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Blind River and Sudbury District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
