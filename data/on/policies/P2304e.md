## Land area description

This area, regulated as a waterway class Provincial Park in 1989, includes White Otter Lake and the Turtle River, as well as some associated waters. It forms a waterway extending from the mouth of the Turtle River at Little Turtle Lake to Agimak Lake, south of the town of Ignace. The area includes a 200 metre deep strip of land on each side of the Turtle River, White Otter Lake and other waters included in the park. In addition, a managed skyline outside the 200 metre recommended park boundary will be applied where appropriate. The park boundary has been, in some cases, particularly on White Otter Lake, extended beyond the 200 metres to include significant cultural and natural nodes. A portion of the Eagle-Finlayson moraine is also included within the park.Present uses include water-oriented recreation, commercial tourism and trapping. The White Otter Castle on the north end of White Otter Lake is a locally significant tourist attraction. Access to the area is primarily limited to air, boat and snow machine.An old logging road, in extremely poor condition, accesses the south end of Ann Bay on White Otter Lake. A forest access road exists to the north of the park at Dibble Lake, and a tertiary forest access road accesses Robinson Lake.In addition, the Turtle River is crossed by the Highway # 622 just north-west of Pekagoning Lake.Two native pictograph sites are found within the park and the Eltrut/Jones Lake area has significant wild rice stands. Earth science features include loess and lacustrine deposits.The name of this park changed to 'Turtle River-White Otter' on May 22, 2003.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The intent is to manage this park as a waterway class provincial park, in accordance with MNR and Ontario Parks Policy.

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992), the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Interim Management Statement, where available. The Interim Management Statement provides direction to ensure custodial management of park resources until such time as a park management planning process is conducted, which includes public review of proposals. At that time, significant decisions regarding resource stewardship, operations, development and permitted uses are made.
