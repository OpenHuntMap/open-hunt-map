## Land area description

This area encompasses most of the land area within the Schreiber and Terrace Bay municipal boundaries, excluding Lake Superior and offshore islands. The area has developed as both a timber harvesting and processing centre and as a service center for the Canadian Pacific Railway. The Aguasabon Hydro generating complex is located in this area. The major industry is forestry which employs a large percentage of the area's population. The area forms a very small part of Kenogami Forest. The close proximity to the moderating influence of Lake Superior results in sharply contrasting climatic conditions from the more inland areas. This has a profound effect on the recreational patterns of the population.This area is located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The primary intent of this area is to provide for mixed uses such as permanent residences, industry, commercial and public services, public open spaces recreation, and resource extraction to allow for the continued growth of the community, subject to official plans and zoning by-laws.Future permanent residential, commercial, tourism and service development will be encouraged within or adjacent to existing built up areas. The Aguasabon Hydro generating complex will continue to be of major importance. Resource extraction activities and intensive recreational facilities within 300 meters of the shoreline of Lake Superior will be permitted only under controlled conditions and within specific guidelines. Any development on Crown land near the Terrace Bay Municipal Airport must comply with Ministry of Transportation and Communications airport requirements.In this particular area, activities will take into account two known historic sites, a significant waterfall and gorge, an area of kettled terrain significant as an earth science feature, and a deer yarding area. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Terrace Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).Terrace Bay/Schreiber General Use Area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Terrace Bay/Schreiber General Use Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS) at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .
