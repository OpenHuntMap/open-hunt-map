## Land area description

Rose Lake Dune Peatland Complex Conservation Reserve represents the best example, within Ecodistrict 5E-1, of weakly broken eolian sand plains (organic). An intricate complex of rolling sand dune ridges surround peat deposits that occupy depressions and interdunal swales. The shoreline of Rose Lake forms part of this site's southern boundary, which adds to its diversity. The majority of this conservation reserve consists of various types of wetland formations including fen, bog, and marsh communities. Creeks flow out of the southwest and northeast corners, and the headwaters of these creeks have become open water marshesextending beyond the site's boundary. Located at the apex of the provincially significant sand flat known as the Kirkwood Delta, the dune fields scattered throughout this site are also considered to be provincially significant features. Some of the sand ridges support unique black spruce-lichen woodland communities, of regional significance, and other ridges support blueberry heaths, low shrub thickets, and open, bracken fern meadows. White spruce and white pine forest communities are present in the easternsection of the site, with poplar mixedwoods dominating the western half. Isolated pockets of silver maple, eastern hemlock, red oak, yellow birch, and black ash are also present. Rose Lake Dune Peatland Complex Conservation Reserve was regulated on April 6, 2001. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.LOCATION:This protected area is located in Rose Township, approximately 14 kilometres (km) north of the Town of Bruce Mines, and less than 2 km west of the Kirkwood Delta Apex Enhanced Management Area (E259n). Additionally, this conservation reserve is surrounded by the Agricultural Lands (G1892), and Multiple Use Area (G1890) General Use areas.

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Rose Lake Dune Peatland Complex Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on May 30, 2003 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis and must pass a Test of Compatibility and comply with requirements under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
