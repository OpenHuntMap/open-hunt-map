## Land area description

The park boundary is discontinuous due to the presence of private townships that straddle the Batchawana River thus separating Crown areas with features and values of provincial significance. The river and shorelands that are within the park extend for some 35 kilometres (about 27 kilometres within the Batchawana-Turkey Lake natural heritage area and another eight kilometres within the upstream portion in the Batchawana East natural heritage area. The waterway provides a fragmented Crown protected area that is roughly aligned east-west through a cross section of habitats in Ecodistrict 6E-17. It includes a variety of features, such as wide meanders, numerous rapids, islands, shifting stream channels and a 13 kilometres long canyon. Along the way the waterway passes terraces from glacial lakes and rivers, shoreline wetlands, and bottom land forests. Self sustaining brook trout populations occur along the waterway. The dominant forest types are white spruce with Jack pine and white birch; deeper soil sites support yellow birch, white pine, and eastern hemlock - here at the northern edge of its range. The park boundary includes the Batchawana-Turkey Lake and Batchawana East natural heritage areas. These areas contain steep hills covered by deciduous forest and hilly tracts of ground moraine supporting stands of old white pine, white spruce and eastern hemlock. Batchawana River Provincial Park was regulated on June 25, 2004.

## Land use intent

The upper stretches of the waterway are separated from the lower portion of the park by private land in Tolmonen, Tronsen, Vibert and Running townships. The park includes Crown land only. At present the aforementioned private townships are owned by one company. MNR would cooperatively work with private landowners on protection initiatives for the private land that separates park lands along the river. The intent is to retain the original Crown reserve and the Crown bed and islands in this navigable river within private land townships that straddle the river and this will be clarified through further planning. Crown lands of this waterway class provincial park will be managed in accordance with MNR and Ontario Parks policy. Management of this park is also governed by the general policies contained in Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) including amendments and clarification of same.

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992), the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Interim Management Statement, where available. The Interim Management Statement provides direction to ensure custodial management of park resources until such time as a park management planning process is conducted, which includes public review of proposals. At that time, significant decisions regarding resource stewardship, operations, development and permitted uses are made.
