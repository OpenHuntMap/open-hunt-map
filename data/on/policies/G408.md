## Land area description

Located in southeastern Ontario, this area comprises a portion of the vast Madawaska Highlands and is characterized by a picturesque, rugged landscape with well forested hills and many interspersed waterbodies. The major watershed is defined by the Madawaska River which links several lakes such as Centennial and Black Donald Lakes and collects water from hundreds of smaller lakes and rivers. The forest is a mixture of mostly middle-aged conifer and deciduous trees, having regenerated following the cutting and fires of the late 1800's to early 1900's. The area is located fully within Hills' ecological Site District 5E-11 characterized by the presence of highly erosion-resistant bedrock resulting in irregular drainage patterns and poor soil development. Wildlife diversity reflects the mix of forest types with deer being the most abundant large vertebrates. Two significant waterfowl areas, Dwyer Marsh and Dead Beaver Creek, are located in the Madawaska Highlands Planning area. Aquatic resources are plentiful with an abundance of lakes and rivers that contain Brook trout or other trout species requiring cold, clean water for survival. The Madawaska Highlands planning area contains approximately 70% Crown land and supports a wide range of resource based recreational activities in addition to forestry, its main economic activity. It also encompasses five provincially significant life science Areas of Natural and Scientific Interest (Area of Natural and Scientific Interests) representative of the natural heritage features and values in the Site District.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Management of this area will be consistent with the overall intent and general Crown Land management strategies of the Madawaska Highlands Land Use Plan to ensure the continuing availability and sustainable development of natural resources by all users. The land use plan was prepared in response to the divergence of public opinion about how Crown lands and resources within the Highlands should be managed. The general resource areas of the Madawaska Highlands planning area generally permit all activities but additional measures have been proposed to address concerns for monitoring, level of access, public notification of management activities, and cooperative work with partners on education and research. The planning area also encompasses five provincially significant life science Areas of Natural and Scientific Interest for which additional, more specific direction is provided. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Madawaska Highlands Land Use Plan (1997), Carleton Place District Land Use Guidelines (1983), and the Tweed District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
