## Land area description

In the Northshore Area, agricultural lands are generally located west of Blind River and as far north as Poplar Dale. The stony and rocky nature of much of this area, coupled with the historically small size of pasture lands, has resulted in a complex pattern of active and abandoned farmland separated by forest, rock outcrops and other land types having low agricultural capability.Situated within this area are forest resources, aggregate deposits, significant fisheries and wildlife habitat, areas of high mineral potential and cottage developments.The following agricultural communities are represented in this area: Blind River (High Road Area), Dean Lake, Patton, Parkinson, Kynoch, Wharncliffe, Dayton, Livingstone Creek, Sowerby, Cloudslee, Ansonia, Roberts Hill, Dunn's Valley, and areas adjacent to Poplar Dale, Rock Lake, Ophir and Rydal Bank, which are situated in the Sault Ste. Marie Administrative District.Coastal portions of this area are also located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The intent is to encourage the continued use of the area for agricultural purposes, and to provide for the expansion of agricultural operations on the areas high capability lands. On Crown lands not required for agricultural purposes, other land uses are encouraged, subject to the guidelines described below. Forest management, particularly on private lands, and habitat management for deer will be encouraged. Cottaging activities will generally be restricted to private land along the North Channel of Lake Huron. Critical agricultural lands in Crown ownership will not be disposed of for other than agricultural purposes. Other Crown lands will generally not be disposed of for uses which may conflict with established agricultural operations; requests for disposition will be evaluated on a case-by-case basis, having regard to Ministry programs and the disposition policy contained in this document.Portions of the area around Iron Bridge, Patton, Parkinson, Kynoch, Wharncliffe and Sowerby contain significant winter deer habitat. In these locations, wildlife management for deer on lands not under active agricultural use will be encouraged.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Blind River District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
