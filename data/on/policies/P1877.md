## Land area description

Montreal River Provincial Nature Reserve encompasses 44 hectares and is located approximately 120 kilometres south of Wawa.Montreal River Provincial Nature Reserve contains geological and biological features of provincial significance. An extensive raised offshore cobble bar complex which formed 6,000 years ago under glacial lake levels is now 60 metres above the present day Lake Superior. The present day shoreline of Lake Superior is a high energy cobble beach. Biological features in the nature reserve includes one of the largest lichen vegetation associations in the Site District. In addition, 200 year old fire-scarred trees in the pine forest growing adjacent to the lichen communities have old growth characteristics. Two kilometres of Lake Superior shoreline within the Park provide some habitat for arctic alpine plants.This area is located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.Montreal River Provincial Nature Reserve was regulated as a provincial park in 1968.

## Land use intent

Montreal River Provincial Nature Reserve will be managed according to Provincial Park policy and Interim Management Statement (1994) where they do not conflict with Land Use Strategy. The nature reserve is managed to protect its significant landscapes and habitats, especially the lichen barrens. As the lichen barrens are easily trampled and eroded, visitors should avoid walking on them. There are no visitor facilities. The unauthorized collection of rocks, plants, or other natural objects is prohibited.

At that time, significant decisions regarding resource stewardship, operations, development and permitted uses are made.
