## Land area description

The area consists of the Upper and Lower Pic River Waterway and two kilometres of the shoreline on either side of the River. This waterway is home to a population of lake sturgeon, and includes spawning beds, suitable habitat and river characteristics necessary to sustain the population. This waterway also has cultural and heritage values of historical importance; a significant waterway for the Hudson’s Bay Company fur trading route, an important waterway for log driving in the early days of forestry in addition to the natural heritage of the various waterfalls in this area.This is an important recreational, tourism and resource sector (forestry, mining) area. It is a significant waterway that includes rugged scenic topography with good recreational capabilities. Existing activities include, but are not limited to forestry, Crown land recreation, sport fishing, hunting, trapping and resource-based tourism. This area is being managed under a sustainable forest licence (SFL). A good portion of the area is road accessible.

## Land use intent

Land use direction and resource management activities will be compatible with enhancing and maintaining the natural and recreational values of the area. The land use intent of this area is to promote and enhance opportunities for diverse recreational activities for the communities located in the Wawa District. Priority will be given to activities that promote well planned access for enhanced recreational activities, community connectivity and improved resource use in the area. Land use direction and resource management activities within this area will also be compatible with protecting the cultural and heritage values of the area, as well as to conserve and manage for the flow, spawning, nursery and habitats and sustainability of the lake sturgeon.This area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Pic River Recreation Access Area Enhanced Management Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS) at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .

## Permitted uses — preface

Management direction for these zones should be to enhance the quality of the resources that would subsequently lead to an enhancement in recreational experiences. Multi-Use Recreation Areas have been applied to areas identified as potentially containing a higher degree of recreational values. Promotion of a variety of recreational activities should be encouraged and enhanced. Recreational activities should not be limited to a particular form, but should allow for a wide array of activities including, but not limited to: · Angling· Hunting· Foraging· Wildlife viewing· Trapping· Traditional Uses· Medicinal gathering etc. Multi-Use Recreation Areas can be used for a wide range of recreation values and management intents, ranging from areas where relatively substantial recreation development is permitted, to areas providing low-density, high-quality recreation in a natural setting. Road development and industrial uses will be permitted and will be conducted in a manner that recognizes recreational development.
