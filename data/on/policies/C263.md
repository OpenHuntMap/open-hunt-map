## Land area description

This conservation reserve, in Site District 4E-3, protects yellow birch and balsam fir dominated forest stands growing on glacial deposits of low, hilly terrain. Old-growth white pine, red maple, white spruce, trembling aspen, and sugar maple constitute the remainder of this site's forest communities. A 4-kilometre stretch of the Garden River, and its associated riparian (shoreline) wetland vegetation, adds to the site's diversity. The cobble and gravel substrate of the Garden River also provides ideal spawning habitat for a number of fish species including brook trout, rainbow trout, and salmon. Garden River Forest Conservation Reserve was regulated on December 21, 2000. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.This conservation reserve is located approximately 50 kilometres (km) north of the Town of Bruce Mines, and 50 km east of the Community of Searchmont, in Curtis Township. This site is surrounded by the Ranger-Garden Lakes-Aubinadong General Use Area (G1919).

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Garden River Forest Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on April 2, 2004 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis, and must pass a Test of Compatibility, and comply with requirements under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
