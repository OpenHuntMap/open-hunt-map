## Land area description

This forest reserve combines two, formally separated, forest reserves around the Kawartha Highlands protected area. These forest reserves, formally known as F28 (Kawartha Highlands Mississauga River) and F31 (Kawartha Highlands) in the Land Use Strategy, have been combined into one forest reserve for clarity of land use direction.The first part of the reserve (formerly F28) is located within the Kawartha Highlands Signature Site Park and contains part of the Mississagua River canoe route and a provincially significant alvar. Many of the mining claims have since lapsed and will become part of the Kawartha Highlands Signature Site Park and be managed consistent with the land use intent and management direction Travelling south to the edge of the Canadian Shield this river contains a number of stretches of fast water and variety of scenic landscapes. This popular canoe route adds to the overall natural and recreational values of the Kawartha Highlands area. The alvar, in ecodistrict context, is provincially significant and is classified as Poverty Grass Alvar Grassland. It is considered to be a globally rare vegetation community type. It sustains the best known example of alvar vegetation between the Carden Plain and the Madoc area, as well as some of the most floristically rich forest stands in the Kawartha Highlands Signature Site.Many of the mining leases of the second part of the reserve (formerly F31) have since lapsed and these lands will become part of the Kawartha Highlands Signature Site Park and be managed consistent with the land use intent and management direction contained in policy report P26. The landscape is dominated by hemlock, sugar maple, red oak, white pine and red maple forests growing on thin-soiled, bedrock hills and rigged ground moraine. Portions of the area contain rock barrens with scrubby growth, shrubs and young forests. The rugged bedrock landscape contains numerous small lakes and wetlands, the shorelines of which contain rich wetlands with uncommon eastern and southern plant species. Species that dominate the understory of the alvar are Poverty Grass with Reindeer Lichens. Within the grassland woody species such as Common Juniper and Eastern White Pine can be found. The alvar also supports a number of characteristic alvar species including Kalm’s Brome Grass, Hairy Rock Cress, and Early Saxifrage The area has high recreational values and contains a number of well used canoe routes and snowmobile trails.This area is located within the Kawartha Highlands Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

Reserve areas not under mining tenure will be regulated as part of the proposed Kawartha Highlands protected area. If mining tenure is surrendered by the tenure owners through normal processes, these areas will be added to the protected area. In the interim, manage this area consistent with the protection of natural heritage values in the Kawartha Highlands.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Land Use Strategy (1999).

## Permitted uses — preface

Policies for forest reserves are similar to the policies for conservation reserves established through the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy, except that mining and related access will be allowed in a forest reserve. Most non-industrial uses (e.g., fur harvesting, commercial fishing and bait harvesting) are permitted if they are compatible with the values of individual reserves. Similarly, most recreational and non-commercial activities that have been traditionally enjoyed in an area can continue provided they pose little threat to the natural ecosystems and features protected by the forest reserve.
