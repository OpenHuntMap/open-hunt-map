## Land area description

This area encompasses roughly the lower reaches of the Goulais River watershed to the shoreline of Lake Superior. The area is sparsely populated, including a few small communities along the two main highways traversing the area (Highway 17 North and Highway 556). A number of lakes have had extensive cottage developments. Severe flooding and erosion problems occur along the shoreline of the Goulais River. The main land use activities presently occurring are recreation and resource extraction.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The general land use intent for the area will be public recreation and resource extraction. Most other land uses will be permitted with restrictions directed at avoiding conflicts between resource users on a case-by-case basis. Sensitive fisheries habitat will be protected. Twelve streams and lakes have also been identified as critical fisheries habitat, as they are used for spawning by a number of fish species. They include the Root and Goulais Rivers, Crystal, Kelly, Cranberry, Kars, Havilland, Stokely, Tier and Robertson Creeks, and Sill and Reserve Lakes. These will be protected as the need arises. All activities in the area will take into consideration the known nesting sites, spawning areas, geology, vegetation and special features sites. The specific location for each can be found in a compendium at the District office. MNR will encourage the protection of the waterfowl habitat on the Goulais River delta. MNR will retain for public use, at least the present area, on Crown islands and Crown shorelines of the Great Lakes (excluding 20 metre reserve and road allowance). Onshore mining developments may expand underground, outward under the Great Lakes, subject to existing international agreements. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
