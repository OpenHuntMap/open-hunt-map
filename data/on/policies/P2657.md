## Land area description

The park is defined as a corridor 200 metres wide on either side of the Albany River from the Highway 599 easterly to Eskakwa Falls. There are lodges and outpost camps along the waterway. A considerable amount of staking and exploration has occurred in an area of high mineral potential around Miminiska Lake. Other uses include: trapping, camping, sport fishing and hunting. There are numerous archeological and historical sites along the waterway.Part of Albany River Provincial Park is located beyond the Ontario's Living Legacy Planning Area. The total area of this park is 95,100 hectares, of which about 65 percent is water.The Albany River was regulated as a provincial park in 1989 and is classified as a waterway park. This park provides back country wilderness opportunities and some earth science representation.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

The intent is to manage this park as a waterway class provincial park, in accordance with MNR and Ontario Parks Policy.

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992), the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Interim Management Statement, where available. The Interim Management Statement provides direction to ensure custodial management of park resources until such time as a park management planning process is conducted, which includes public review of proposals. At that time, significant decisions regarding resource stewardship, operations, development and permitted uses are made. Where a use may be permitted (Maybe or Yes on the table), the long-term management direction will be determined through planning.
