## Land area description

The area lies in an east-west belt from Wasaw Lake to the mouth of the Little Grassy River. The area is moderately developed and moderately well accessed with a system of secondary provincial highways and municipal roads. A considerable portion of the area consists of patented lands. Existing uses include commercial fishing, agriculture, forestry, hunting, trapping and aggregate extraction. A large proportion of the farmlands are unimproved.The area comprises part of the Crossroute Forest. Timber harvesting is conducted on small, scattered parcels throughout the area. The interspersion of wooded and cleared parcels make it one of the most suitable areas for deer production. The area is predominantly of low potential for metallic minerals and relatively unknown potential for aggregate. Peat accumulations are shallower and less extensive than those to the south.Issues which will be taken into account in future management include the need to facilitate wildlife management on patented lands; the need to more effectively manage fur production from non-registered traplines; the need to identify and protect critical aggregate reserves; the need to reflect Ministry concerns in municipal land use planning documents; and non-resident use of Crown lands and waters.Water Management Areas have been removed from this Policy Report area as revisions, to more accurately reflect First Nation reserve lands affected by flooding.

## Land use intent

Crown lands within the area will be devoted predominantly to resource production purposes. The primary uses of Crown lands and waters will continue to be sport fishing, forestry and deer management. Owners of patented lands will be encouraged to manage their properties for wildlife production. Dispositions of Crown lands for agriculture will be discouraged. Road access will be managed primarily for resource production and resource management purposes. The Province does not have jurisdiction over the Big Grassy Indian Reserve. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Fort Frances District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
