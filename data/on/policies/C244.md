## Land area description

Rawhide Lake Conservation Reserve, found within the broad geological area known as the Abitibi Uplands, captures a spectacular, rugged landscape. Nearly one third of this site protects large, clear, cold water lakes including: Grey Trout, Rottier, Bevans, Vasseau, Tenfish, Blue Sky, Cobre, Bhynmine, No Peer, and Rawhide lakes. Most of these lakes are designated as lake trout lakes, and Rawhide Lake is additionally designated as a tourism lake. Old-growth red pine and white pine dominate the numerous ranges of forest communities in this conservation reserve, which is situated within Ecodistrict 4E-3. Eastern white cedar populates the south shores of many of the site's lakes, and jack pine stands are supported by the sandy deposits around Vasseau and Tenfish lakes. Of the site's most unique features, extensive talus slopes have formed at the bases of north-facing cliffs on Rawhide Lake and Grey Trout Lake. Rawhide Lake Conservation Reserve has a varied history of cultural, economical, and recreational importance. Thunder Mountain overlooks the south shore of Rawhide Lake, and is representative of the area which has long been recognized by Aborignal peoples for its spiritual and cultural significance. Mining and timber harvesting activities date back to the 1800s, and logging camps that were established during the early 1900s were converted to commercial tourism establishments in the 1930s. The Cobre Lake Hiking Trail was established in the early 1970s and provides access into the western portion of the conservation reserve, and lakes within the site are recognized as part of the Boland River Canoe Route. Two small parcels of land, designated as the Rawhide Lake Forest Reserve (F244) abut the conservation reserve's southwest boundary in Sagard Township. It is intended that this forest reserve will become part of the Rawhide Lake Conservation Reserve if/when the existing mining tenure is retired through normal processes, as identified in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999).Rawhide Lake Conservation Reserve was regulated on April 6, 2001. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act. LOCATION:This protected area is located approximately 28 kilometres (km) north of the City of Elliot Lake and 55 km northeast of the Town of Blind River, mainly in Sagard and Viel townships, with portions extending into Raimbault and Piche townships. Mississagi Provincial Park (P191) is immediately adjacent to the conservation reserve's southwest boundary, and the Rawhide/Boland Area (G1883), and Multiple Use Area (G1890) General Use areas surround the remainder of the site.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

To protect the natural and cultural heritage values of these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities. Thunder Mountain exemplifies the area's cultural and spirital significance for Aboriginal peoples, thereby representing a possible co-management or partnership opportunity with local First Nation communities.

## Addendum

Note: The policies outlined in this table do not supersede any Aboriginal or treaty rights that may exist, or other legal obligations.TOURISM LAKES:Rawhide Lake is designated as a tourism lake. Rawhide Lake is also designated as a quality lake trout lake (refer to Lake Trout Lakes section above). Outpost camp operations on this lake are not in conformity with existing MNR policy. Should the outpost camp site on this lake be returned to the Crown in the future, tenure will not be reissued to other operators. Otherwise, the outposts will continue to be permitted, and transfers from one operator to another will be considered acceptable.Management of this conservation reserve is carried out within the context of the Rawhide Lake Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest, and by Conservation Reserves Policy as amended by the policies for new conservation reserves outlined in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy.
