## Land area description

The Narrows Island Conservation Reserve is located on Lake Temagami southwest of the town of Temagami. The values within the reserve include a representative old growth red and white pine forest which has been aged at 175 years and the opportunities for nature appreciation and outdoor recreation activities. The Narrows Island Conservation Reserve also has high cultural heritage value for the Temagami First Nation and the Teme-Augama Anishnabai First Nation.Narrows Island was regulated as a conservation reserve on July 4, 1997.Lake Temagami is designated for lake trout management. Refer to the Inland Ontario Lakes Designated for Lake Trout Management (2006) accessible in the Source of Direction portion of this policy report. NOTE: Part or all of this Management Area contains lands set aside pending resolution of the Temagami area aboriginal land claim. These lands will not be re-opened for staking and will not be included in the forestry land base.

## Land use intent

The intent of the Narrows Island Conservation Reserve is to protect the representative old growth forest ecosystem and to provide opportunities for natural heritage appreciation and interpretive activities. The concerns of this area include the need for natural disturbances to create suitable conditions for pine regeneration. The management area objectives are to allow natural processes to occur in an area without resource extraction. The land use strategies are to develop interpretive, non-motorized trails to create awareness of natural systems in the area.

## Permitted uses — preface

Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing conservation reserve policy, the Temagami Land Use Plan (1997), and the Narrows Island Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (2002). Any new uses and commercial activities associated with conservation reserves will be considered on a case-by-case basis and will be required to pass a test of compatibility to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information.
