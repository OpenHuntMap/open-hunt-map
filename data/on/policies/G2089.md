## Land area description

The Remaining ("Straggler") Tourism Lakes from the Cochrane District Remote (Wilderness) Tourism Strategy (RWS) occur outside of the 3 identified tourism areas. These lakes will receive different degrees of protection based on their individual circumstances. . The area encompassing these lakes is a major remote tourism area for wilderness recreation (e.g. fly-in fishing, hunting, nature appreciation). It is also an important resource area for forestry and mining. Public recreational uses include fishing, hunting, ATV's, and snowmobiling. Forest management is expanding northwards for future harvesting areas. The increasing road access to this remote area has caused significant concerns from the tourist outfitters.This area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.”

## Land use intent

The management priority in this area is to protect the remote quality of the tourism lakes. Other resource uses, including harvesting, and public recreation will be permitted subject to conditions to protect tourism lakes. Identified tourism lakes will be managed in accordance with the remote tourism strategy. The maintenance of significant fish and wildlife populations and habitat will also be a priority in the area.Management of this area is governed by the general policies contained in Cochrane District Land Use Guidelines (1983) and the Approved Cochrane District Remote (Wilderness) Tourism Strategy (1997). Additional management direction for these lakes (which is more prescriptive in nature) is contained in the RWS and must also be followed. It is the intent to capture these prescriptive in appropriate resource management plans, such as forest management plans and fisheries management strategies.

## Addendum

The following lakes are designated as (viable for remote tourism) ''Category 1'' tourism lakes: Cooks Pond, Haultain, Noon, Stringer, and Thomas. In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies contained in the District's remote tourism strategy will apply to the management of these lakes.Forest Access Roads- No primary roads within 2 kilometres of lakes, no secondary roads within 1 kilometre, winter tertiary roads only from 1 kilometre to 500 metres, no road construction with 500 metres of a remote tourism lake.- Winter access to gravel within 1 kilometre of remote tourism lakes and where this is not feasible, rehabilitate gravel as soon as possible following extraction.- Non-winter gravel operations within 1 kilometre of a remote tourism lake will be determined annually in a meeting with the tourist operator. The time selected will be based on a “no booking period” that usually occurs each summer.Public Access- Some new resource access trails such as trappers’ trails may be authorized on a case by case basis.- Traditional public use (i.e. snowmachine, fly-in, canoe, foot) of these lakes will be permitted.- Culverts will be removed to restrict public access on all secondary roads within 2 kilometres of a tourism lake after all forest operations have ceased. Harvesting Patterns- 200-metre no operations around tourism lakes and outpost camps.- 200 metres to 500 metres from tourism lakes – winter harvest only using enhanced careful logging techniques, extraction corridors parallel to the shore of the lake so that all flight paths into the lake will be at right angles to the corridor.- 500-metre to 1 kilometre winter harvest only using careful logging techniques.- The above harvesting prescriptions could, in the future, be replaced with more advanced modified harvesting techniques.The following lakes are designated as (accessed but still viable for remote tourism) ''Category 2'' tourism lakes: Audrey and Snoopy. In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies contained in the District's remote tourism strategy will apply to the management of these lakes.Access Roads- No primary or secondary roads within 400 metres of tourism lakes.- Tertiary winter roads only from 400 metres to 120 metres.Public Access- Road use strategy for all primary and secondary roads.- Removal of culverts within 1 kilometre of lakes after all operations cease.Harvesting Patterns- 120-metre no-harvesting reserve around tourism lakes and outpost camps.- Timber harvesting permitted for remaining uncut areas and around the lake in keeping with current careful logging up to 120 metres.The following lakes are non-designated (viable for remote tourism) ''Category 3'' tourism lakes: 20D-34, 21H-20, Bad River, Bayley, Christie, Grace, Knight, Lauzon II, Maund, Nick’s, Rhonda, S. of Bad River (19D-10), Twin, W.
