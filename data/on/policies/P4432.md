## Land area description

The 682.23 hectare Bronte Creek Provincial Park was first regulated under theProvincial Parks Act in 1975 and subsequently in 1979 for a boundary amendment. Bronte Creek Provincial Park is located within the Regional Municipality of Halton immediately north of the Queen Elizabeth Highway on the Burlington-Oakville municipal boundary. The park is administered by the Park Superintendent, reporting to the Southwest Zone office in London. Bronte Creek is located in Ecodistrict 7E-2. Bronte Creek contains provincially significant archaeological and historical values in its heritage sites, farmstead and buildings. The Nature Reserve Zone, a provincially significant Area of Natural and Scientific Interest, protects a forested valley lands complex with a variety of vegetation communities such as rich willow and maple floodplains, forested valley slopes and mixed upland hardwoods. Carolinian and prairies species are also present in the zone. The park supports a wide variety of outdoor recreational opportunities for large numbers of peoplewhile maintaining the protection of core heritage values for future use and enjoyment. Due to its proximity to large urban centres, Bronte Creek is considered a gateway to other Ontario Parks, providing visitors with an introduction to the natural, cultural and recreational experiences found throughout the Ontario provincial park system.Located between Burlington and Oakville in Halton Region. The park offers year round activities and camping. The day use and campground areas in this park are separate; each has its own entrance.The park was regulated in 1975.

## Land use intent

This recreational class park is protected by regulation under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act. The current boundary of the park is delineated by Ontario Regulation No. 178/04.

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of the park is currently guided by the Bronte Creek Provincial Park Management Plan (1998). Additional management direction is provided in OntarioProvincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992).Uses and management activities are governed by the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserve Act and the applicable management planning documents.The permitted uses associated with provincial parks are found in approved management direction, which are available on the Ontario Parks website.
