## Land area description

The park contains representative features of ecoregion 2W, as well as recreational opportunities. The region supports a combination of sub-arctic and boreal forest. The 100-km long park is part of a larger canoe route. Significant earth science features include drumlins and granite outcrops. Surrounding First Nation communities of Bearskin Lake and Big Trout Lake actively use the park for traditional resource harvesting activities There are no visitors' facilities. The park is accessible by float plane only. Wilderness and whitewater skills are required. Backcountry camping and canoeing are among the recreational opportunities available in the park, which is accessible by float plane only. Location: 450 km north of Sioux Lookout.The park was regulated in 1989.

## Land use intent

This waterway class park is protected by regulation under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, 2006.
