## Land area description

There are two major waterbodies, Eagle Lake and Wabigoon Lake, located in this area. Existing resource activities in the area include commercial recreation, Crown land recreation, timber harvesting, sport fishing and commercial fishing. The area includes portions of the Wabigoon Forest, the Dryden Forest and the Crossroute Forest. The Wabigoon Lake Indian Reserve is located in this area.The following are designated as Tourism Lakes: Eagle, Stormy and Long.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Resource harvesting, extraction and recreational activity will be of equal importance in this area, recognizing that the area is presently used in a multiple use manner. Additional Crown land cottaging may be provided on a number of lakes in this area including Eagle Lake. Development of cottages on Eagle Lake will be preceded by a lake management plan which will recognize the prime importance of Eagle Lake in terms of the sport fisheries and the Tourism Development presently on the lake. Resource access roads will continue to be developed consistent with the concept of multiple use and recognizing the variety of activities going on in the area. Roads in the area will generally be open to all users. All activities in this area will take into consideration the known sensitive areas which include the following: three eagle nests, a soapstone quarry, a native burial ground, a pictograph, two tree plantations, two pickerel spawning areas, a hybrid poplar trial area, a heron rookery, a freshwater spring, an Indian camping ground, a black spruce production area and a climax forest.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Dryden District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
