## Land area description

The area essentially consists of the Manitou chain of lakes as well as the area centered on the Trout River from Kaopskikamak Lake to Spawn Inlet. The area is very sparsely developed and is largely inaccessible by road outside of the Highway 502 corridor. With the exception of a few scattered patented properties, the area is comprised of Crown lands. Major existing uses include tourism, sports fishing, hunting, timber extraction, trapping, and Crown land recreation. The area is almost entirely within the Crossroute Forest. Approximately thirty-six tourist enterprises are presently located within the area. Angling pressure is heaviest on the Manitou Chain and adjacent to Highway 502. Most timber harvesting to date has taken place adjacent to Highway 502 or the Cedar Narrows Road. Mineral potential varies from low to high with a substantial strip of high potential lands occupying the eastern portions of the area. Considerable prospecting activity occurs in the vicinity of the Manitou Lakes. Issues which will be taken into account in future management include the need to protect trout lakes and the potential impact on tourism of timber harvesting and its attendant road access.The following are designated as tourism lakes: Kawawia, Winkle, Esox, Dogfly, Vickers, Grant, Meggisi, Entwine, Skinny, Kinewan, Lower Manitou, Kaopskikimak, Otukamamoan, Fishtrap, Sakwhite, Whitewater, Crackshot and Highrock lakes.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The role of this area will be to provide a mix of resource extraction, tourism and recreational benefits. Future tourism will be restricted to low density activities with the emphasis on fly-in operations. Crown land recreation opportunities will centre around canoeing and angling. To protect tourism, sport fish and recreational values, road access will be strictly controlled and road access controls may be applied during periods of resource extraction and resource management activity. Furthermore, modified management areas will be managed on a priority basis to protect these values. Considerable timber harvesting will occur in the northernmost portions of the area both east and west of Highway 502. The potential for a commercial fishery on Otukamamoan Lake will be considered, and potential exists for increasing commercial fur harvests. Due consideration will be given to the protection of Indian pictographs on Vickers Lake and Manitou Stretch. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Fort Frances District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
