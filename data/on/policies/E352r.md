## Land area description

This area is located within the Obabika River watershed. The values and uses of this area include the access controls at the boundary between Wawiagama and Fry Lake Management Area, the adjacent Obabika River Waterway Park, as well as the Little Fry Lake and the adjacent wetland that have good wildlife viewing potential. Additional values include Clearwater Lake which is a high quality lake trout fishery with a remote outfitter, day use and winter angling by snowmobile, the remote tourism fly-in outpost camp on Fry Lake, and the existing and heritage portages.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Inland Ontario Lakes Designated for Lake Trout Management (2006) accessible in the Source of Direction portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The land use intent is to provide opportunities for forestry and mining related activities in a manner consistent with the adjacent park’s values. Additional intents include protecting backcountry recreation and remote tourism opportunities as well as the boundary of Obabika River Provincial Park.Concerns include the effectiveness of the Goulard Gate in preventing public motorized access and the park-related values maintained through the management of resource extraction activities.The management area objectives are to prevent new roads within 350-metres of Little Fry Lake, Clearwater Lake, and the Obabika River Waterway Park boundary, to allow resource extraction with restricted public motorized access, and to assess the potential for a wildlife viewing platform and non-motorized trails in the Little Fry Lake area. Additional objectives include maintaining the remote tourism values and the existing canoe routes, and retaining the potential for new routes while also minimizing road crossings of canoe routes.The strategies include planning access and developing road use strategies which restrict public motorized access and minimize conflicts, applying viewscape management and Area of Concern guidelines to resource extraction activities, and developing a use management strategy through the FMP process to confirm the most effective means of controlling access on the Goulard Road.
