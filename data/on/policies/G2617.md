## Land area description

This area extends southward from the CNR tracks to Highway 17, and from Lake Nipigon and the Nipigon River eastwards. Existing activities include timber harvesting, mineral exploration, cottaging, tourism and trapping. Almost the entire Lake Nipigon Forest is contained in this area. The Nipigon River is extensively developed for hydro power generation.This area is located within the Lake Nipigon Basin Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

The primary use will continue to be the resource extraction activities of timber, mining and aggregate removal. Recreation will be a secondary use. Land use conflicts will be resolved, recognizing the importance of forestry and mining in the area. Lake trout lakes, tourist lakes and rare flora, will receive special protection from access and resource extraction. Onaman Lake is managed under the Onaman Lake Fisheries Management Plan. Forestry operations and other development within 300 metres of the Nipigon River will be subject to special planning considerations to ensure fishery and scenic attributes of the area are maintained.Concerns over road access to tourist and lake trout lakes are an issue which will have to be addressed in this area.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Nipigon District Land Use Guidelines (1983).Onaman Lake/Barbara Lake General Use Area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Onaman Lake/Barbara Lake General Use Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS) at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .

## Permitted uses — preface

In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of Natural lake trout lakes:- lakes shall have a 120 metre area of concern- when feasible permanent forest roads will be located a minimum of 1.6 kilometres- temporary roads will be destroyed after intended uses are fulfilled
