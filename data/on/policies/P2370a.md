## Land area description

The Northern Peisk Lake Park Addition is immediately adjacent to the northeastern portion of Woodland Caribou Provincial Park. The most notable landscape feature of the Northern Peisk Lake Park Addition is the Peisk Lake Cliffs which include a near bald granite rock buttress which rises up roughly 70 meters at the north side of Peisk Creek. Upland areas are covered primarily by jack pine dominated forest, or have been recently burned. Woodland caribou wintering habitat occurs in the area, and a specimen of theLapland Buttercup (a regionally significant plant species) has also been found here. This area is rich in common juniper, pin cherry, drought resistant vascular plants, and Cladina spp. lichen, the latter being found on open bedrock. The Northern Peisk Lake Park Addition includes part of the headwaters of theBloodvein (Canadian Heritage) River.Currently this area supports remote outpost camps, but is otherwise undeveloped. Management boundaries that cover this area include baitfish blocks, bear management areas, traplines, and a wildlife management unit. The Pine Ridge Forest access road terminates within 3 km of Peisk Lake and could influence activities in the Northern Peisk Lake Park Addition. Recreational use of the area includes icefishing with access by snowmobile and/or aircraft, and travel by dog sled, a pursuit which is increasing in popularity in northwestern Ontario. Opportunities for new eco-tourism, recreation, and appreciation of boreal ecological and cultural landscape activities may exist in the area and may be investigated through planning.This area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.”

## Land use intent

The intent is to regulate this area as a provincial park, consistent with the decision made through the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas Land Use Strategy planning process. The proposal was presented in the Whitefeather planning process. It is also an information item in the ongoing, consultative planning process for the Woodland Caribou Signature Site. Steps to regulate the area will proceed under the Ontario Provincial Parks Class Environmental Assessment. Subsequent planning and managementdirection will be provided under the Signature Site umbrella. Until such time as the Northern Peisk Lake Park Addition is added to the Woodland Caribou Signature Site, land use and resource management direction for this area will follow the policy direction provided in this report.
