## Land area description

This large area is located north of the City of Sault Ste. Marie, generally north of the Goulais River. The westerly boundary of the area is adjacent to the Highway 17 North Corridor Area Designation. The easterly boundary was determined to be the area accessible from the Canadian National Railway (CNR), formerly the Algoma Central Railway (ACR). Approximately half of this area is Crown land whereas the other half is privately owned. The land uses presently occurring in the area include resource extraction and recreation. One hydro-electric power facility exists in this area situated at Montreal River; provisions for its continued use will be made. Various locations in Nicolet, Norberg, Wishart and Vankoughnet Townships are research sites utilized by Provincial and Federal government agencies. Their locations will be taken into consideration by all Ministry programs to minimize conflict.The following are designated as Tourism Lakes: Adelaide, Mamainse and Pancake.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The general land use intent for the Batchawana area is public recreation. All other land uses will be permitted in the area. However, site specific restrictions may be imposed on activities where Crown land recreation facilities are developed (e.g. picnic sites, camping, swimming areas, etc.). Three commercial outpost camp lakes are located in this area on Pancake, Adelaide, and Mamainse lakes. All activities in the area will take into consideration known nesting sites, and historical sites. The specific location for each can be found in a compendium at the District office. A number of lakes have been designated as critical fisheries habitat. MNR will protect the lakes from habitat and/or species degradation on a case by case basis as the need is determined. These lakes include: Gavor, Bone (Olsen), Drew, Little Drew, Kerwin, #37 (Norberg), Cedar, Middle Pancake, Upper Pancake and Annie. Development proposals and dispositions, including water lots, on the Great Lakes shoreline will be reviewed for conformity with the District’s Shoreline Management Plan.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
