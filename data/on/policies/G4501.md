## Land area description

This policy report applies to the Big Sandy Bay Management Area, comprising provincial Crown land located on the shores of Lake Ontario, in the southwest portion of Wolfe Island in the Township of Frontenac Islands, Frontenac County.Most of the area has been identified as a Provincially Significant Life Science Area of Natural and Scientific Interest (ANSI), and includes sand beach/dunes, woodlands, and wetlands that provide habitat for rare plants, birds and other wildlife including species at risk. The area also includes the Big Sandy Bay Provincially Significant Wetland.Management of the area is supported by the Township of Frontenac Islands. The area is typically used for seasonal beach use, bird watching and hunting.

## Land use intent

The Big Sandy Bay Management Area, designated a General Use Area, will be managed to provide for the long-term protection, conservation and restoration of the ecological integrity and sustainability of the Big Sandy Bay Management Area for the benefit of present and future generations.The area shall be managed in accordance with the following objectives:(a) Maintain and protect the landform features, flora and fauna, ecological functions and scenic beauty of the Management Area;(b) Maintain or restore natural self-sustaining vegetation, wildlife and its habitat;(c) Provide adequate opportunities for environmental research and outdoor education; (d) Ensure continued public access to the Management Area, subject to appropriate restrictions or specified prohibitions; (e) Ensure that any land use, recreational activity or structural development undertaken within the Management Area is compatible with the land use intent.

## Permitted uses — preface

MNRF will consider the Land Use Intent and Management Direction outlined in this policy report when reviewing applications for permitted activities that require licences, leases, permits, or other forms of approval. Commercial Activities and Land and Resource Management Activities listed as permitted may require authorization under the relevant legislation (e.g., Public Lands Act, Aggregate Resources Act, Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act, etc.). The review of individual applications involves the consideration of a variety of factors and requirements on an application and site-specific basis, in addition to land use policy.Individuals seeking to carry out any of the permitted activities listed below should contact the local MNRF district office to find out if an authorization is required.Crown lands are managed in a manner consistent with the Constitutional protection afforded to existing Aboriginal and treaty rights.
