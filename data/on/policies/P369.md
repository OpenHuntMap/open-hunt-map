## Land area description

O'Donnell Point Provincial Park is situated on the Georgian Bay coastline in Site District 5E-7, south of The Massasauga Provincial Park. The park is underlain with the Go Home Domain gneiss's of the Algonquin Terrain, situated in the Central Gneiss Belt. The landscape is dominated by acidic rock ridges and a wide variety of wetland types. These include open graminoid bogs, peatland and coniferous swamp forest.This area is located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). O'Donnell Point Provincial Park was identified as a candidate Nature Reserve class park in the Parry Sound District Land Use Guidelines (1983). The park was regulated in 1985. The regulated park boundary was amended in 2017.

## Land use intent

O'Donnell Point Provincial Nature Reserve is protected by regulation under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, 2006. The boundary of the park is delineated by Ontario Regulation No. 316/07.O'Donnell Point Provincial Nature Reserve is regulated as a nature reserve class park to represent distinctive natural communities and landforms, including species at risk. The lands are protected for educational and research purposes and for their intrinsic values. Permitted activities and management practices are described in the approved management direction and are directed solely toward the achievement of protection and heritage appreciation objectives as outlined in the legislation.An area on the south shore of Twelve Mile Bay on Georgian Bay has been approved for transfer to the federal government as part of an addition to reserve request by Moose Deer Point First Nation. The 2017 boundary amendment accommodates the approved transfer of Crown lands to Canada.

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992), the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the O’Donnell Point Park Management Plan (2007).. The permitted uses associated with provincial parks are found in approved management direction, which are available at Ontario.ca.
