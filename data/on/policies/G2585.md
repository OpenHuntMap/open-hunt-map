## Land area description

The area consists of the shore of Sabaskong Bay from the Big Grassy Reserve to Nestor Falls as well as major upland areas in Mathieu, Rowe, Croome, Menary and Claxton Townships. With the exception of the Morson and Nestor Falls locales, the area is relatively undeveloped and inaccessible. Other than Highway 71, the principal means of road access is by forest access roads. Outside of Morson Township, virtually the entire area is comprised of Crown lands. Existing uses include rural residences adjacent to Morson and Nestor Falls, as well as limited farming in Morson Township. Commercial fur is harvested from registered traplines and aggregate is extracted adjacent to road corridors. A considerable proportion of the area has recently been harvested for timber or will be harvested in the near future. Hunting occurs for moose, deer, bear and small game. The shoreline of Sabaskong Bay is used by anglers originating from Nestor Falls and Morson. A significant proportion of wild rice harvest originates from here. Virtually the entire area is of low potential for metallic minerals.Issues which will be taken into account in the future management of the area include the impact of forest harvest adjacent to Sabaskong Bay on tourism activity on the Bay, and the need to reflect MNR concerns in municipal planning documents.The following are designated as tourism lakes: Caliper and Pinus Lakes.Water Management Areas have been removed from this Policy Report area as revisions, to more accurately reflect First Nation reserve lands affected by flooding.

## Land use intent

The area will be managed primarily for resource production. The primary uses of the area will continue to be tourism, timber harvest, trapping, hunting, and wild rice harvesting.In upland areas, road access will be managed primarily for resource extraction and resource management purposes. Adjacent to Lake of the Woods, road access will be managed primarily to protect tourism interests. In Mathieu and Croome Townships, primary and secondary access roads will generally not be permitted within 800 metres of Lake of the Woods. Access roads may be located within 800 metres of the lake only where local physiography makes it otherwise impossible to economically harvest allocated timber. It was necessary to moderate the half mile setback for resource extraction roads which appears in the Lake of the Woods Plan to ensure economical skidding distances, and to provide the flexibility necessary to route roads through difficult terrain. The opportunity exists to increase production of commercial fur and wild rice within the area. Due consideration will be given to the protection of Indian pictographs on Splitrock Bay.Consistent with Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy, the sale of existing Crown land cottage lots on Lake of the Woods in the Township of Morson and on Kakabikitch Lake in the Township of Claxton will be permitted.The Province does not have jurisdiction over the Big Island Indian Reserve. An area on the south shore of Hay Bay on Lake of the Woods has been selected for transfer to the federal government as part of the Rainy River First Nation land claim settlement. The future intent of this area is Indian Reserve. Management of this area will be consistent with this future land use intent.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Fort Frances District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
