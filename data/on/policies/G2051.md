## Land area description

There are three major and two minor soil areas in the District having a high capability to support agricultural production. The major areas are: the Blezard Valley area in the Sudbury Basin, where agricultural production is moderately high, the Hagar - St. Charles area which has smaller areas of moderately high capability for agricultural production and, the Alban - Noelville area, which has a slightly lower capability for agricultural production in scattered areas: The minor areas are: the Burwash area and the Wanup area.These areas primarily support the production of beef and dairy cattle and forage crops to supply local markets. The Blezard Valley area also produces some cash crops, such as potatoes and other vegetables. Private land holdings are extensive within these areas and several municipalities can control land use under the Planning Act. Numerous small areas of land in each of these areas have been issued under a sustainable forest licence (SFL) to a number of small timber operators, primarily to supply local wood-using mills. Mineral aggregate extraction has usually occurred along the major highway corridors and close to concentrations of urban, rural and seasonal residential development.Several moose wintering areas have been identified throughout these areas. Wildlife management efforts will continue to monitor the deer, moose, and elk populations in the interests of increasing wildlife management populations and harvest opportunities. The Alban / Noelville area contains one lake trout lake; Upper Sturgeon Lake.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Agriculture and rural residential development will continue to be the primary land uses. Wood production for local use, fish and wildlife management, general recreation, tourism and cottaging are all important secondary uses. Also, special guidelines for any development and resource management activities in proximity to the osprey nesting site in Fairbank Township will be prepared to ensure its protection. Any development proposals in the waterfowl nesting areas in the Nickel Valley and on the West Arm of Lake Nipissing will be subject to a thorough review to ensure the protection of these important wetland areas.
