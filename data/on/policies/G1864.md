## Land area description

This area is located within the Montreal River/Sturgeon River watershed. The values and uses of this area include the high potential for hiking opportunities linked to the Okiniada fire tower and the Ishpatina Ridge in Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater Provincial Park.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Inland Ontario Lakes Designated for Lake Trout Management (2006) accessible in the Source of Direction portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The land use intent is to provide opportunities for forestry and mining related activities while mitigating impacts on recreational opportunities, Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater Provincial Park, and the Montreal River headwaters.The concerns include the sustainability of a natural brook trout fishery in Okiniada Lake as well as the impacts of road development, forestry and mining related activities on hiking trail potential and the Okiniada Lake fishery. The management area objectives are to provide water access to LESWPP (Smoothwater Lake) via the Montreal River and to maintain no direct road access to LESWPP. Objectives also include retaining hiking opportunities to Okiniada Fire Tower/lookout as well as the potential for backcountry trail opportunities linking access at Montreal River to Ishpatina Ridge. Another objective includes addressing the snowmobile trail through this area and managing Okiniada Lake as a remote brook trout lake with low angling pressure and limited access (e.g., trail).The strategies include identifying appropriate location for snowmobile trail which will not impact fisheries and park-related values as well as identifying appropriate hiking trail corridor linking Okiniada Ridge and Fire Tower and Ishpatina Ridge with a linking trail corridor to access at Beauty Lake Road (e.g., at Montreal River). Strategies also include applying appropriate viewscape and Areas of Concern guidelines for resource extractive activities and developing fisheries strategy to manage Okiniada Lake as a natural brook trout lake with limited access.
