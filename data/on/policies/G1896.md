## Land area description

This area includes that portion of the North Channel of Lake Huron west of the Town of Blind River. Existing uses include commercial fishing, angling, boating and cottaging. The rocky nature of much of the shoreline and the lack of offshore islands restricts the type and amount of recreation which can occur in this area.The northern boundary of this area follows the normal highwater mark of Lake Huron.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The intent is to protect the existing commercial fishery and fish spawning areas from adverse uses. Recreational uses will be permitted in most areas.Disposition of Crown lands in offshore areas will not be permitted, except for water lots for private or public use, subject to official plans, zoning by-laws and zoning orders, where no adverse impacts on Ministry programs are anticipated and subject to the protocol for disposition as per the Wikwemikong Land Claim.Protection of nesting sites for migratory species will be emphasized.Land under the waters of Lake Huron is withdrawn from exploration and staking out under the Mining Act. Onshore mining developments may expand underground, outward under the North Channel, provided this is in accord with existing international agreements.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Blind River District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
