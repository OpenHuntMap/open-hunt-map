## Land area description

These recommended additions (Peisk Lake and Anchor Lake areas) to the existing Woodland Caribou Provincial Park will increase representation and add to park diversity. The area includes the headwaters of the Bloodvein River (Canadian Heritage River) and a number of representative landscape features. This area is located within the Woodland Caribou Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report. The recommended park additions are located between Woodland Caribou Provincial Park and Red Lake. Life science values consist of a provincially significant prairie plant and sensitive wildlife, such as forest dwelling woodland caribou. The majority of plant species found in Woodland Caribou Provincial Park additions are characteristic of a boreal forest ecosystem. The additions are comprised mainly of large tracts of bare bedrock, with a small pocket of till that is part of the extensive Eagle-Finlayson Moraine.

## Land use intent

Peisk Lake and Anchor Lake areas will be protected on an interim basis, and will be added to the park once replacement wood supplies are secured from other locations. The intent is to regulate this area as a provincial park (wilderness class).

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of the recommended provincial park additions is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992), the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the provincial park management plan. The park management plan provides direction regarding resource stewardship, operations, development and permitted uses. With the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements in the Sources of Direction listed below may be out of date.The permitted uses associated with provincial parks are found in approved management direction, which are available on the Ontario Parks website.
