## Land area description

This area lies to the east of Rainy Lake and is bisected by both Highway 11 and the Seine River. Only portions of the area, particularly those close to Highway 11, are road accessible. The area consists predominantly of Crown lands, although clusters of private property do exist. Major existing uses within this predominantly upland area include forest management, trapping and hunting. The area lies within the Crossroute Forest. Portions of the area between the Seine and Little Turtle Rivers are of high or moderate aggregate potential. This belt also exhibits moderate, and in some cases high, mineral potential.Grassy Lake is designated as a tourism lake.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.Water Management Areas have been removed from this Policy Report area as revisions, to more accurately reflect First Nation reserve lands affected by flooding.

## Land use intent

The overall role will be resource production. Major uses will continue to be forestry, trapping and hunting. Road access will be managed for resource extraction, and to provide recreational access to warmwater lakes and upland areas.Consistent with Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy, the sale of existing Crown land cottage lots on Calm Lake in the Township of Bennett will be permitted.The Province does not have jurisdiction over the Sturgeon Falls and Seine River Indian Reserves. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Fort Frances District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
