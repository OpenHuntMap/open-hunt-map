## Land area description

This management area covers the majority of the North Channel of Lake Huron - running from Aird and Darch Islands on the west to Heywood Island on the east. This area does not include Bay of Islands, McGregor Bay or Great LaCloche Island. The most common existing use in this area is recreation - cottaging in the near mainland areas and boating in the more open water areas. Commercial fishing is also ongoing. The North Channel has a recreational potential of international significance.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The intent is to identify and maintain non-structured recreational use as the primary and most appropriate use of this area. In particular, MNR will assist in protecting the physical resources of the North Channel that combine to make this area one of world significance for small boat sailing. The majority of the islands in this area are within the proposed North Channel Islands - La Cloche Addition Provincial Park. As in area 7, Ministry management activities will assist in the protection of nesting areas for colonial birds and predators such as eagles and ospreys. The waters around Heywood Island will continue to be managed for experimental sport fish reproduction purposes.On-shore mining developments may expand underground, outward under Lake Huron, subject to existing international agreements.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Espanola District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Addendum

The Heywood Island Area provides a sport fishery for lake trout backcross as the result of an experimental stocking program. This program should be assessed to determine the success of the stocking and if natural reproduction is occurring.
