## Land area description

This area comprises several small watershed units draining northerly and easterly from the Algonquin Highlands into the Mattawa and Ottawa Rivers. Soil depths are relatively deep in the western portion of the unit, becoming shallower to the east. Terrain is moderately broken in the south, but slopes are less steep in the Ottawa River Valley and in sections of Lauder Township. There are very few private land holdings. Most of the area is unorganized.The Algonquin Park Area of Concern applies to this area. Timber production from Crown land is a major industrial use. The area contains the headwaters of several brook trout streams. The area is generally moderate to high carrying capacity for moose.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

This is a sparsely developed area and the primary uses will be resource production and wildlife management. Recreation and tourism are acceptable uses. The Algonquin Park Area of Concern is an area of no finite distance surrounding the park boundary where development and management activities are identified and reviewed to ensure park values are not impacted. Resource and recreational activities outside the park will be reviewed in conjunction with Algonquin Park District and using the Area of Concern Guidelines contained within the Algonquin Park Management Plan. Significant aggregate resources occur in Boulter, Papineau and Cameron Townships and these should remain accessible for future use. Several significant sites such as waterfowl concentration areas, moose aquatic feeding and winter concentrations areas, critical fish habitat, etc., are found in this area. These and other such sites, which may be found in the future, will be protected during resource management operations and will be identified as values requiring protection in municipal planning. Intent is to manage Papineau Lake for lake trout.MNR is responsible for the management of Crown land and natural resources in organized and unorganized townships. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in North Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Addendum

Papineau Lake is designated as a Type B2 lake trout lake. Type B2 lakes are managed for lake trout but other uses can be considered on a lake-by-lake basis. In addition to provincial and regional polices, the following local policies will apply to the management of this lake:- Protecting critical fish habitat consistent with the Forest Management Guidelines for the Protection of Fish Habitat, and carefully controlling other activities within 120 metres of the shoreline to prevent erosion and the deposition of sediment or other debris in the water are important objectives within this area.
