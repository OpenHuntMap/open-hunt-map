## Land area description

Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother Mnidoo Mnising Provincial Park (Natural Environment Class) is located on the west end on the south shore of Manitoulin Island, approximately 52 kilometres (by road) west of Gore Bay and 230 kilometres west of Sudbury. The park is accessed from Highway 540, which is accessible from Highway 6, south of Espanola and Highway 17.Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother Mnidoo Mnising is undeveloped. Old roads and trails in the area were previously developed for logging, and to access hunt camps. Roads and trails are unmaintained. The past landowner removed a number of the hunt camps when the area was private land. The past landowner sold these lands to the Nature Conservancy of Canada (NCC) who was working in partnership with the Ontario government. Unopened Crown road and shoreline allowances (in Deer Yards G2026) have been regulated as part of the park. This unusual landscape is dominated by alvar rockland on south-dipping Palaeozoic rocks of the Amabel formation in the Michigan Basin. These slightly sloping fossiliferous rocks are sparsely vegetated with plants more typical of dry prairie habitats. Some grasses and herbaceous plants that occur here are species at risk in Ontario; some are the largest populations of these rare plants in Ontario. White cedar and jack pine are scattered in the otherwise-open alvar rockland, while poplar, white birch, white and red pine and sugar maple are found further inland where soils are deeper.Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother Mnidoo Mnising Provincial Park was regulated on March 20, 2014 under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, 2006.

## Land use intent

Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother M’Nidoo M’Nissing will be managed to protect provincially significant, special and representative natural heritage values of the Manitoulin Island Eco-district. Scientific research is encouraged. Management of the park will protect its natural and cultural values while offering low-intensity recreational activities, such as hiking and ecotourism-based opportunities to complement existing local and regional tourism.

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park will be carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies, 1992, A Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves, 2005, and the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, 2006. This direction will ensure custodial management of park resources until such time as a park management planning process is conducted with public and Aboriginal review. At that time, significant decisions regarding resource stewardship, operations, development and permitted uses will be made.The permitted uses associated with provincial parks are found in approved management direction, which are available on the Ontario Parks website.
