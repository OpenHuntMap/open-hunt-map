## Land area description

This policy report applies to the Pine River Fishing Area Crown lands that are part of the Niagara Escarpment Parks and Open Space System (NEPOSS) and subject to the requirements of the Niagara Escarpment Plan. The Niagara Escarpment Plan’s NEPOSS is a system of more than 160 publicly owned parks and open spaces which are connected by the Bruce Trail. The Pine River Valley is used as a public fishing area and for hunting, picnicking and Bruce Trail hiking. The property also includes a portion of the Terra Nova Provincially Significant Wetland Complex. The Niagara Escarpment Planning and Development Act, 1990 established a planning process to ensure that the Niagara Escarpment would be protected through the implementation of the Niagara Escarpment Plan. The Niagara Escarpment Plan protects the Escarpment’s unique ecological, historic and scenic qualities. In addition to the Niagara Escarpment Plan, these Crown lands are located within the boundaries of the Greenbelt Plan and the A Place to Grow: Growth Plan for the Greater Golden Horseshoe. These lands contribute to the Natural Heritage System identified under these plans.

## Land use intent

The Pine River Fishing Area will be managed to achieve the purpose of the Niagara Escarpment Plan. The purpose of the Plan is to provide for the maintenance of the Niagara Escarpment and land in its vicinity substantially as a continuous natural environment, and to ensure only such development occurs as is compatible with that natural environment. The NEPOSS is a provincially coordinated system that secures and protects significant Escarpment features and scenic landscapes. The system also provides the public with opportunities for compatible recreation that satisfies the park and open space objectives set out in the Plan. In this context, the objectives of NEPOSS are: 1.To protect the Niagara Escarpment’s natural heritage resources and conserve its cultural heritage resources; 2.To provide opportunities for outdoor education and recreation; 3.To provide for public access to the Niagara Escarpment; 4.To complete a public system of major parks and open spaces through land acquisition and Master/Management planning; 5.To secure a permanent route for the Bruce Trail; 6.To protect and enhance the natural environment of the Niagara Escarpment, including the protection of natural heritage and hydrologic features and functions; 7.To support tourism by providing opportunities on public land for discovery and enjoyment by Ontario’s residents and visitors; 8. To provide a common understanding and appreciation of the Niagara Escarpment; and 9. To show leadership in supporting and promoting the principles of the Niagara Escarpment’s UNESCO World Biosphere Reserve designation through sustainable park planning, ecological management, community involvement, environmental monitoring, research and education. The Pine River Fishing Area is classified under the Niagara Escarpment Plan NEPOSS classifications as a Resource Management Area, meaning that it is intended to be managed primarily for resource related benefits, such as forestry, fish and wildlife, or flood control. Resource Management Areas also provide recreation opportunities, and protection and conservation of resources. The Land Use Designations cited in Part 1 of the Niagara Escarpment Plan and the Development Criteria in Part 2 also apply. The Pine River Fishing Area falls within the Escarpment Natural Area designation. Reference to the Niagara Escarpment Plan must be made to determine the Development Criteria applicable to any site-specific proposal.

## Permitted uses — preface

MNRF will consider the Land Use Intent and Management Direction outlined in this policy report when reviewing applications for permitted activities that require licences, leases, permits, or other forms of approval. Commercial Activities and Land and Resource Management Activities listed as permitted may require authorization under the relevant legislation (e.g., Public Lands Act, Aggregate Resources Act, Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act, etc.). The review of individual applications involves the consideration of a variety of factors and requirements on an application and site-specific basis, in addition to land use policy. In addition, the Niagara Escarpment Commission is an independent regulatory agency of the province and has been delegated the legal authority to make development control decisions in accordance with the Niagara Escarpment Planning and Development Act and the Niagara Escarpment Plan. To ensure that the Escarpment’s natural resources, ecosystem health and scenic landscape are protected, certain activities and development (e.g., quarries, sand & gravel pits, road construction, buildings, etc.) require permitting through the Niagara Escarpment Commission. Individuals seeking to carry out any of the permitted activities listed below should contact the local MNRF district office to find out if an authorization is required. Crown lands are managed in a manner consistent with the Constitutional protection afforded to existing Aboriginal and treaty rights. In accordance with the provisions of the Niagara Escarpment Plan, existing uses (as defined under the Niagara Escarpment Plan) that are not otherwise listed as permitted uses are permitted to continue where the use was legally existing prior to the approval of the Niagara Escarpment Plan, an amendment to the plan, or an amendment to the plan area.
