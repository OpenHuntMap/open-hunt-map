## Land area description

This area is located generally west of the Town of Fort Frances and within 25 kilometres of the Rainy River. It includes the Towns of Emo and Rainy River as well as the Rainy River First Nation. The majority of the area is organized within a municipality. The primary land use at present is agriculture. Virtually all of the high capability agricultural lands within this well accessed area are patented. Other existing uses outside urban areas include forestry, trapping and hunting. This area presently produces a significantly high proportion of the local annual deer harvest. The area also comprises part of the Crossroute Forest. Timber harvesting is conducted on small, scattered parcels throughout the area and mainly on private land. Crown lands have a largely undetermined peat potential, as well as wildlife and timber values. The area is predominantly of moderate potential for metallic minerals and significant aggregate deposits may exist. Issues which will be taken into account in the future management of the area include the impacts of wetland drainage for agriculture, the need to facilitate wildlife management , the potential of peat resources, and the need to reflect Ministry concerns in municipal land use planning documents.

## Land use intent

This area will be devoted primarily to resource production. Agriculture will continue to be the primary use. Expansion of agriculture on Crown land will be considered in areas of high priority for agricultural use and in conjunction with recommendations from Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs. High priority lands are Crown lands with a high capability for agriculture located within an area of predominantly high agricultural capability, including capability for speciality crops. Owners of patented lands will be encouraged to manage their properties for wildlife production. Crown lands will be primarily managed for deer and timber. Regeneration efforts on Crown lands will be focused on production forest lands which do not have high capability for agriculture (defined as Classes 1, 2, 3 or 4 of the Canada Land Inventory.) Crown lands with peat potential will be managed to safeguard future development options. The Province does not have jurisdiction over the Rainy River First Nation lands. Due consideration will be given to the protection of the Hungry Hall Indian burial grounds. Four Islands in Rainy River and three Crown land parcels north of Manitou Rapids Indian Reserve #11 have been selected for transfer to the federal government as part of the Rainy River First Nation land claim settlement. The future intent of these areas is Indian Reserve. Management of these areas will be consistent with this future land use intent.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Fort Frances District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
