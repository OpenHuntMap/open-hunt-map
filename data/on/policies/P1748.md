## Land area description

Little Abitibi Provincial Park is located approximately 88 kilometres northeast of Cochrane. This park is anchored at its southern extremity by a number of large lakes on the Little Abitibi River system, notably Pierre, Montreuil and Harris Lakes. The park follows the Little Abitibi River from the outlet at Harris Lake for about 70 kilometres to a diversion dam, constructed in 1963 to divert water into Newpost Creek, to the confluence at the Abitibi River. At the north end, the park boundary expands to include a portion of the Abitibi River and adjacent land base for the protection of a locally significant historical site. The park encompasses a total area of 20,000 hectares.Little Abitibi Provincial Park was not classified at the time of its regulation in 1985. Until such time as a park management plan is prepared for the park and a classification determined, the Little Abitibi River - Newpost Creek section of the park will be considered waterway class and the Pierre - Montreuil section, natural environment class. This split classification is consistent with the last public consultation and approved land use direction (Cochrane District Land Use Guidelines) associated with classification of the park.The abundance of eskers, kames, kettles and moraines are found in the park. All of these landforms are covered by a layer of clay, deposited by a vanished lake. Other outstanding natural features in the park include a 26-metre waterfall and a stand of 300 year old red pine.Little Abitibi Provincial Park was regulated as a provincial park in 1985. Amendments to the regulated boundary were made in 2014.This area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.”

## Land use intent

Little Abitibi Provincial Park is protected by regulation under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, 2006.

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992), the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Interim Management Statement, where available. The Interim Management Statement provides direction to ensure custodial management of park resources until such time as a park management planning process is conducted, which includes public review of proposals. At that time, significant decisions regarding resource stewardship, operations, development and permitted uses are made.The permitted uses associated with provincial parks are found in approved management direction, which are available on the Ontario Parks website.
