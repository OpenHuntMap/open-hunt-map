## Land area description

The area consists of the Despair/Pipestone/Loonhaunt chain of lakes in addition to other waterbodies which are within a short portage, excluding the area of the Pipestone Conservation Reserve. With the exception of secondary provincial highways which lead to Burditt and Despair Lakes, and forest access roads which approach Panorama and Kishkutena Lakes, this area is inaccessible by road. The waterbodies are used heavily by tourist operators, cottagers and recreationists. Tourism enterprises are currently located within the area. A commercial fishing operation exists on Burditt Lake. Three quarters of the fisheries capability of the area is found in cold-water lakes. Major uses include timber harvesting, hunting for moose, deer, bear and small game, as well as trapping. Mineral potential is generally low although high and moderate potential does exist along the western and northern edges. The area is situated within the Crossroute Forest. Issues which will be taken into account in the future management of the area include the high harvest pressure on sport fish resources, the protection of trout lakes, management of tourism, recreational and cottaging interests, road access to inaccessible portions of the lake chain, and the potential conflict between commercial fishing and angling activities.The following are designated as tourism lakes: Burditt, Despair, Jackfish, Off, Loonhaunt, Dash, Kaiarskons and Kishkutena lakes.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Management will be directed towards the protection of existing tourist, recreational and cottaging interests. Timber harvesting operations will consider tourism and recreational values. Potential exists for some increased tourism activity from existing facilities based on deer hunting opportunities here and in the ''Penassi Road'' area. Cottage lots may be developed on the Despair/Footprint/Jackfish chain of lakes. The possibility exists that commercial fishing could be introduced on up to seven lakes in the area. Also the opportunity exists to increase commercial fur production. Road access, while required primarily for resource extraction, will consider tourism and aesthetic values. Due consideration will be given to the protection of the Indian pictographs at Burditt Lake. The Province does not have jurisdiction over Northwest Bay Indian Reserve. The Gates Ajar Narrows area on Pipestone Lake has been selected for transfer to the federal government as part of the Rainy River First Nation land claim settlement. The future intent of this area is Indian Reserve. Management of this area will be consistent with this future land use intent.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Fort Frances District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
