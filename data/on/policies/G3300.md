## Land area description

The Central Whitefeather General Use Area extends centrally, from the southern limit to the northern limitof the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas. The area is characterized by a gently rolling landscapeand covered by coniferous boreal forest with excellent forestry potential. Jack pine forests thrive on thesandy soils of this area and represent the dominate upland forest type. At its center is the northern extentof the Lac Seul Moraine, also know as Ayohtahtahgwahgahneewahng or the “backbone of the land” bythe Pikangikum people. Customary winter and summer travel routes, and important waterways traversethe Central Whitefeather. Fish and wildlife species typical of the boreal forest are found here, includingspecies at risk woodland caribou and wolverine. First Nation people conduct various traditional activitieson this land to support their livelihood, and to maintain their cultural heritage.Development in the Central Whitefeather consists of the Nungesser Road (all-weather), two winter roads,one main base tourist lodge (not remote), several outpost camps, private recreation camps, boat caches,an access point, one campground, temporary commercial moose hunt camps, several aggregate pits, awaste disposal site, one hydro corridor, one communications tower, some fuel caches, and several trapcabins. In addition, the Bak Lake forward forest fire attack base is located in the southern part of thisarea. Management boundaries that cover the area include bear management areas, baitfish blocks,traplines, and wildlife management units. There are identified areas of Provincially Significant MineralPotential and all or part of several mining claims.

## Land use intent

The land use intent is to establish a community forestry opportunity for Pikangikum First Nation, alongwith other opportunities that can support community economic renewal. Access will be managed toconsider the WFAA Strategy stated objective of remoteness. Water quality and the ecological integrity ofwaterways are to be preserved and considered in all management decisions. Activities associated withthe mineral sector, eco-cultural tourism, recreation, and non-timber forest products will be encouraged.New economic opportunities will be encouraged in ways that sustain the remote character of the area.Implementation of an adaptive management approach, rooted in Pikangikum Indigenous Knowledge andthe best of western science, is a critical element of forest management in the area. Maintenance ofnatural disturbance regimes will be given high importance.
