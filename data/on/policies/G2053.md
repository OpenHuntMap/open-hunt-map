## Land area description

This area is dominated by mineral exploration and development. Timber harvesting and aggregate extraction are secondary uses. Bait fishing, trapping, sport fishing and hunting are also prevalent. Virtually all of the area is held under private ownership. Forest management of the area is carried out under two sustainable forest licences (SFLs). The major road, rail and air transportation networks and facilities which radiate out of the City of Greater Sudbury pass through this area. Urban and mining-related land uses dominate human settlement and industrial patterns, while commercial recreation activities have been characterized by accommodation and supply services since the 1960's.Problems associated with resource development in the area have stemmed from undesirable resource management practices, as well as competition between recreationists and residential uses for the same land base required by mining, forestry and agricultural uses.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Mineral and aggregate extraction will be the primary uses. Timber harvesting and rural residential development are of secondary importance. Lake trout lakes will be managed to ensure rehabilitation and protection of lake trout population.The Turbine deer wintering yard in Drury Township supports a population of approximately 100 deer. Any development which would reduce cover and browse will be prohibited on Crown land and not supported on private land. No further Crown land disposition will be permitted within the deer yard. Also, special guidelines for any development and resource management activities in proximity to the heronry in Norman Township will be prepared to ensure its protection. Throughout the remainder of the area, Crown land disposition will be considered, on a case-by-case basis, having regard for impact on Ministry programs and the general Crown Land Disposition policy.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sudbury District Land Use Guidelines (1983)

## Permitted uses — preface

In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of Natural lake trout lakes:­ no additional access will be permitted­ 120 metre Area of Concern will be applied­ forest management activities may be permitted within the Area of Concern subject to concerns for erosion/sedimentation and the protection of lake trout habitat.
