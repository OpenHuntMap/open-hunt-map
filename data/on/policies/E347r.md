## Land area description

This area is located within the Temagami River watershed. The values and uses of the area include the high mineral potential, past activity, and numerous mining properties, the significant large old white and red pine stands in the Skyline Reserve, the Bear Island Indian Reserve, and the extensive island cottaging with limited mainland development. Other values include high quality lake trout fishery, the concentration of commercial tourism and youth canoe camps, and the existing and heritage portages. All the islands in Lake Temagami (except part of Temagami Island) are withdrawn from staking as are the Aboriginal maple syrup area.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Inland Ontario Lakes Designated for Lake Trout Management (2006) accessible in the Source of Direction portion of this policy report.NOTE: Part or all of this Management Area contains lands set aside pending resolution of the Temagami area aboriginal land claim. These lands will not be re-opened for staking and will not be included in the forestry land base.

## Land use intent

The land use intent is to maintain the Lake Temagami as a significant recreation and tourism lake, the unique cultural heritage landscape, and the high value natural lake trout fishery. The intent is also to maintain the old pine component of the Skyline Reserve for its aesthetic beauty.Concerns include the mining related impacts on aesthetics and water quality, the pine regeneration in the Skyline Reserve, the competition for campsites (e.g., houseboats, boats, canoes), the use of portages as ATV trails, and the unplanned access and roadside camping.The management area objectives are to prevent new road access to Lake Temagami and to retain the authorized road access points including Strathcona Road, Mine Landing, Baie Jeanne, the Town of Temagami, and Finlayson Point Provincial Park. It is important to note that mainland development will not be permitted within the management area, but carefully planned island development will be permitted where appropriate. Additional objectives are to maintain existing canoe routes and retain the potential for new routes, campsites, and trails. Objectives also include retaining viewscape and maintaining high quality fishery while restricting access from Lake Temagami by ATV (restrict to fuelwood permit-holders only). Potential access at Ferguson Bay and Shiningwood Bay will be determined by a Class Environmental Assessment (EA) process.The strategies include applying management prescriptions for mining-related activities in the Skyline Reserve developed by MNDM and MNR, fostering partnerships to develop and implement strategies and resolve issues (i.e., vegetation management strategy, access management, etc.), and addressing access issues with appropriate processes (e.g., Class EA). Strategies also include integrating Lake Temagami access points into a future access management and visitor distribution system for the Temagami Recreational Area and area parks and developing a strategy to address ATV use (fuelwood strategy for Lake Temagami).
