## Land area description

French River Provincial Park is located 60 kilometres south of Sudbury in Ecodistricts 5E-5 and 5E-7. The French River is a broad, tree-lined, east to west drainage from Lake Nipissing to Georgian Bay. The River cuts through the Canadian Shield rocks with open water lake widenings separated by falls, rapids and steep narrow gorges. The rugged topography supports more than 450 plant species, some rare. The river's mouth is a rock delta on Georgian Bay with wetland habitat for perhaps the largest community of Virginia chain fern in the province. Eastern Massassauga rattlesnake, the only poisonous snake in Ontario, is also found in this area. One of the largest populations of white-tailed deer in the province inhabits the rocky forests on the southern shores of the upper river.There are numerous landform vegetation types, including forests dominated by large wind-swept white pine (some as old as 180-210 years), white cedar, jack pine, white birch, poplar, spruce and maple growing on bare bedrock and on low hills with sandy soil.This area is used for a variety of recreational activities including backcountry camping, boating, canoeing and kayaking, fishing, trapping, hunting and commercial bear hunting; there are a number of recreation camps and lodges in the area.French River is an operating park, and although there are no campground facilities, permits are available for backcountry campsites in the park. There are many roads with access to the river. is canoe, kayak and motor boats with a variety of tourist cottages, lodges and fishing camps as inholdings within the park.French River Provincial Park is a waterway class park that was originally regulated in 1989. Additions were made in 1997 as a result of the Keep it Wild initiative and again in 2006 as a result of Ontario’s Living Legacy.

## Land use intent

French River Provincial Park will be managed according to Provincial Park policy and Park Management Plan (1993). Management of the park will provide recreation and camping opportunities while protecting the natural and cultural environment of the French River.
