## Land area description

This area is located within the Temagami River watershed. The values and uses of the area include the significant recreation and remote angling lakes adjacent to Lake Temagami (Kokoko, Spawning, and Chambers), a number of small stocked lakes accessible from Lake Temagami by portage, the hiking trail opportunities associated with Lake Temagami, and the existing and heritage portages.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Inland Ontario Lakes Designated for Lake Trout Management (2006) accessible in the Source of Direction portion of this policy report.NOTE: Part or all of this Management Area contains lands set aside pending resolution of the Temagami area aboriginal land claim. These lands will not be re-opened for staking and will not be included in the forestry land base.

## Land use intent

The land use intent is to maintain the management area as a significant recreation area for remote tourism and canoeing accessible from Lake Temagami and to provide opportunities for forestry and mining related activities.Concerns include the motorized access to Kokoko, Spawning, Lake Temagami and the numerous stocked speckled trout lakes increasing angling pressure and reduced semi-remote opportunities. Additional concerns include access through parts of the skyline reserve which may be required for resource extraction activities.The management area objectives are to mitigate the impacts of resource extraction activities, to maintain the existing canoe routes and retain potential for new routes, and to retain the potential for the completion of the Lake Temagami Skyline Trail. Additional objectives are to restrict public motorized access and to maintain the potential for remote commercial tourism infrastructure (huts) related to non-consumptive uses on Kokoko Lake, Spawning Lake, etc. Further objectives are to maintain the speckled trout angling opportunities in semi-remote areas adjacent to Lake Temagami and to maintain Kokoko Lake as a semi-remote angling destination.The strategies include planning access and developing road use strategies to restrict public motorized access and minimize conflicts while also applying viewscape and Area of Concern planning to extractive activities.
