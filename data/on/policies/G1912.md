## Land area description

This area is composed of the waters of Batchawana and Sand Bays. Batchawana Bay includes the waters inside a line drawn from 1 kilometre west of the mouth of the Batchawana River due south to Batchawana Island, along the shore of the island to its most southern extremity and southeasterly to the northern extremity of the point which forms the western boundary of Horseshoe Bay. Sand Bay includes the waters inside a line drawn across the mouth of the bay. The land uses presently occurring in this area are public recreation (mostly angling, motor boating, and sailing) and some commercial fishing. Commercial fishing in the area is presently restricted from Inner Batchawana Bay (gill netting) and within 1,000 metres of the mouth of rivers or streams through legislation and conditions of licences. Commercial fishing authorized by Ontario Commercial Fishing licences is presently restricted from Inner Batchawana Bay.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The general land use intent for this area is public recreation. MNR will protect and encourage the protection of the waterfowl habitat around the east end of Batchawana Island. All activities in the area will take into consideration known spawning areas. The specific locations can be found in a compendium at the District office. The District will dispose of Crown water lots for public or private use subject to official plans and associated zoning by-laws. Major disposition is also subject to the Ministry of the Environment's review which may result in the requirement of an environmental assessment. Disposition which would result in direct or indirect negative impacts on any Ministry programs will be discouraged. The shoreline areas, the small protected bays and natural harbours offer attractive recreational boating opportunities (motor boats, yachts, sailboats, canoes). Some of these areas are well known and have been publicized in various publications and consequently are relatively extensively used by cottage owners and tourists. Therefore, in order to maintain the availability of such opportunities, MNR will retain for public use, at least the present area, on Crown islands and Crown shorelines of the Great Lakes. However, no future development for recreation is planned at the present. Islands and lands under the water of this area have been withdrawn from staking. Development proposals and dispositions, including water lots, on the Great Lakes shoreline will be reviewed for conformity with the District’s Shoreline Management Plan.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
