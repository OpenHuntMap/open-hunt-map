## Land area description

Our Colleagues Conservation Reserve, in Site District 4E-3, protects provincially significant outcrops of a rock formation described as jasper conglomerates of the Lorrain Formation. This rock is commonly known as puddingstone. Located in the Boland River Valley, this site also protects a 2-kilometre (km) section of the Boland River, which provides important habitat for brook trout. Eastern white cedar populates shore fen wetland communities lining the river, along with boulder lags that were deposited during glacial times. Relatively shallow glacial soils support forest communities dominated by white pine, balsam fir and white birch. The shoreline of a small unnamed lake, a rich conifer swamp, and a super-canopy stand of white pine in the site's eastern portion, increase the diversity of this small conservation reserve.This protected area also serves as a memorial to four Ministry of Natural Resources employees, colleagues and friends, who tragically lost their lives in January, 2003, while conducting an aerial moose survey. Our Colleagues Conservation Reserve was regulated on February 27, 2004. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.Our Colleagues Conservation Reserve is situated approximately 35 km northeast of the City of Elliot Lake, in Hughson Township. It is less than 5 km east of Rawhide Lake Conservation Reserve (C244) and Mississagi Provincial Park (P191), and is surrounded by Multiple Use General Use Area (G1890), and Whiskey-Quirke Lakes Enhanced Management Area (E232a).

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Our Colleagues Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on April 5, 2005 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis, and must pass a Test of Compatibility, and comply with requirements under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures, to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
