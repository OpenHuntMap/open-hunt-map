## Land area description

Woodland Caribou Provincial Park is located between Red Lake and the Manitoba boundary. Woodland Caribou was established in 1983 and is classified as a Wilderness park. This area is located within the Woodland Caribou Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (OLL) (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.Two (2) park additions recommended in OLL were regulated in 2014. They are the Sydney Lake and Douglas Lake additions.Woodland Caribou Provincial Park protects representative earth and life science features such as the Eagle-Finlayson Moraine, prairie-boreal vegetation, Red Lake White Elm site, and habitat for Woodland Caribou, a threatened species. This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report. An important link (with other non-park designations) between the park and the community of Red Lake has also been established.The park provides opportunities for backcountry canoeing, camping and outpost camp and main base lodge fishing. The OLL additions also provide opportunities for hunting and some mechanized recreational travel. This area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.”

## Land use intent

This wilderness class park is protected by regulation under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, 2006.

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992), the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Woodland Caribou Signature Site Management Plan and the Woodland Caribou Provincial Park Management Plan. It is important to note that there are differences in permitted uses for the park area regulated in 1983 compared to the areas regulated in 2014. A primary forest access road crossing between Rowdy and Sydney Lakes will be permitted as determined through the forest management planning processes.With the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements in the Sources of Direction listed below may be out of date.The permitted uses associated with provincial parks are found in approved management direction, which are available on the Ontario Parks website.
