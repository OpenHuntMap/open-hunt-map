## Land area description

This conservation reserve, in Site District 4E-2, protects an exquisite range of topography with steep ridges, cliffs and rock knobs harbouring extensively forested valleys. Nestled between the Chippewa River on the west and north sides, and a tributary of the river in the south, this relatively small protected area demonstrates an unusually high degree of biological diversity. Dense, mature and old-growth sugar maple mixedwood forest communities and pockets of old-growth yellow birch mixedwoods characterize a majority of this conservation reserve. Smaller stands of white spruce, white pine, balsam fir, and eastern white cedar are interspersed throughout portions of the site, and an isolated patch of mixed, mature and regeneration red oak has been identified within the site's center. Meandering portions of the Chippewa River have been encompassed within the site, along with small lakes, ponds, feeder streams and wetlands. Harmony Forest Conservation Reserve was regulated on December 21, 2000. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.The conservation reserve is found within the geographic townships of Olsen and Brule, approximately 65 kilometres north of the City of Sault Ste. Marie. It is situated mostly south of the Chippewa River, north of MacDonald Creek, and east of Mile 38 Road.This site is surrounded by the Batchawana General Use Area (G1908).

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Harmony Forest Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on May 30, 2003 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis, and must pass a Test of Compatibility and comply with requirements under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
