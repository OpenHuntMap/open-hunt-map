## Land area description

The yarding areas for the deer population are situated, for the most part, west of the City of Sault Ste. Marie, east of the city along the north channel of Lake Huron and on St. Joseph Island. One area is found within the limits of the City of Sault Ste. Marie. These yarding areas are of varying sizes. They are extremely important for deer as they provide shelter, food and open areas for travel.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

MNR will manage Crown lands and attempt to influence the use of private lands in a manner that will benefit deer populations. Forest management practices will play a large part in maintaining or improving the habitat.In core areas, land disposition may be controlled to ensure that deer management is not jeopardized. The existing land uses in the area will be maintained but new developments will be encouraged only where the impact on deer populations will be minimal or beneficial (e.g. habitat improvement involving forestry activities).MNR will retain for public use, at least the present area, on Crown islands and Crown shorelines of the Great Lakes (excluding the 20-metre reserve and road allowance).MNR will encourage the preservation of important fisheries habitat, including Echo, Bar, and Thessalon Rivers, and Gawas and Milford Haven Creeks. Rock, Gordon, Ottertail and Desbarats Lakes also require special management and protection from deterioration and destruction of the fisheries (spawning) and waterfowl habitat. All activities in the area will take into consideration the known nesting sites, sensitive wildlife habitats and historical sites. The specific locations can be found in a compendium at the District office.On-shore mining developments may expand underground, outward under the Great Lakes, subject to existing international agreements.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
