## Land area description

Searchmont South Forest Conservation Reserve, in Ecodistrict 5E-13 (formerly classified as Site District 4E-2), protects spectacular rugged topography, and habitat communities associated with the Dam Creek valley. A tributary of the Goulais River (west of the site), Dam Creek, runs through the southern half of the conservation reserve, and Stoney Creek runs perpendicular to Dam Creek, northward. Steep-sided cliff faces, and ridges are located in the western and southeast portions of the site. The rugged terrain supports mature yellow birch and sugar maple mixedwood communities in the northern half of the site, and poplardominatedmixedwood stands in the southern half. Along the ridge in the south, an old-growth yellow birch and sugar maple community is also present. Further to the west, eastern white cedar, tamarak and black spruce are associated with a large wetland complex. Searchmont South Forest Conservation Reserve was regulated on April 6, 2001. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.LOCATION:This protected area is located approximately 40 kilometres (km) northeast of the City of Sault Ste. Marie, and less than 4 km south of the community of Searchmont, in Hodgins Township. The Algoma Central Railway forms a portion of the site’s western boundary, and Highway 556 (Ranger Lake Road) delineates the southern boundary. The Agriculture (G1905), Goulais (G1906), and Ranger-Garden Lakes-Aubinadong Area (G1909) General Use areas surround this conservation reserve.

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Searchmont South Forest Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on May 30, 2003 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis and must pass a Test of Compatibility and comply with requirements under the ClassEnvironmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
