## Land area description

Located approximately 45 kilometres west of the Highway 11 corridor, this area is accessed from Highways 66, 65 and 560. The West Montreal River, Montreal River and the lakes within those two river systems are the dominant natural features. The area includes the communities of Gowganda, Elk Lake and Matachewan. The Township of James and the Township of Matachewan have municipal control over portions of the area.A popular recreation area, particularly for fishing and water based activities, the area contains three designated canoe routes, nine official access points, 13 commercial tourist operations and over 100 cottages.The area includes a number of past producers. Mineral potential is high to medium. Forestry is a major activity and some of the timber commitments for mills are expected from this area.Water control structures include one Ontario Hydro dam and three MNR dams.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The intent for this area is to maintain high quality recreation opportunities, particularly those that are water-based. The priority is to Protect the resource base necessary for public recreation, commercial tourism and cottaging. Resource extraction remains a priority in non-shoreline areas, and plans will be co-ordinated to ensure compatibility with recreation requirements.Water storage for hydroelectric power generation is a significant use in shoreline areas. Industrial development will be encouraged to locate within or adjacent to existing communities and in accordance with municipal development controls. Development on hazard lands will be discouraged.Two historic locations (Powell and Cairo Townships) and two archaeological sites (Baden and Van Hise Townships) will be given consideration during the review of proposals and the implementation of plans. The Ministry will investigate further mechanisms for protecting the regionally significant cultural resources on Duncan Lake and at Fort Matachewan. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Kirkland Lake District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of Natural lake trout lakes:­- no additional access will be permitted­- 120 metre Area of Concern will be applied-­ forest management activities may be permitted within the Area of Concern subject to concerns for erosion/sedimentation and the protection of lake trout habitat.
