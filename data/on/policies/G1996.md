## Land area description

This area is located within the Obabika River watershed. The values and uses of this area include the high-use lake trout lakes (Wawiagama, Yorston), the active logging camp at Wawiagama Lake, and the well-used canoe route through Wawiagama.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Inland Ontario Lakes Designated for Lake Trout Management (2006) accessible in the Source of Direction portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The land use intent is to provide opportunities for the forest and mining related activities while maintaining and enhancing the aesthetics of Wawiagama Lake, Yorston Lake, and other lakes. Additionally, the intent is to protect park-related values adjacent to the Obabika River Provincial Park, Sturgeon River Provincial Park, and connecting canoe routes. Some areas have low-intensity recreational use.The concerns include the aesthetics and recreation values on Wawiagama Lake, Yorston River, Pilgrim Creek, and Sturgeon River canoe routes as well as the effectiveness of Goulard gate in continuing to prevent public motorized access to Fry Lake. Concerns also include the unplanned use of logging camps when no longer required for forest management.The management area objectives are to provide some areas (i.e., lakes and hunting areas) for low-intensity recreational use and ensure no further infrastructure development on Wawiagama Lake. Objectives also include retaining aesthetics along water bodies with recreation values, preventing new roads within 350-metres of park boundaries, and allowing resource extraction with public motorized access. Additionally, the objectives are to review the effectiveness of Goulard gate in continuing to prevent public motorized access through the FMP process and closing logging camps once it is no longer required for forest management.The strategies include planning access and developing road use strategies which will facilitate resource management, provide areas of limited road access for motorized recreation, and ensure appropriate access for surrounding management areas. Strategies also include developing a use management strategy through the FMP process to confirm the most effective means of controlling access on the Goulard Road and applying viewscape and Area of Concern planning to extractive activities.
