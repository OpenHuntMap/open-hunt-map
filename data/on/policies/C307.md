## Land area description

Ile Parisienne Conservation Reserve consists of an island and surrounding waters. The land portion captures most of the island (991 ha), which is 8 kilometres (km) in length north to south, and 2 km at its widest point. Small patented areas of the island are not included in the conservation reserve. A marine zone, extending 1.6 km from the island's shore into Whitefish Bay, accounts for the remaining 80% of the conservation reserve's area. Protected within Ile Parisienne Conservation reserve are provincially significant Precambrian-aged Jacobsville Sandstone bedrock outcrops, postglacial raised beaches of sand and cobble, vegetated sand dunes, and unique boulder lags. Foreshore flats (shoals) of sandstone occur along the entire western side of Ile Parisienne. A sandy beach with dunes along its backshore occurs along the southwest side of the island, near the lighthouse. Abandoned (raised) postglacial Algoma stage beaches and swales also occur inland from the present shore. Large boulders are interspersed along the shallow waters off the western shore of the island, the result of winnowing of the original glacial sediments on the island over the last 3000 years by storm waves. The shoals and waters surrounding the island provided important spawning areas for lake trout and whitefish. The land-based portion of the conservation reserve provides feeding, nesting and breeding habitat for waterfowl, raptors and migratory birds. Located within Site District 4E-2, this site also protects super-canopy white pine and black spruce forest types, balsam fir and white birch approaching old-growth status, and various wetland complexes. A Department of Transport, Coast Guard lighthouse located at the south tip of the island, although not encompassed by the conservation reserve, is a testament to the island's historical importance in marine culture and travel. Ile Parisienne has been used as protection from Lake Superior storms by commercial traffic for over 100 years, and by lake travelers for centuries. Ile Parisienne Conservation Reserve was regulated on April 6, 2001. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.This protected area is located approximately 25 km northwest of the City of Sault Ste. Marie, at the north end of Whitefish Bay, in Lake Superior.Additionally, this protected area is surrounded by the Offshore Lake Superior-North Channel General Use Area (G1910), and is located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Ile Parisienne Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on April 5, 2005 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy and the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy. Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis, and must pass a Test of Compatibility and comply with responsibilities under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
