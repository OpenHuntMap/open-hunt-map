## Land area description

Located just south of the Temagami Area, this large expanse is important to the tourism, recreational and resource sector. Extensive glacial deposits, a moraine, several glaciofluvial kames and a sand and gravel outwash extend southwesterly across the west and north central sections of Sisk and Thistle Townships. Here there are a number of tourism and cottaging lakes. Generally shallow soils with exposed bedrock predominate the south central and the southeast edge portion where cottaging lakes, and brook trout habitats associated with Smoky and Tomiko Rivers, and Kaotisinimigo Lake offer additional recreational value to the overall area. The Nipissing Crown Game Preserve makes up most of the east half of the enhanced management area. This game preserve was established in 1929 to protect resident populations of fur bearers and other wildlife and to conserve and propagate game in local areas. It extends northward into the planning area of the Temagami Land Use Plan. Bedrock terrain with shallow soils and sandy outwash plains are the dominant landforms. The Marten River canoe route is primarily within this eastern section of the area. Two access points, one of which is designated, receive significant use. Surrounding the Kenny Lake Forest, this Crown Game Preserve houses a number of lakes having cottaging, tourism and lake trout habitat value. In the most extreme south east corner are lakes important to the watershed of Jocko River. The area contains both organized and unorganized townships. This area abuts and surrounds several protected areas including: Marten River, Kenny Forest and parts of the Jocko River and Temagami River provincial parks; as well as the McLaren Forest, Holdridge Creek and Gooderham Old Growth conservation reserves.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

This enhanced management area is to be managed as an important tourism and recreation area. Resource extraction activities area to be considered in the context of future semi-remote access planning designated to protect semi-remote tourism portions and important recreation areas; where protection is the main theme of management (e.g. heronries, cold water fisheries habitat, etc.) are found in these areas. These and other such sites, which may be found in the future, will be protected during resource management operations and will be identified as values requiring protection in municipal planning. Land adjacent to Cross Lake will be administrated in a manner conforming to the Temagami Land Use Plan. A number of lakes designated as tourism lakes will be given special consideration in land use decisions because of their importance to the tourist industry. As well, land use planning will consider cottaging lakes. The disposition for new crown land cottage lots is not to occur within semi-remote areas on lakes important to the cottaging industry. A portion of the area is subject to First Nation land claims negotiations; this Enhanced Management Area designation will not hinder or affect that negotiation process. These lands will not be re-opened for staking and will not be included in the forestry land base until this matter is addressed. Any proposed planning on activities adjacent to any provincial park will consider the management intent and direction of these protected areas. MNR is responsible for the management of Crown land and natural resources in unorganized townships.Management of this area to be consistent with protecting recreational and natural heritage values of the various provincial parks and conservation reserves within its area. This area includes the Nipissing Game Preserve but it does not affect any wildlife management regulations within the game preserve.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Land Use Strategy (1999) and the North Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Recreation enhanced management areas have been applied to areas with high recreational use of significant recreation values for activities such as angling, hunting, motorized and pedestrian trail use, and canoeing. The enhanced management area can be used for a wide rang of recreation values and management intents, ranging form areas where relatively substantial recreation development is permitted, to areas providing low-density, high-quality recreation in a natural setting.Where the Recreation enhanced management area has been identified to protect remote recreation values, all activities need to be carried out in such a way as to maintain or enhance the remote recreation qualities.In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of Natural lake trout lakes:­ 120 metre Area of Concern will be applied­ forest management activities may be permitted within the Area of Concern subject to concerns for erosion/sedimentation and the protection of lake trout habitat.
