## Land area description

This area includes the southern part of Lanark County paralleling the Rideau River corridor and swinging north to the Ottawa River. Predominant land uses include extensive agricultural activities as well as forestry and general recreation such as fishing, hunting, boating and cottaging. The Rideau Trail, a major recreation trail, passes through this area. Trapping and wild rice harvesting also occur. Development along the Highway 7 corridor is considered very dense.Special features include: - identified life science features of special significance; Burnt Lands Alvar.- significant deer winter concentration areas; - federal wildlife sanctuary on McEwen Bay of Mississippi Lake- significant wetland areas; Stewart Lake Swamp, Bob's Lake peat bog, Mississippi Lake wild rice areas- significant lake trout and pickerel spawning areas- heron rookery; Otty Lake- silica deposits; Bathurst TownshipProblems and issues:A potential for conflict over use of land, particularly forestry and agriculture, exists although it is not considered critical at present. Critical deer winter concentration areas are threatened by increasing urban development pressure. Similarly, the identified life science features could be seriously damaged by inappropriate development initiatives.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

This area is significant for resource production and recreation and will be managed primarily for forestry and forest related uses. Wildlife and fisheries concerns also will be highlighted, in particular deer winter concentration areas, lake trout lakes, fish spawning areas and wetlands.Discussion: This area represents the primary private land forestry base and includes part of the Lanark County Forest.Urban expansion is reducing the available wildlife habitat and putting stress on the wildlife populations. The wetland areas are significant for their contribution to fur-bearer, bullfrog and wild rice management.One area of natural and scientific interest, the Burnt Lands Alvar, has been identified in this area. The Burnt Lands Alvar is a representative alvar vegetation complex covering some 1,000 hectares (2,500 acres). Of this total area, the Crown land has been designated as a provincial park in the Ontario's Living Legacy Use Strategy. On both Crown and privately owned lands, the nature of the site, shallow soils over lime stone bedrock, makes it susceptible to damage from overuse. Silica deposits occur in this area and are underlying mainly agricultural lands. These deposits have been identified as having a high potential for future extraction.Specific strategies:- encourage forest management to enhance wildlife habitat in identified deer winter concentration areas. In such areas, forestry extension services will involve consultation with wildlife staff; - outline specific management in the district deer management plan which is being prepared; - in co-operation with local municipalities, develop resource management plans for areas of special significance such as the Burnt Lands Alvar - negotiate with the Mississippi Valley Conservation Authority to ensure the maintenance of satisfactory water levels on Mississippi Lake to protect wild rice areas; and- identify concerns regarding the silica deposits to the local municipality. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Carleton Place District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
