## Land area description

Existing activities in this area include urban and rural residential, farming, cottaging, commercial recreation and Crown land recreation. The majority of the area is comprised of patented land. The area is well accessed by provincial highways, township roads and resource access roads. The Eagle Lake Indian Reserve and part of the Wabigoon Indian Reserve are located in this area. This area forms part of the Wabigoon and Dryden Forests.Issues to be addressed in the management of the area include: additional Crown land for agricultural purposes, continuing rural residential development and future cottaging opportunities on Crown land along a portion of the shoreline of Eagle Lake contained within this area.The following are designated as Tourism Lakes: Pritchard and Thunder Lakes.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The primary use of this area will continue to be urban and rural residential development, farming, cottaging, commercial and Crown land recreation. Expansion of the agricultural land base will be encouraged primarily in this area, firstly on existing farms, secondly on patented land and thirdly on Crown land. Disposition of Crown land for agricultural purposes in this area will primarily be of an infilling nature.Eagle Lake may have potential for additional cottaging. Any disposition of Crown land on Eagle Lake for cottaging purposes will be preceded by a lake management plan which would recognize the prime importance of Eagle Lake in terms of the sport fisheries and the tourism development presently on the lake. There will be no Crown land disposition for tourism development which is directly dependent on an increased harvest of fish and wildlife resources.Road access will be managed to facilitate the overall land use intent. Guidelines for resource extraction along Eagle Lake and Wabigoon Lakes will be particularly cognizant of the existing rural residential and recreational activity. All activities in this area will take into consideration the known sensitive areas of three cemeteries; the three seed orchards; the black spruce plantation; the two eagle's nests; the blue heron rookery and the Indian pictograph. All Crown land development and uses will attempt to conform to official plans and zoning by-laws. Other ongoing activities (e.g. forestry, mineral exploration, aggregate extraction, hunting, trapping, commercial fishing, etc.) will continue. MNR will also be looking to private land to encourage forest management practices with the view to realizing a portion of the District's fiber target in this area. It is hoped that future development on the Indian reserves will be compatible with the land use intent expressed for this area. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Dryden District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
