## Land area description

Located to the east and southeast of Sturgeon Lake, this area contains Manion, McLaurin, and Bertrand Townships and part of Abitibi Block No. 7. The many large lakes found within this area are important for tourism recreation opportunities. Present development in the area includes four tourist lodges, seventeen outpost camps, three private recreation camps and a number of private cottages. Bell Lake is currently under licence for commercial fishing. The Allanwater canoe route runs through the southern edge of the area. Several earth science features are located in the Shikag-Pipio Lakes area. These features include loess deposits and parts of the Sioux Lookout Moraine. In the same general area are two historical sites.The following are designated as Tourism Lakes: Quest, Glitter, Vista, Vanessa, Seseganaga, Barge, Kawaweogama.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

The primary intent of this area is to encourage commercial tourism while still allowing timber harvesting to continue. Therefore, existing commercial tourism facilities will be maintained and additional commercial tourism opportunities will be considered on several lakes. Seseganaga, and Kawaweogama Lakes will be recognized as important tourism lakes. The commercial food fishing licence on Bell Lake will be extended.Earth science features in the Shikag-Pipio Lakes area will be documented further in order to determine their sensitivity to development. The osprey and bald eagle sites and the historical sites noted above will be recognized as sensitive areas and protected in resource management plans.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Ignace District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
