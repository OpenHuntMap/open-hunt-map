## Land area description

This area borders the southern and western reaches of Onaping Lake. Most lake development occurs at the southern end of the lake and is not extensive. Several lodges and over 200 privately-owned cottages have been built on the lakeshore. All of this area is held under a Sustainable Forest Licence (SFL). Commercial fur harvesting and bait fishing also occur in this area. Fish and wildlife resources and the rugged terrain have attracted fishermen and hunters to this area. The commercial tourist industry has focused on these resources. Fisheries resources and the moose population have been subjected to over-harvesting. Accessibility associated with timber harvesting has contributed to this problem. This accessibility is also regarded as being in conflict with the desire of some recreationists and tourist operators to prohibit access and maintain a natural environment recreational experience. A declining sport fishery, erosion and dock damage, the latter caused by water fluctuations associated with the operation of control dams by SFL licences, complaints about sewage dumping from boathouses, and inadequate access facilities are major concerns in this area.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Commercial timber harvesting and public and private recreation will continue to be the primary uses. Shoreland development including Crown land recreation activities will be designed to complement private interests. In the remainder of the area, resource extraction activities will have regard for recreation and tourism values and fish and wildlife habitat. Management will be consistent with the protection of Halfway Lake Provincial Park and the Onaping Lake Conservation Reserve.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sudbury District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of Natural lake trout lakes:­ no additional access will be permitted­ 120 metre Area of Concern will be applied­ forest management activities may be permitted within the Area of Concern subject to concerns for erosion/sedimentation and the protection of lake trout habitat.
