## Land area description

The area includes a narrow corridor comprised of the lands bounded by the shoreline of Lake Superior and a line 1 kilometre east of Highway 17 North. The southern boundary of the area is immediately south of Government Creek and extends north to the Montreal River. Development in this area has generally been directed towards the main activities presently occurring: transportation, recreation and tourism. The area is extensively used by local residents and is also considered a prime tourist attraction to the Sault Ste. Marie area. A number of public and private day-use facilities and commercial tourism developments are found in this area. Numerous cottage subdivisions are also found along the shoreline of Lake Superior.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The general land use intent for this area is transportation and recreation with special emphasis for the protection of the scenic landscape along this route. Provisions of recreational facilities will include: cottaging, fishing, picnicking, camping and commercial tourist establishments. MNR will encourage the protection of the fisheries habitat of the following creeks: Jones, Government, Sawmill, Downey, Cabinansha, Cottrell Cove, Mamainse, Mica Bay, Cozens Cove, Alona Bay, Gamma, Beta #1 and #2 and rivers: Harmony, Batchawana, Carp and Pancake. All activities in the area will take into consideration the spawning areas, geology, historical, special features and vegetation sites. The specific location of each can be found in a compendium at the District office. MNR will recognize an MOE sampling station in Kincaid Township. Crown land disposition will be permitted for commercial or residential use in conjunction with existing commercial establishments. Discussion with appropriate local and provincial agencies will occur prior to any disposition. MNR will retain for public use at least the present area and frontage on Crown shoreline of Lake Superior. Onshore mining developments may expand underground, outward under the Great Lakes, subject to existing international agreements. Development proposals and dispositions, including water lots, on the Great Lakes shoreline will be reviewed for conformity with the District’s Shoreline Management Plan.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
