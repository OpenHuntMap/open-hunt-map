## Land area description

Bridgland Spillway Enhanced Management Area is located approximately 16 km north of the Town of Thessalon, in Haughton and Bridgland Townships. This site is situated just south of Mississagi River Provincial Park (P238), and is easily accessed via both Franklin Lake Road and Shellrock Loop Road. In addition to these roads, a number of the Houghton Plantation forest access roads, and a variety of unnamed trails, are found throughout the Enhanced Management Area.Bridgland Spillway Enhanced Management Area encompasses a spectacular landscape rich in glacial history around what is now the Little Thessalon River valley. There was once a great channel of glacial meltwater that flowed southward through the region from ancient lakes. The meltwater scoured the land and the lake levels left tell-tale marks in the landforms. Features include abandoned waterfalls and tracts of scoured land called "scablands", which had their coverings of sand and gravel washed away by extremely powerful meltwater flows. Abandoned river channels now support swamps and bogs. Given that these earth science features are exceptionally well-developed and are a record of the rapidly falling waters of glacial Lake Algonquin, Bridgland Spillway Enhanced Management Area is considered to be provincially significant. This area of earth science significance is in Ecodistrict 5E-1.

## Land use intent

The land use intent of this area is to protect the earth science features found in Bridgland Spillway Enhanced Management Area, while allowing for a range of resource activities.Land use direction and resource management activities will be compatible with protecting the recreation and natural values of Mississagi River Provincial Park.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Blind River District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Natural heritage enhanced management areas are intended to protect areas with significant natural values, while allowing for a range of resource activities. Those uses or management activities not listed in the following table are governed by the existing policy for natural heritage enhanced management areas. Any new uses or commercial activities associated with this enhanced management area will be considered on a case-by-case basis and may be subject to the Class Environmental Assessment for MNR Resource Stewardship and Facility Development Projects (2003).
