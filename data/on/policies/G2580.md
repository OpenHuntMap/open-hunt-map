## Land area description

This area comprises the dominantly upland areas to the east and west of the Turtle River. Access to the southern portions of the area is via the Bowes Camp Road and the Turtle River Road. The area is sparsely developed. The most important existing uses of the area are timber extraction, trapping and hunting. The area lies within the Crossroute Forest. The potential for metallic minerals is low, but scattered areas have high or moderate aggregate potential.The following are designated as tourism lakes: Tesup and Johnson Lakes.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The role of this area will be primarily resource production. Trapping and forest management will continue to be important uses, while hunting activity will increase as the area becomes more accessible by road. Due to the scarcity of existing tourist interests, new roads will be located and managed to serve primarily resource extraction and recreational interests. Moose management has its highest priority within the area. The potential exists to increase commercial fur harvests. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Fort Frances District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
