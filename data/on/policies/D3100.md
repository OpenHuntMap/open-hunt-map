## Land area description

The Cultural Landscape Waterways Dedicated Protected Area is located along many waterwaysand/or portions of waterways as described in the Land Use Strategy for the Whitefeather Forestand Adjacent Areas. Waterways include the Berens River, Throat River, Nungesser River,Serpent River, Mamakwash Lake, Dowling River, and others. The policy map and theWhitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas Land Use Strategy should be referenced for additionaldetail on a case-by-case basis. In all cases, The Cultural Landscape Waterways DedicatedProtected Area is complemented by the adjoining Cultural Landscape Waterway EnhancedManagement Area in various widths, providing an additional focus on remoteness and culturalheritage values.These waterways, which include rivers and lakes along their course, are a defining feature of thelandscape. Waterways are a hub of activity for customary travel and cultural purposes. Sacredsites are found throughout, and First Nation people conduct various traditional activities forlivelihood and cultural purposes. Notable fish and wildlife includes the Berens River lakesturgeon population, woodland caribou and wolverine. Existing development includes severalremote tourism base lodges and outposts, a private boat cache, and several locations with allweather and/or winter road crossing. There is a proposed hydro corridor in the area. There is apotential interest in new crossings for road corridors to access neighboring First Nations.

## Land use intent

The intent is to regulate the cultural landscape waterways as a provincial park or conservationreserve, although the specific designation and category have not been determined. The culturallandscape waterways can provide outstanding recreational water routes with representativenatural features and historical resources, and high quality recreational and educationalexperiences. Maintaining existing tourism, and new eco-cultural tourism, and natural and culturalheritage appreciation are important land use intents. While maintaining the remote nature of thewaterways is a key objective, limited crossing of waterways to facilitate access will be supportedfor new opportunities including mining and forestry, community access, energy transmission andcommunications corridors. Providing support for the conservation of woodland caribou and otherspecies including lake sturgeon is also a key factor.Management will also be governed by the Land Use Strategy for the Whitefeather Forest andAdjacent Area, MNR policies, and/or new policies developed through cross-cultural dialogue.

## Permitted uses — preface

The dedicated protected area designation (i.e. Park Class or Conservation Reserve) is yet to bedetermined. Interim direction for this area is provided in the Land Use Strategy for theWhitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas (WFAA Strategy) and in the table below. Additionalspecific direction will be determined by the final designation assigned to this dedicated protectedarea through cross-cultural dialogue, and by future resource management planning.In the absence of resource management planning, new proposals for specific activities will besubject to a review by MNR in consultation with Pikangikum First Nation. This review will considerthe intent of the area, direction provided in the WFAA Strategy, impacts on resources and valuesassociated with the cultural landscape waterways, compatible economic opportunities, andpotential for economic benefit to Pikangikum First Nation.Values associated with the cultural landscape waterways include the natural and cultural heritagefeatures of the area, special features, and existing land uses. Maintenance of natural disturbanceregimes and remoteness will be given high importance as will the conservation of fish and wildlifespecies, including woodland caribou, and wolverine.
