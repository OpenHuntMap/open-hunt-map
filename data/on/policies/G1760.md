## Land area description

This area includes the critical agricultural lands along the Highway 11 corridor including the Towns of Smooth Rock Falls, Cochrane, Iroquois Falls and most of the Corporation of the Township of Glackmeyer. The majority of land in this area is privately owned. Along Highway 11 there are a number of commercial retail and commercial tourism uses (e.g. hotel/motel, tent and trailer, cabins, lodges, etc.). Within this agricultural area, there is a wide variety of uses. The main uses are urban (approximately 15,000 residents) rural residential (2,500-3,000 residents), farming (approximately 146 farms in total), and cottaging (approximately 144 cottages).Also, the Iroquois Falls Cross-Country Ski Club has a number of kilometres of ski trails in this area on private lands. The area contains improved agricultural lands as well as both Crown and private previously cleared lands that are critical to meeting the areas agricultural demands. In addition to agriculture, some of the areas contain significant aggregate and mineral potential. Forest management activities also constitute a major use in the area Existing hydro electric generating stations are at Long Sault Rapids, Iroquois Falls and Smooth Rock Falls. Residential development on high quality agricultural lands remains a problem in this area. The Taykwa Tagamou Nation (formerly New Post First Nation),has indicated that its’ reserve in Mewhinney Township inadequate for residential purposes. As a result, MNR has permitted the relocation of the Taykwa Tagamou Nation to a 140 hectare parcel in Brower TownshipThis area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

The priority in this area is the protection and use of Class 3 and 4 agricultural areas for agricultural purposes. Urban and industrial uses are a priority in urban areas. MNR will encourage municipal planning documents to restrict non farm development on critical agricultural land outside urban areas. Only Calder, Newmarket, Pyne, Fox, Kennedy and portions of Fournier, Clute and Blount Townships have no municipal planning control. Structural development will not be permitted on lands subject to shoreline erosion or flooding. One special management area, the Nahma Bog, is located in this agricultural area. The 300 hectare Nahma Bog is a raised string bog of provincial significance. The bog is sensitive to changes in water levels and may also be damaged by fire, construction or through the alteration of its nutrient stability. Disruption of this bog site will be discouraged. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Chochrane District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
