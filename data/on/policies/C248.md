## Land area description

This conservation reserve, in Eco District 5E-13 (formerly classified as Site District 4E-2), was designated to protect old, deciduous forests growing on moderately rolling hills consisting of glaciofluvial deposits. Dominant upland forest types include yellow birch, sugar maple and red maple communities, some of which are approaching old-growth status. White spruce, black spruce, and balsam fir populate low-lying valley areas. Associated with several small unnamed lakes and creeks systems, including O'Connor Creek, are wetland communities (meadow marshes, thicket swamps, conifer swamps and muskegs) scattered throughout the site. La Verendrye/Ogidaki Conservation Reserve was regulated on May 21, 2003. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.This protected area is situated approximately 60 kilometres (km) northeast of the City of Sault Ste. Marie, in Dablon, Lunkie, La Verendrye, and Hynes townships. Located less than 5 km from this site are O'Connor Conservation Reserve (C294), and Goulais River Provincial Park (P253) which is also part of the Algoma Headwaters Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). This conservation reserve is immediately surrounded by the Batchawana (G1908) and Ranger-Garden Lakes-Aubinadong Area (G1909) General Use areas.

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The La Verendrye/Ogidaki Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on April 5, 2005 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy. Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis, and must pass a Test of Compatibility, and comply with requirements under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures, to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
