## Land area description

The Vermilion River Enhanced Management Area consists of three main land areas situated along the Vermilion River: The Vermilion River Delta, a riverine and riparian land section of the Vermilion River, and the area north of Onwatin Lake formerly identified in Ontario’s Living Legacy(1999) as Capreol/Hanmer Delta Forest Reserve.The Vermilion River and the Vermilion River Delta are two Provincially Significant Wetlands, located on the fringe of a highly populated and developed urban/rural area and are unique physiographical and biological features in the Sudbury Region. The many river meanders,accompanied by numerous ox bow lakes, are an excellent example of river dynamics in concert with vegetative succession. This riverine system extends some 35 kilometres between Onwatin Lake in the northeast to Vermilion Lake in the southwest.The Vermilion River area contains glaciofluvial outwash deposits, glaciofluvial delta deposits, icecontact kame moraines and eskers, and glaciolacustrine beach deposits which were depositedduring the Pleistocene Period of continental glaciation. The Onwatin Lake area to the northeast was once the mouth of an ancient river flowing into a small glacial lake that occupied the Sudbury Basin. This landform, on the north rim of the Sudbury Basin, is now elevated above thesurrounding landscape. There is a history of ancient lake fluctuation on this landform, including four terraces that were eroded into the flanks of the delta. Poplar and white birch stands now cover this landform, which offers significant four season recreational opportunities to the City ofGreater Sudbury. The Vermilion River Delta area contains a diverse array of swamp and marsh communities along with important waterfowl staging habitat.

## Land use intent

The Vermilion River and Vermilion River Delta are accessible and have great educational, recreational, and interpretive potential within the Sudbury Region. Land use direction and resource management activities within these areas will protect the hydrologic and/or biological connections between the ox bows and riparian wetland pockets along the corridor; proposed activities within the Onwatin and the Vermilion River Delta area will be subject to mitigation thatminimizes adverse affects upon natural and recreational values. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Sudbury District Land Use Guidelines (1983). The Vermilion River and Vermilion River Delta have been evaluated as Provincially Significant Wetlands and will be subject to special land use planningconsiderations.
