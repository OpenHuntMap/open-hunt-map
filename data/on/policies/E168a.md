## Land area description

Large scenic island located on the upper French River, containing a mature hardwood forest with sugar maple, hemlock and yellow birch. The southern shoreline is located along the French River Provincial Park, an area of high recreation and tourism values. Forest management activities are important in areas set back from the river. Constraints to development include property damage caused by water level fluctuations, and flood hazards or wave action along the length of the River. The floodplain mapping will be used as a development control guide to prevent residential development in flood prone areas.

## Land use intent

This area is situated on the French River and borders the French River Provincial Park. The land use intent is to maintain the remote access characteristics of the area for recreational purposes while providing for continued forest management. The entire island will be managed as a remote access area by maintaining the existing road access controls at the bridge. This remote access land use direction supports protection of the adjacent French River Provincial Park.Public private recreation and tourism will be the dominant uses. Forestry, mining, wildlife management, bait fishing, etc. will continue as secondary uses as long as they do not degrade the recreational landscape or the resources upon which recreation and tourism depend. The mining rights to the islands in the French River were withdrawn under the Mining Act in 1926.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Sudbury District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Remote access enhanced management area are intended to maintain the remote character of selected areas. Typically, these are relatively large areas which provide the public and tourism operators with high-quality remote recreational experiences including hunting, fishing, canoeing, and camping. Given the large size, remoteness, and relative absence of roads, these areas will play a significant role in protecting wilderness values outside the parks and protected areas system.
