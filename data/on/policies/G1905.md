## Land area description

This area comprises the lands of critical agricultural importance. The majority of these lands are found east of the City of Sault Ste. Marie and on St. Joseph Island. Pockets of agricultural lands are also found along the Goulais River watershed and west of the City of Sault Ste. Marie. Most of the area has organized municipal governments in place with existing official plans and associated zoning by-laws. The primary present land use is agriculture. Other land uses include recreation and some resource production.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The general land use intent is agriculture. MNR will encourage through the local planning boards the maintenance of good agricultural farm lands, and will actively discourage the conversion of these lands to other uses which would effectively remove agricultural lands from production for a long time. Other compatible resource production activities such as forest management will also be encouraged. Some deer yards are found within this area, located mostly in the uncultivated part of the area (bush lots). In these yarding areas, MNR will encourage their protection and maintenance essentially through proper forest management techniques. MNR will support uses which will not remove areas of high agricultural potential from agriculture. MNR will encourage the preservation of important fisheries habitat including Gawas Creek and Milford Haven Creek, Echo River, Thessalon River and Bar River. All activities in the area will take into consideration the known nesting sites, geology sites and spawning areas. The specific locations can be found in a compendium at the District office. On-shore mining developments may expand underground, outward under the Great Lakes, subject to existing international agreements. MNR will retain for public use, at least the present area, on Crown islands and Crown shorelines of the Great Lakes (excluding the 20-metre reserve and road allowance).Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
