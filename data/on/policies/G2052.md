## Land area description

Glaciofluvial outwash deposits, glaciofluvial delta deposits, ice-contact kame moraines and eskers, and glaciolocustine beach deposits were deposited during the Pleistocene Period of continental glaciation.Glaciofluvial outwash deposits, formed by deposition of water carried materials along glaciofluvial spillways and at the terminus of spillways, are concentrated in the Cartier-Levack area (Venturi, Hart, Moncrieff, Hess, Harty and Cascaden Townships). They are generally located in a north-south direction and follow the major river systems such as the Vermilion River. They generally contain well stratified sand and gravel materials.Glaciofluvial deltas occur where glaciofluvial outwash was deposited into glacial Lake Algonquin. Major deltas occurred at the end of the Onaping Spillway (Dowling Township), Nelson Spillway (Lumsden Township), Vermilion Spillway (Hammer and Capreol Townships) and Wanapitei Spillway (Aylmer and MacLennan Townships). These deltas cover an extensive area and contain large amounts of well stratified sands and gravels.Ice contact deposits occur as kame moraines (water carried deposits) and eskers (long ridges formed by rivers flowing beneath the glacial ice). The major kame deposits formed in the District are located in Trill and Hammer Townships. Along with sand and gravel, the kames also contain varying amounts of silt, clay and till. Eskers are relatively abundant in the north-central and western portions of the District, but are small and located in Dowling and Cascaden Townships and discontinuous. Two relatively larger eskers are in MacLennan, Falconbridge and Garson Townships. Glaciolocustrine raised beach deposits occur as small, discontinuous sand and gravel deposits within the area that was once inundated by glacial Lake Algonquin. These deposits commonly occur up against the southern exposure of bedrock knobs at elevations between 250- 300 metres above sea level. Pockets of coarse gravel, silt, clay and till also occur along with the sand and gravel. These deposits occur in the southern half of the District, primarily in Eden, Dill, Secord, Laura, Halifax, Attlee, Sale, Carlyle, Rutherford, Waldie, Cosby, Hoskin and Cherriman Townships. Two isolated deposits in Dryden, Street, Scadding and Loughrin Townships are also glaciolocustrine deposits. These areas are considered essential to accommodate the demand for mineral aggregates. Those deposits not included under area 16 are included under area 1 and area 15 in the Hagar- Charles area. They were not considered essential for target achievement and will accommodate local wayside pit requirements instead.Reference should be made to Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources Sudbury for a more detailed description of the boundaries of each mineral aggregate deposit.A portion of this area is located within the Spanish River Signature Site, the Killarney Signature Site and the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, two of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

All new commercial aggregate operations will be promoted in these areas. Other uses which will not preclude aggregate extraction will be permitted. The Sudbury Airport is situated on the large aggregate deposit located just southwest of Boland's Bay, Lake Wanapitei. Any development near the airport must comply with Ministry of Transportation airport requirements. At the time of any Crown land disposition or the issuance of any applicable aggregate permits and work permits or licences in this area, these requirements and the following publications will be consulted and relevant conditions applied: Land Use Policy Near Airports; and, Ontario Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing, Height Restrictions Adjacent to Airports.The development of those deposits adjacent to District Land Use Guidelines areas 7 and 8 will occur with regard for the land use intent of the adjacent land use policy areas in areas. For those deposits in proximity to Killarney Provincial Park, site planning will ensure that the recreational values associated with the park are not jeopardized.
