## Land area description

An area extending from Bluff and Ducell Lakes northerly to Chorus Lake and from the Steel River Provincial Park westerly to a line just east of Rocke and Chapman Lakes. The area is part of both the Kenogami Forest and the Pic River Ojibway Forest. Present uses of the area are timber harvesting and public recreation. The Aguasabon River Diversion is of primary importance to the generating station at Terrace Bay. This area borders the Fishnet Lake Conservation Reserve in the southeast corner.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The primary intent of the area is to provide for resource extraction activities such as forestry and mining. The area may also provide increased public recreational opportunities for such unstructured activities as hunting, fishing, camping and picnicking. Only developments associated with water control or timber extraction will be permitted on or adjacent to Water Power Lease Agreement 22. In this particular area, activities will take into account the presence of a known Osprey nesting site and a black spruce provenance study area. The existing roads in this area should continue to provide access for resource extraction and recreational activities. New commercial tourism development will be permitted at a site specific location known as Chorus Landing. New commercial tourism elsewhere within the area will not be considered.Lake trout and tourism lakes will be given protection.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Terrace Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).Aguasabon River General Use Area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Aguasabon River General Use Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS) at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .
