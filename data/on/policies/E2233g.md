## Land area description

The Lake Superior Shoreline is a scenic and highly traveled coastline with rugged cliffs and bays that provide habitat for representative and endangered wildlife and vegetation. This area has high tourism and recreational value, and many pristine environments.This area is located along the northern coast of Lake Superior between the Town of Nipigon and the Pic River south of Marathon, and generally includes all land between Highway 17 and Lake Superior except where the lands have been recommended for regulation as Provincial Parks or Conservation Reserves. Between the hamlet of Rossport and Rainbow Falls Provincial Park, the boundary is set 1 kilometre back from the lakeshore. With the exception of the larger areas of Crown land, most of the municipalities of Schreiber, Terrace Bay and Marathon are excluded. The Lake Superior Shoreline Enhanced Management Area is largely Crown land, but there are a number of private properties, mining claims and mining leases within the designation. A 200-hectare Area of Natural and Scientific Interest is located along the west bank of the Pic River near its mouth. This ANSI contains a significant array of earth science, life science, historic and prehistoric values.Lake Superior is designated as a tourism lake.This area is located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The area is intended to provide long term tourism and recreational benefits to local communities through conservation of the coastline’s significant scenic, recreational and tourism attributes. Local municipalities, First Nations and resource stakeholders will play a major role in the future planning and management of this area.In the coastal areas, ecosystem protection and the promotion of recreation and tourism are the primary land use and resource management goals.Crown land cottaging will not be encouraged. Resource management plans will take into consideration known wildlife and geological features. Traditional Crown land activities such as commercial and sport fishing, hunting, fur harvesting, boating, camping and trail use will be permitted to continue. Management of the area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Nipigon District Land Use Guidelines (1983) and the Terrace Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).Lake Superior Shoreline Enhanced Management Area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Lake Superior Shoreline Enhanced Management Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS) at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .

## Permitted uses — preface

Great Lakes Coastal Areas is a category of enhanced management area that has been applied to specific land and waters along the shore and islands of Georgian Bay, the North Channel, and Lake Superior. This category fits within a larger "featured area" concept-- the Great Lakes Heritage Coast-- that applies to all Crown lands along the portion of Great Lakes shoreline within the planning area. The Great Lakes Enhanced Management Area has been applied in conjunction with the other land use designations and enhanced management areas that occur within the Heritage Coast.Fires on the island portions of this enhanced management area that threaten human life, property or other values will receive a full response. All other fires on the islands will be monitored, and if risks are acceptiable, allowed to burn in order to obtain ecological benefits. Consult the Interim Fire Response Plan for the Islands of Lake Superior (2005) for further direction on fire supression.In the coastal areas, ecosystem protection and the promotion of recreation and tourism are the primary land use and resource management goals.Activities occurring on or within the water portion of this land use category should be undertaken in a manner consistent with the recreation and tourism focus of the category and with the land use intent of the adjacent land use category.
