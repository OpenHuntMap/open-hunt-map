## Land area description

The Ottertail Creek Conservation Reserve is located within the Ottertail Creek watershed. Values and uses within the reserve include representative old growth white and red pine stands that are between 121 and 130 years old, growing in combination with younger pine stands on a broken till plain (these stands are the only old growth pine sites found on this landform type in the Temagami Area). There are also a number of old growth black spruce stands, a regionally significant wetland, and a number of moose aquatic feeding areas in the reserve.Ottertail Creek was regulated as a conservation reserve on July 19,1997.

## Land use intent

The intent of the Ottertail Creek Conservation Reserve is to protect the representative natural heritage values and to provide opportunities for research, low impact recreation, natural heritage appreciation and interpretation.The management area objectives are to allow natural processes to occur. The concerns include the need for natural disturbances to create suitable conditions for pine regeneration. The land use strategy is to identify appropriate natural heritage appreciation opportunities (e.g., trails).

## Permitted uses — preface

Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing conservation reserve policy, the Temagami Land Use Plan, and the Ottertail Creek conservation reserve Statement of Conservation Interest. Any new uses and commercial activities associated with conservation reserves will be considered on a case-by-case basis and will be required to pass a test of compatibility to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information.
