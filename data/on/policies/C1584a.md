## Land area description

This site contains moderately broken shallow sandy till uplands made of lacustrine fine sand and weakly broken deep lacustrine organic clay plain. On the sandy till landform 31 to 100 year old black spruce and 31 to 70 year old white birch and jack pine with 41 to 110 year old white cedar dominate the vegetative stands. On clay deposits age class 2 black spruce, white birch and white cedar prevail. Also present are weakly broken aeolian organic sands and weakly broken outwash plain areas. Here age class 2 black spruce also predominate with other conifers and deciduous stands present in stand sizes between 100 and 1 hectare in size. Wetland areas occur south of Tatachikapika River. This addition will increase the size of the parent conservation reserve by 628 hectares, thus permanently protecting a greater portion of its representative ecosystem, biodiversity and the provincially significant natural and cultural heritage.

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this conservation reserve is carried out in accordance with the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act 2006, Conservation Reserve Policy, and Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Future management direction (Statement of Conservation Interest) will elaborated on permitted uses. Any new uses, and commercial activities associated with conservation reserves, will be considered on a case by case basis, and they must pass a test of compatibility to be acceptable.
