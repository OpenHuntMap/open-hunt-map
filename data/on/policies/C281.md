## Land area description

Tilley Creek West Conservation Reserve, in Ecodistrict 5E-13 (formerly classified as Site District 4E-2), is characterized by a varied topography of lowland areas backed by a series of ridges, supporting a high concentration of tolerant hardwood tree species. Pure stands of mature sugar maple, as well as mixed stands of red maple, yellow birch, balsam fir, hemlock and eastern white pine grow on hilly bedrock-drift deposits. Several tributaries of Tilley Creek (west), part of the Lake Superior Watershed, pass through the conservation reserve. Organic deposits associated with this creek system support a thicket swamp and a treedspruce bog in the northwest corner of the site. A wetland complex, situated in the center of the site, contains mixedwood species dominated by balsam fir, white birch, yellow birch, white spruce, sugar maple, eastern white cedar, black spruce, and tamarack. Tilley Creek West Conservation Reserve was regulated on December 21, 2000. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.LOCATION:This conservation reserve is situated within the geographic township of Wishart, approximately 55 kilometres (km) north of the City of Sault Ste. Marie, between Tilley Lake South and Kerwin Lake. It is less than 5 km east of the Batchawana Bay Carp/Lake Raised Delta Enhanced Management Area (E280n), and Batchawana River Provincial Park (P282). Additionally, this site is surrounded by the Batchawana General Use Area (G1908).

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Tilley Creek West Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on May 30, 2003 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis, and must pass a Test of Compatibility, and comply with requirements under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), to be acceptable. Compatibility is normallydetermined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
