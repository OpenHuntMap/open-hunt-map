## Land area description

The Alexander Lake Forest, located in the townships of Antone and Mattawa, offers significant shoreline protection along the Ottawa River. Located in the south eastern portion of the Site District 5E-6, this area is the best and only representative of Strongly Broken RockKnob Terrain; it also represents a good example of an Esker Outwash Complex. These valley landforms shelter remnant old growth forest and a diversity of habitat types flora and fauna and locally significant species. This contains an esker/outwash complex and steep hills of broken rock knob terrain along the Ottawa River in Site District 5E-6. These valley landforms shelter remnant old growth forests and at least 17 different vegetation associations.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

In Natural Environment Parks, the emphasis is on providing high quality recreational and educational experiences in an attractive outdoor setting. Park management will incorporate recreational landscape with representative natural features to provide a high quality recreational and educational experience.Resource Planning will consider the sensitivity of this site related to the steep topography and often shallow soils of the rock lands hilltops and esker ridges which support some of the more significant old growth forest associations. The braided esker has been recognized as a significant earth science feature that has been modified by lake water. Crooked Lake divides the esker into two parts. Along the shore line of Crooked Lake significant erosion of the esker by the lake waters has occurred. While there are no species recorded that are rare, the presence of certain southern species (Beech, Basswood, Hobblebush and Silver Maple, indicate favorable rich site conditions exists to support species approaching their biogeographic limits. Protection has been afforded in the past with the site designated as a no-cut area to protect natural or near natural forested communities that are undisturbed and/or in the process of recovery from human disturbance.The intent is to manage this area as a Natural Environmental Class provincial park.Management of this area is governed by policies for provincial parks including direction as amended by Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999).
