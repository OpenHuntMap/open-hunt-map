## Land area description

The Temagami River Park is located in the geographic townships of McWilliams, Thistle and McCallom (within the West Nipissing Planning Area and the area governed by the West Nipissing Planning Board). This waterway park begins below Surveyor Lake and follows the river south through Red Cedar and Thistle lakes to bellow Ragged Chute, just 8 kilometers northeast of River Valley. The park ends where the Temagami River flows under the Baie Jeanne Road. The park encompass only part of Red Cedar Lake including Campten Bay and several islands and incorporates all of Island (Thistle) Lake with its many islands. On Red Cedar Lake, the park links to the western limits of Holdridge Creek Conservation Reserve (C142).A short waterway, in terms of total length, the Temagami River offers 30 kilometres of very exciting white water between Cross Lake and Red Cedar Lake and then again between Red Cedar and the bridge crossing north of River Valley. Rapids sections like Ragged Chutes, attract expert canoeists. This bedrock controlled drainage has some very scenic sections with small islands and a narrow gorge. Shorelands are forested with White Pine, Jack Pine and Poplar. The river is in Site Districts 5E-6 and 5E-4.Temagami River Provincial Park was regulated as a provincial park on December 21, 2000.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Waterway parks are outstanding recreational water routes and are meant to provide high quality recreational and educational experiences. Park planning will incorporated 'white' river appeal with scenic sections and other recreational/educational experiences.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999).

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992), the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Interim Management Statement, where available. The Interim Management Statement provides direction to ensure custodial management of park resources until such time as a park management planning process is conducted, which includes public review of proposals. At that time, significant decisions regarding resource stewardship, operations, development and permitted uses are made.
