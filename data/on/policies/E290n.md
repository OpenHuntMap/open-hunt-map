## Land area description

Bellevue Hanging Delta Enhanced Management Area is located in the Goulais River valley approximately 32 km northeast of the City of Sault Ste. Marie and 1.5 km north of the Algoma Central Railway bridge at Bellevue. The Enhanced Management Area straddles the VanKoughnet and Deroche Township boundary, with the majority of the site located in VanKoughnet Township. Considering the rugged terrain, the site is relatively accessible. Access to the site can be achieved by taking an unmarked forest access road which runs north from Hwy 556 to Quinn Lake and Maple Lake. In addition to the forest access road, backcountry ski trails and mountain bike trails enter the Enhanced Management Area at numerous locations. Bellevue Hanging Delta Enhanced Management Area was established to protect a hanging delta created during glacial times. Historically, the waters of glacial Lake Algonquin lapped at the edge of a glacier, and a delta formed where a river entered the lake. As the water level of the glacial Lake Algonquin dropped, it left the former delta, deposited when the water was higher, "hanging" on the edge of the steep hill. In addition to the hanging delta, there are a number of old beach bluffs and kettle features found throughout the Enhanced Management Area. The beach bluffs were also formed as the water levels of the glacial Lake Algonquin dropped. Bellevue Hanging Delta Enhanced Management Area is situated in Ecodistrict 5E-13 and provides one of the most dramatic displays of the glacial Lake Algonquin on the Canadian side of Lake Superior. As such, this site is considered to be provincially significant.

## Land use intent

The land use intent of this area is to protect the provincially significant earth science features in Bellevue Hanging Delta Enhanced Management Area, while allowing for a wide range of resource management and recreational activities.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Natural heritage enhanced management areas are intended to protect areas with significant natural values, while allowing for a range of resource activities. Any new uses or commercial activities associated with this enhanced management area will be considered on a case-by-case basis and may be subject to the Class Environmental Assessment for MNR Resource Stewardship and Facility Development Projects (2003).
