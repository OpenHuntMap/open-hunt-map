## Land area description

This waterway, in Ecodistrict 5E-10 protects Waterloo Lake Uplands Area of Natural and Scientific Interestand an ecological link to Algonquin Park. The area offers a variety of recreational opportunities including anAlgonquin Park canoe route that connects to the Ottawa River.Bissett Creek flows from Big Bissett Lake in Algonquin Park to the Ottawa River. Waterloo Lake, which contains lake trout and brook trout, drains via Waterloo Creek into Bissett Creek near the Algonquin Park boundary. At the west end of Waterloo Lake is a rugged, bedrock controlled landscape dominated by young, intolerant coniferous and mixed upland forests on thin soil or on virtually open bedrock. Closer to Algonquin Park, Bissett Creek is more rugged containing small waterfalls and large boulders in the creek bed. Bissett Creek is flatter and shallower as it meanders through relatively flat terrain towards the Ottawa River.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The park was regulated as a waterway class park on June 20, 2006.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999).

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning andManagement Policies (1992), the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Bissett CreekProvincial Park Interim Management Statement (2007). The Interim Management Statement provides directionto ensure custodial management of park resources until such time as a park planning is conducted, whichincludes public and Aboriginal review of proposals. At that time, significant decisions regarding resourcestewardship, operations, development and permitted uses will be made.
