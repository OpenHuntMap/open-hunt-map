## Land area description

This Enhanced Management Area (EMA) referred to as the Amethyst Highlands is centred on a group of sixteen naturally reproducing brook trout lakes, which have limited or no road access. It is located approximately 50 kilometres northeast of the City of Thunder Bay, within the Municipality of Shuniah, the Township of Dorion and unorganized territory. There are substantial areas of private land interspersed throughout the eastern portion of the EMA. Of the sixteen naturally reproducing brook trout lakes to be protected in the EMA; - Focal Lakes: Thirteen lakes are considered to be focal lakes; namely Anderson, Bigger, Bisect, Elbow, Hilma, Little Hilma, Hunter’s 2 and Hunter’s 3 (collectively known as Hunters Lakes), Upper Hunter’s, MacIntosh, Clegg, Long and Wideman- Secondary Lakes: Three lakes are considered to be secondary lakes; namely Nalla, Sprat and Mackenzie.Access to the EMA will be through existing routes only, providing a regionally unique and sustainable angling experience, as well as other backcountry recreational opportunities. Existing activities in this area include: mineral exploration, amethyst mining, cottaging, Crown land recreation, wind power generation, sport fishing, hunting, trapping, bait harvesting and forestry.

## Land use intent

The primary land use intent for this area is to provide for the sustainability of the brook trout resource and to maintain the remoteness of the area. Other land uses and activities may occur in a manner that is consistent with the primary intent and where permitted by the specific land use policies for this area. The key means to achieve this primary intent will be through: 1) Managing access within the EMA to reduce the risk of introductions of non-native species and overfishing. This will be accomplished by:I. Prohibiting development of new roads or trails (including winter road or trails) for any purpose within 1000m of the thirteen focal brook trout lakes. Any activities occurring within this zone that require road access must use existing roads or trails.II. Prohibiting development of new roads or trails for any purposes (including winter road or trails) within 120m of the three secondary brook trout lakes.III. Applying road use restrictions and rehabilitation requirements to all new permitted roads that are planned and/or developed. Upgrades of existing roads will be considered for commercial purposes; however public access within the area will not be improved.IV. Allowing recreational access into the Highlands through existing routes, including trails and portages.2) Excluding activities within 600 m of the thirteen focal lakes and within 120m three secondary lakes that have the potential to negatively impact the water quality and aesthetic of these lakes. Good water quality is important to the success of brook trout populations. The aesthetics of these lakes is needed to preserve the remote fishing experience.3) Excluding activities within the groundwater recharge areas that eventually discharge into the thirteen above named focal lakes and the three secondary lakes. Intact groundwater flow systems are critical for the spawning success of brook trout. In order to protect these sensitive recharge areas, activities that could result in an adverse impact will not be permitted.This area also contains lakes designated for lake trout management: Nolan, Mackenzie, MacInotsh, Hilma, Little Hilma and Nalla. For a current list of the designated lakes associated with this area, please refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index, accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained within the Thunder Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Fish and Wildlife Enhanced Management Areas are intended to be managed for the maintenance and enhancement of fish and wildlife habitat and populations, while allowing for the multiple uses of other natural resources. These areas may include: known or potential fish or wildlife habitat for species that require special management practices, or areas where enhanced hunting and fishing opportunities may be provided through specific management and land use practices.
