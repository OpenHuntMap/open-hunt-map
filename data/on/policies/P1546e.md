## Land area description

Chapleau-Nemegosenda River Provincial Park is located 100 kilometres northeast of Chapleau, in Ecodistrict 3E-5. The park is generally flat and dominated by weakly broken deep lacustrine sand plain with diverse age classes of black spruce, poplar/aspen and jack pine. Mixed age white birch, and white cedar, and young white spruce are represented. Also, trace amounts of young white pine and larch ranging in ages up to 120 years old are present.The park combines two north flowing rivers, the Chapleau on the west and Nemegosenda on the east. Both rivers are free flowing on the Precambrian Canadian Shield. These river valleys include provincially significant lowland tamarack and black spruce forest associations along with veteran white cedar and regionally and locally significant open marsh/fen wetlands. There are also locally significant white pine associations that grow on glaciofluvial esker-kettle landforms. Species found in the park are typical of the Boreal Forest Region, although some species typical of the Great Lakes-St. Lawrence Forest Region are also present.The park provides excellent outdoor opportunities including motor boating, canoeing, backcountry camping, fishing and hunting.Chapleau-Nemegosenda River Provincial Park is a waterway park and was originally regulated in 1973. Additions were made to the park in 2003 and 2005, both as a result of Ontario’s Living Legacy.

## Land use intent

Chapleau-Nemegosenda Provincial Park will be managed according to Provincial Park policy and Interim Management Statement (1991).
