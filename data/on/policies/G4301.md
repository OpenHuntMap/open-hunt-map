## Land area description

This policy report applies to provincial Crown land in the Minesing Swamp, which is located about 16 kilometers west of Barrie in the Township of Clearview, Simcoe County. The Swamp is a 6,000-hectare Class 1 Provincially Significant Wetland. Key waterways flowing through the Swamp are the Nottawasaga River, Mad River, Coates Creek and Willow Creek. Minesing Swamp acts as a major water storage area and is an important natural flood control reservoir. Its vast size, wide diversity of plants and animals, and unique combination of adjacent boreal and Carolinian forests make the area provincially and nationally significant. Minesing Swamp lands are owned by several agencies and individuals; with Nottawasaga Valley Conservation Authority owning the majority. Management of the area is supported by the Nottawasaga Valley Conservation Authority. These Crown lands are also located within the boundaries of the A Place to Grow: Growth Plan for the Greater Golden Horseshoe. These lands contribute to the Natural Heritage System identified under this plan.

## Land use intent

The goals for the Minesing Swamp General Use Area are: • to preserve and restore where appropriate and feasible, through sound management, the ecological functioning of the Swamp; and • to direct management efforts to successfully integrate wildlife interests with important human uses and activities. The area shall be managed in accordance with the following objectives: • To protect the ecological integrity of Minesing Swamp; and • To promote compatible educational, recreational and tourism opportunities within Minesing Swamp.

## Permitted uses — preface

MNRF will consider the Land Use Intent and Management Direction outlined in this policy report when reviewing applications for permitted activities that require licences, leases, permits, or other forms of approval. Commercial Activities and Land and Resource Management Activities listed as permitted may require authorization under the relevant legislation (e.g., Public Lands Act, Aggregate Resources Act, Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act, etc.). The review of individual applications involves the consideration of a variety of factors and requirements on an application and site-specific basis, in addition to land use policy. Individuals seeking to carry out any of the permitted activities listed below should contact the local MNRF district office to find out if an authorization is required. Crown lands are managed in a manner consistent with the Constitutional protection afforded to existing Aboriginal and treaty rights.
