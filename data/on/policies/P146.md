## Land area description

Widdifield Forest, located within the northern portion of Widdifield Township, extends into the southern part of Mulch Township. The site is 20 kilometres northeast of North Bay, just west of the village of Redbridge. Part of the site falls within the municipality of North Bay.The site is a diverse area of landforms and representative vegetation communities. The hilly glacial deposits of sand, clay, silt and rich soils are covered by mature stands of sugar maple, yellow birch and eastern hemlock. Some trees in the forest have been found to be from 120 to 170 years old, remnants of a much older forest. The deep loamy soils here are uncommon for this Eco District (5E-5). These site attributes have created an uncharacteristic combination of landform and vegetation not found in other locally protected areas.The most northerly portion of the Widdifield Forest consists of a large wetland which is comprised of marsh, fen and conifer swamp. The conifer of the forest contains small, shallow beaver controlled lakes and ponds which provide water to several small lakes to the north and the low-lying wetland. The cold waters of these lakes are also an important source for three major brook trout streams; the North River, Widdifield Creek and Redbridge Creek systems, which flow into the Mattawa River. The site, combining its original landforms and at least 20 distinct vegetation communities, is important habitat for waterfowl, large mammals and several rare and uncommon species of plants.Widdifield Forest Provincial Park was regulated as a provincial park on December 7, 2002.

## Land use intent

Land use planning emphasis will be on providing high quality recreational and educational experience in an attractive outdoor setting. Natural environment parks incorporate outstanding recreational landscapes with representative natural and historic features to provide high quality recreational and educational experiences. Land Use and Resource Use decisions must consider the sensitivity of the site related to maintenance of the cold water streams and a native forest cover.In addition the hydrology of the site, being a headwater, is sensitive to other disturbances that may affect discharge into streams that flow south into the Mattawa River. Steep slopes are sensitive to vegetation removal and wildlife (herons, raptors) are sensitive to disturbances during critical nesting periods. Some earlier protection was afforded to the site through a land use designation of it as a no-cut area.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999).
