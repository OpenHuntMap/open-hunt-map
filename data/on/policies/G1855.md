## Land area description

This area is accessed by Highway 11, Highway 101 and forest access roads but is sparsely settled. The rural areas of five municipalities, the Township of Black River-Matheson, the Town of Kirkland Lake, the Township of Larder Lake, the Township of McGarry and the Township of Gauthier, are within the area. Four other townships, Munro, Michaud, McCool and Guibord are within the Black River-Matheson Planning Area but the implementing zoning by-law does not include those four townships. Part of the area is under a sanitary control order held by the Town of Kirkland Lake. Forestry is the major activity in the area. Mineral potential is high to medium. Some good quality aggregate areas are found but distance to market influences their utilization. Recreation activities are generally low density types (e.g. hunting and fishing). There is one official access point on the Ghost River. The area includes moose winter concentration and habitat areas (e.g. Ghost Range and near Dokis Lake) and wetland wildlife areas (Ghost River Marsh, Guibord Lake). Clarice Lake is partially within the Province of Quebec.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

The intent of this area is to provide an adequate land base for resource extractive activities, particularly forestry and mining. Other extractive activities and general recreation activities will also be encouraged. The Black River-Whiteclay River will be managed to provide recreation and tourism opportunities. Where applicable, development will be encouraged to conform to municipal land use policies and controls. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Kirkland Lake District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
