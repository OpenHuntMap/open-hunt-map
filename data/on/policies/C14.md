## Land area description

Mellon Lake has the typical bare rock ridge and valley topography characteristic of Site District 5E-11. It is linked with two other significant natural ecosystems in the area: the Kaladar Jack Pine Barrens and the Puzzle Lake area. The rock barrens and other habitats support rare animals - such as the Prairie Warbler and Five-lined Skink and several rare plants. Close to 600 native plants grow here, including an unusual population of cactus growing in the dry barrens. Other important features include the wide diversity of upland and aquatic habitats and a provincially significant wetland along Clare River.Mellon Lake was regulated as a conservation reserve on April 21, 2001.

## Land use intent

Currently this site is regulated as a Conservation Reserve under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act. MNR is currently preparing supporting documentation for a land use amendment to recognize significant changes made to the Conservation Reserve boundary which includes lands north and west of the original site, including areas around the Little Skootamatta River provincially significant wetland, adjacent to the Kaladar Jack Pine Barrens Conservation Reserve. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Land Use Strategy (1999).

## Permitted uses — preface

Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing conservation reserve policy and by the approved Mellon Lake conservation reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) prepared on September 11, 2001. Further management direction may be elaborated through a Resource Management Plan. Any new uses and commercial activities associated with conservation reserves, will be considered on a case-by-case basis, and they must pass a test of compatibility to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
