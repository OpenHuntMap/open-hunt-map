## Land area description

A large relatively remote area containing portions of the Onaping River drainage basin. The southern portion of the area is located within the City of Greater Sudbury. Well used for a variety of Crown land activities including hunting, fishing, canoeing, snowmobiling, trapping and baitfishing. Important for resource uses including forestry and mining exploration. Contains significant aggregate deposits.Glaciofluvial outwash deposits, glaciofluvial delta deposits, ice-contact kame moraines and eskers, and glaciolocustine beach deposits were deposited during the Pleistocene Period of continental glaciation.Glaciofluvial outwash deposits, formed by deposition of water carried materials along glaciofluvial spillways and at the terminus of spillways, are concentrated in the Cartier-Levack area (Venturi, Hart, Moncrieff, Hess, Harty and Cascaden Townships). They are generally located in a north-south direction and follow the major river systems such as the Onaping River. They generally contain well stratified sand and gravel materials.Glaciofluvial deltas occur where glaciofluvial outwash was deposited into glacial Lake Algonquin.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The land use intent is to maintain the area's remote character. Manage recreational and resource sector (forestry, mining) uses consistent with the enhanced management area (remote access) policies. This area will be managed to ensure the proper realization of the aggregate resource potential within the context of maintaining the area's remote character. MNR will continue to recognize the Official plan of the Greater City of Sudbury an its associated secondary plans, as amended.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Sudbury District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Remote access enhanced management area are intended to maintain the remote character of selected areas. Typically, these are relatively large areas which provide the public and tourism operators with high-quality remote recreational experiences including hunting, fishing, canoeing, and camping. Given the large size, remoteness, and relative absence of roads, these areas will play a significant role in protecting wilderness values outside the parks and protected areas system.
