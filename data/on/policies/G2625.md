## Land area description

The townships surrounding the City of Thunder Bay are the basis of this area. They are Sibley, Including the community at Silver Islet, McGregor, Dorion, McTavish, Gorham, Ware, Jacques, Conmee, O'Connor, Oliver, Paipoonge, Lybster, Gillies, Scoble, Blake, Crooks, Pardee, Pearson and portions of Fowler, Forbes, Marks, and the Dawson Road Lots. Not all of these townships have organized municipal status. This area is primarily private land with development levels ranging from high to sparse. Land uses are mixed: rural residential, mining, aggregate extraction, agriculture, cottaging, public and commercial recreation such as lodges, campgrounds, parks and access points, forestry, secondary industry and commercial.Utilities such as roads, hydro corridors, and pipelines cross the area. A major issue in this area is the lack of Crown land and water to meet the urban and rural demands for recreation and natural resource products such as aggregate and fuelwood.This area is partially located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The intent of this area is to support from both Crown and private land the resource demands of the large population of Thunder Bay and its surrounding townships.Ministry extension programs for woodlot improvement, stream rehabilitation or parks facilities will be focused in this area to bring private and municipal vacant land into production so it may contribute to the local economy and help meet local resources needs.Through the one window planning process, MNR will also encourage local plans and private development proposals to consider resource concerns. This will ensure that the production of aggregate, fuelwood, wood fibre for the mills, minerals and agricultural produce is not inhibited by short termed plans. The significance of these products is not in their quantity but in their proximity to the demand centre of Thunder Bay.Crown land will play a mixed land use role. Depending on the resource potential, location, adjacent land uses and demands, Crown land will supply public recreation needs, protect sensitive features or produce natural resource products.Public recreation on Crown land will include the maintenance or rehabilitation of rivers and streams to support angling and viewing, the protection of deer yards, waterfowl habitat and moose wintering areas for wildlife viewing and hunting and the provision of both park and Crown land recreation facilities (ranging from campgrounds to ski trails). Aggregate extraction or forestry uses will be permitted on Crown land under operation guidelines which protect other resource and recreational values. Known nest sites, the Lakehead University Bog, the Red River Road Bog, the spectacular Nor'Wester Mountain Range, the Thunder Bay amethyst deposits, historic sites of Lappe and Pictured Lake, Silver Mountain, the historic waterway of the Kaministikwia River, and the trout waters of Loch Lomond, Oliver, Miner, Innes, MacIntosh and Silver Lakes will receive special management consideration and protection.Recognizing the recreational, tourism and aesthetic values of the Lake Superior Shoreline, Crown land along the lake will be protected and activities within 300 metres (1000 feet) will re-quire special Ministry approval under stringent operation guidelines.The Municipal Plans for the rural townships, Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing zoning orders, and the Planning Act will guide private land uses in this area. Ministry of Natural Resources' policies for aggregate production, hazard land protection and other resource policies will be addressed through these documents. This area has presently four (4) existing parks and there are ten (10) areas of Natural and Scientific Interest. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Thunder Bay District Land Use Guideline (1983).Rural Areas General Use Area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Rural Areas General Use Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS)at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .
