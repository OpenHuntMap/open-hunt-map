## Land area description

An area from the southern end of Pijitiwabik Bay to Mungo Park Point some 3 kilometres in width has been identified to facilitate planned development along the eastern shoreline of Lake Nipigon. The area has high recreational use and high mineral potential and is an important shoreline access area to southern Lake Nipigon. The Rocky Bay Indian Reserve and MacDiarmid lie within this area. Most of the Enhanced Management Area is within the Municipality of Greenstone. Recreational facilities exist at High Hill Harbour and Poplar Point, which are managed by the municipality.This area is located within the Lake Nipigon Basin Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.This area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.”

## Land use intent

The overall land use intent for the Lake Nipigon-Beardmore Enhanced Management Area is to encourage tourism and recreation development in a nodal fashion, focused on the existing campground and marina facilties. The other portions of the EMA will be managed for more remote recreation opportunities. Public access to the southeast portion of Lake Nipigon will continue to be promoted through exisitng municipal and private access points in the EMA. A diversity of recreational uses will be promoted ranging from facility orientated recreation to the use of accessible trail networks and enjoyment of quality backcountry recreation. Crown land development opportunities that will be permitted within the EMA The Lake Nipigon Basin Signature Site Ecological Land Use and Resource Management Strategy, which includes the Lake Nipigon- Beardmore Enhanced Management Area Resource Management Guideline, guides the management of this area.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Nipigon District Land Use Guidelines (1983).Lake Nipigon - Beardmore Enhanced Management Area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Lake Nipigon - Beardmore Enhanced Management Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS) at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .

## Permitted uses — preface

Recreation enhanced management areas have been applied to areas with high recreational use of significant recreation values for activities such as angling, hunting, motorized and pedestrian trail use, and canoeing. The enhanced management area can be used for a wide range of recreation values and management intents, ranging form areas where relatively substantial recreation development is permitted, to areas providing low-density, high-quality recreation in a natural setting.Where the recreation enhanced management area has been identified to protect remote recreation values, all activities need to be carried out in such a way as to maintain or enhance the remote recreation qualities.
