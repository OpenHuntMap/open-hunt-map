## Land area description

This is an oval shaped intrusion of Post Huronian, mafic intrusive rocks into an area of predominately sedimentary rocks. It is referred to as the Sudbury Nickel Irruptive portion of the Nipissing Diabase. The metallic and industrial mineral potential of this area is exceptionally high. Here, it should be explained that mineral potential is understood to mean mineral exploration potential and, although production potential is a relative parameter, the exploration activity must occur before production. Reference should also be made to Ministry of Northern Development and Mines, Geological Map 2170-Sudbury District for a detailed description of the boundaries of the Nickel Irruptive.This area has vast reserves of copper, nickel and platinum group metals. Altogether, there are an estimated 15 mineral products and by-products originating from this area. In the Official Plan for the City of Greater Sudbury, this area is referred to as the Mineral Reserve. Throughout the area, numerous stands of timber have been committed under Sustainable Forest Licence (SFL) to supply local mills. Also, several aggregate deposits, identified as being critical for meeting mineral aggregate commitments are situated within this area. Windy and Fairbank Lakes have a moderate to high capability for intensive recreation activities.Cameron Creek is an important brook trout fishery and one of the few deer yards in the District is situated in Drury and Hyman Townships. Problems associated with resource development in this area have stemmed from undesirable resource management practices and competition between recreationists, and forest and mining industry demands for the same resources, particularly in lakeshore areas.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Mineral exploration and development will continue to dominate land uses. Other uses which will not preclude the possibility of future extraction will be permitted. In the vicinity of lake trout lakes, an emphasis will be placed on managing and protecting lake trout populations. Windy and Fairbank lakes will also be managed for their recreational capacity. Renewable and non-renewable resource extraction and related activities will be managed with regard for the recreational values in the area.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sudbury District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of Natural lake trout lakes:­ no additional access will be permitted­ 120 metre Area of Concern will be applied­ forest management activities may be permitted within the Area of Concern subject to concerns for erosion/sedimentation and the protection of lake trout habitat.
