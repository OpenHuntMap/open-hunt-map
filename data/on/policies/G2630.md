## Land area description

This area includes Kashabowie, Burchell and Greenwater Lakes. These waterbodies have traditionally been major cottaging, tourism, lodge, Crown land camping and fishing focuses for the residents of Thunder Bay.Limited resource extraction such as trapping, mining and forestry have gone on in the past, but have given careful consideration to the other land uses and development on these lakes.Although development levels for the area are sparse, there are pockets of moderate to dense development along the shoreline of Burchell and Kashabowie. Highway 11, CN rail and several Ontario Hydro transmission lines cross this area.There are numerous eagle nesting sites and historic and archaeological sites.The water levels on Kashabowie and Greenwater are regulated. The primary issue is the blending of resource extraction and recreation land uses.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

In view of traditional recreational uses and developments, the proximity to the City of Thunder Bay and the degree of access, this area's main role will be to meet recreation and tourism needs.The watershed of Quetico Provincial Park is an Area of Concern. Resource management and land use activities in the watershed will be carried out in consideration of the protection of values within the park. Lake management plans will be prepared to reduce land use impacts on the lakes and guide the use of the lakes for power generation, fishing, boating and recreation. Cottaging uses is also expected to expand. Private land development should meet most cottage demands. Lake trout lakes, eagle nesting sites and historic and archaeological sites will be protected.Resource extraction activities will also be modified to protect the tourism, recreation and aesthetic values of this area, and reduce conflicts with other landowners and land uses.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Thunder Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
