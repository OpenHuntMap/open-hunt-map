## Land area description

This area comprises Rainy Lake, Namakan Lake, Little Turtle Lake and Bad Vermillion Lake as well as the land area surrounding these water-bodies excluding the islands of Rainy Lake. With the exception of Namakan Lake, all three major lakes in the area are accessed by provincial highway, although the north and south basins of Rainy Lake are virtually inaccessible by road. Major existing uses of the resource base include forestry, commercial fishing, angling, tourism, wild rice harvesting, trapping, hunting, cottaging and outdoor recreation. Tourism, cottaging and other recreational uses are heaviest adjacent to Highway 11 and the Town of Fort Frances. Substantial portions of the area possess high or moderate potential for metallic minerals. The area is situated within the Crossroute Forest. Considerable prospecting activity occurs in the vicinity of Bad Vermillion Lake. Issues which will be taken into account in future management include the use of Crown lands and waters by non-residents, the potential conflict between angling and commercial fishing operations, the management of tourist, residential and recreational values, and the need for day-use recreational opportunities close to the Town of Fort Frances.The following are designated as tourism lakes: Rainy, Little Turtle, Sphene and Bad Vermillion lakes.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.Water Management Areas have been removed from this Policy Report area as revisions, to more accurately reflect First Nation reserve lands affected by flooding.

## Land use intent

The role of the area will be to continue to provide a wide variety of resource production, tourism and recreational benefits. Cottage lots have been identified and may be made available for sale on Northwest Bay, Swell Bay and Redgut Bay of Rainy Lake as well as Little Turtle Lake. Consistent with Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy, the sale of existing Crown land cottage lots on Seine Bay of Rainy Lake will be permitted. Provision may be made for development of new tourist camps based on the fisheries of Namakan Lake and the South Arm of Rainy Lake. Areas of Concern will be identified for the protection of tourist and recreational values. Opportunities exist to increase commercial fur and wild rice production. Road access will be managed to serve a full range of objectives, including resource extraction and the protection of tourism values and recreational access. Due consideration will be given to the protection of the mining areas south of Little Turtle Lake. The Province does not have jurisdiction over the 5 Indian Reserves located in this area.Sphene Lake and the surrounding lands have been selected for transfer to the federal government as part of the Rainy River First Nation land claim settlement. The future intent of these areas is Indian Reserve. Management of these areas will be consistent with this future land use intent.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Fort Frances District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
