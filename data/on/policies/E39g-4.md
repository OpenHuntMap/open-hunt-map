## Land area description

This shoreline area is characterized by rugged Canadian Shield topography, renowned for its scenic vistas with windswept pine trees. Bare bedrock is interspersed with pockets of shallow soils, with discontinuous vegetation cover. The area provides habitat for a wide diversity of fish and wildlife species, including several vulnerable and threatened species. There are a number of significant wetlands and wetland complexes. A wide range of recreational activities is carried out in the area, including boating (large cruisers, as well as small boats, canoes and kayaks), hunting, fishing, camping and nature appreciation. Commercial tourism operations support the various recreational activities. There are a number of areas with extensive cottage development on the adjacent private land. This area includes the local Indian Reserves. As the reserves area under the jurisdiction of the Federal Government and administered locally by their respective Bend Councils, they have been excluded from the planning process. This area is situated in and adjacent to the Township of Rutherfored and George Island. In addition to the commercial fishing port development at the town of Killarney, commercial recreation establishments and seasonal recreation development occur throughout this area. Mineral and aggregate extraction occur in the form of a small scale surface pit and quarry operation on Badgeley Point and a silica quarry on Badgeley Island. Recreation, mineral exploration and development, commercial tourism and commercial fishing activities will continue to be permitted. All other uses such as hunting, trapping, sport fishing and seasonal residential uses should neither preclude future resource extraction activities, nor the viability of the commercial tourism industry.This area is located within the Killarney Signature Site and the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site.

## Land use intent

Manage this area consistent with the Enhanced Management Area (Great Lakes Coastal Area) policies of the Land Use Strategy and the overall intent of the Great Lakes Heritage Coast land use planning approach. The majority of lands and lands under water will be managed to protect their outstanding beauty and sensitive ecosystems, and to promote their recreation and tourism potential. The majority of lands will be retained in public ownership and MNR will actively seek cooperation and partnerships in its future planning and management. Crown lands should not be sold for cottaging development and substantial new road access should be discouraged. This retention of Crown land shall not preclude current and future First Nation discussions regarding land base for Reserves. Any new access should be identified as part of more detailed comprehensive local planning and be consistent with maintaining the scenic and ecological values and remote characteristics of the coastal area.Any new development such as tourism-related infrastructure (marinas, mooring areas) should be planned and implemented in a manner consistent with the overall objective of protecting the natural elements of this unique ecosystem.Activities occurring on or within the water portion of this designation must be undertaken in a manner consistent with its recreation and tourism focus, and with the land use intent of the adjacent designation.Commercial fishing on the Great Lakes is regulated by licenses issued under the Fisheries Act. Commercial fishing will continue to be managed according to Great Lakes Fisheries Management Plans, and will take into consideration values as identified in any protected area plan.MNR will encourage wherever possible uses on the lands which area compatible with adjacent areas. At the request of the Federal Government or First Nation, MNR will provide assistance in resource management where it is feasible and appropriate. By agreement with the Federal Government, MNR is responsible for forest fire management on these Indian Reserves.The mining rights to the islands in Georgian Bay were withdrawn under the Mining Act in 1926. In those few areas not withdrawn from staking (e.g. Badgeley Island) onshore mining developments may expand under ground, outward under the Great Lakes, subject to existing international agreements in the remainder of the area.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Espanola District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Great Lakes Coastal Area is a category of enhanced management area that has been applied to specific lands and waters along the shore and islands of Georgian Bay, the North Channel, and Lake Superior. This category fits within a larger "featured area" concept -- the Great Lakes Heritage Coast -- that applies to all Crown lands along the portion of the Great Lakes shoreline within the planning area. The Great Lakes Enhanced Management Area has been applied in conjunction with the other land use designations and enhanced management areas that occur within the Heritage Coast.In the coastal areas, ecosystem protection and the promotion of recreation and tourism are the primary land use and resource management goals.Activities occurring on or within the water portion of this land use category should be undertaken in a manner consistent with the recreation and tourism focus of the category and the land use intent of the adjacent land use category.
