## Land area description

Lake Wanapitei is one of the sources of fresh water for the City of Greater Sudbury. It provides an excellent base for recreational activities, including small craft boating, canoeing, cottaging, warm and cold water sport fishing, camping and bathing. The majority of the shoreline is held under Crown ownership.Access is provided by logging and cottaging roads, the Canadian National Railway line and two Federal Government docks at Boland's Bay and Portage Bay, and numerous water access points, all of which have influenced the pattern of land use around the lake. Land use activities including seasonal residential development, mineral exploration and development and aggregate extraction have increased in this area in the past few years. Commercial fur trapping and baitfishing also occur. Another important use of the Lake is made by Ontario Hydro. A dam at the south end of Outlet Bay controls water levels on the Lake for hydro-electric power generation purposes for the Stinson, Coniston, and McVittie generating stations downstream on the Wanapitei River. Thin soils, flood hazards and wave damage along the shoreline place constraints on seasonal residential development as well as garbage and sewage disposal facilities. Garbage, water pollution and sand bluff erosion problems on the north shore of the Lake have also been identified.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Public and private recreation, timber harvesting and commercial fishing will continue to be the primary uses. Fish and wildlife management, in harmony with timber management, will ensure protection of the fish and wildlife resources upon which recreation opportunities are dependent. Management emphasis will ensure the protection of the lake trout population (and habitat requirements) from development and use pressures. Management of this area will be compatible with the adjacent Wanapitei Provincial Park. Where conflicts occur between recreation and resource extraction activities, the impact on recreation values will be minimized, wherever possible.Mining rights to the islands in Lake Wanapitei were withdrawn from staking under the Mining Act in 1926. Since 1976, the mining rights to the lands under the waters of Lake Wanapitei have been withdrawn with the exception of a few older mining patents and claims. This practice of withdrawing the lake bottom from staking will continue in the interests of protecting the water supply for the City of Greater Sudbury. MNR is aware of proposed drilling and other exploration programs considered by the mining industry under the waters of Lake Wanapitei. Since the City of Greater Sudbury draws its water, in part, from the Lake and for other environmentally-related concerns, MNR will exert a close scrutiny on development in the area. Therefore, all withdrawn lands will be retained under Crown ownership until critical standards for the development of the area are established.Management of this area is also governed by the general polices contained in Sudbury District Guidelines (1983).
