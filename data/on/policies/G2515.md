## Land area description

This area includes the majority of the Sioux Lookout District within the Ontario Living Legacy planning area outside provincial parks, wilderness areas, conservation reserves and enhanced management areas. Present uses include mining, forestry, cottaging, tourism, Crown land recreation, fishing, hunting, and fur harvesting. The communities of Sioux Lookout, Hudson, Alcona, and Savant Lake are the major population centres. First Nation communities found within the area include Frenchman's Head, Whitefish Bay, Kejick Bay, New Slate Falls and Saugeen. Mishkeegogamang First Nation abuts the are on the norther edge. Mining, forestry and resource-based tourism are the major industries in the area.This area includes two forest management units: Lac Seul Forest and Caribou Forest. Sioux Lookout District administers forest management on both Forests.The area contains a significant resource based tourism industry, ranging from road accessible main base lodges to remote outpost camps. The following lakes have been designated as tourism lakes: Abram, Aerial, Antennae, Armit, Austin, Bamaji, Barrington, Big Sandy, Big Vermilion, Birmingham, Blackstone, Butterfly, Carling, Clace, Coles, Cover, Dominion, Doran, Fawcett, Flint, Gull, Heathcote, Highstone, Jabez, Kashaweogama, Kezik, Kirk, Lake St. Joseph, Little Savant, Little Vermilion, Lynxpaw, Marchington, Maskara, McCrea, Medcalf, Minnitaki, Otatakan, Pashkokogan, Pelican, Pusher, Ragged Wood, Roadhouse, Rockcliff, Root, Savant, Snelgrove, Sunlight, Tew, Tully, Wapesi, Wesleyan, Upper Wapesi, Wilkie, Zarn and Zionz.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

This is a multiple use area that is largely governed by existing provincial policy. Sioux Lookout District did not have an approved District Land Use Guidelines in 1983. In the absence of a District Land Use Guidelines, land use direction has been provided over time through the interpretation of provincial policy. Although most land use activities are permitted within this area, all new activities will be subject to a review and evaluation. The review will ensure that new activities are consistent with approved forest, fisheries and other resource management plans. The results of such a review may result in the activity being approved, modified or denied, depending on the extent of resource or user impacts.
