## Land area description

The area contains a number of sizeable lakes, many of which have been newly accessed by a provincial secondary highway to the Detour Lake mine site. The area has medium with some high recreation potential and supports considerable angling pressure.Present major uses include a mixture of commercial tourism, remote cottaging, public recreation, timber harvesting, aggregate extraction and mineral exploration. The area contains one patented parcel of land, that being 2.71 hectares on Little Abitibi Lake which is used for a commercial tourist lodge. There are several remote cottages and some outpost camps.The area also includes significant fish and wildlife habitat such as moose winter concentration areas, osprey and heron nesting sites, pickerel spawning beds as well as timber plantations and Indian burial sites. The significance of each of these resources and features varies.The Middle Detour Lake Road Corridor Area is located in the Iroquois Falls Forest, managed under a sustainable forest licence. Harvesting and a number of access roads are planned. With this access and the new highway, development pressures will be put on aggregate resources in the area and the existing commercial tourist outfitters. New accessible areas for cottaging and Crown land recreation will become available.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.This area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.”

## Land use intent

The primary use in shorelines of this area will be recreation with the emphasis on cottaging. In non-shoreline areas resource extraction will be encouraged. Structural development on and near significant historical sites or fish and wildlife habitat areas will be discouraged. Resource management plans will also contain guidelines for the protection of these sites. A small portion of this area overlaps with the Burntbush, Two Peaks and Straggler Lakes Areas of the Approved Cochrane District Remote (Wilderness) Tourism Strategy. Management of the overlapped area will be subject to the Approved Cochrane District Remote (Wilderness) Tourism Strategy (1997). Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Cochrane District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
