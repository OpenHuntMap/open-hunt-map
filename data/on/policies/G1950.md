## Land area description

This area occupies a lowland area to the northwest and south-east of Lake Nipissing. In the northern section an expanse of deep lake sediments consisting chiefly of varved clays (below the escarpment) interspersed with rock outcrops has provided a good agricultural area. Much of the area is cleared and there is evidence of widespread erosion along the many rivers and streams draining into Lake Nipissing. For the Veuve River Area, private land holdings are extensive. The entire area is organized. In the southern portion, there are several watershed units draining northwards from the Algonquin Highlands to Lake Nipissing. Terrain is weakly broken with good agriculture soils through much of the area and increasing steepness of slopes and shallower soils in the south-west part. Powassan, Trout Creek and portions of five townships with planning controls are included. Agricultural uses predominate. There is very little Crown land in the area. Recreation, tourism and cottage uses occur on several lakes, of which Wistiwasing Lake (Wasi Lake) the largest.There are winter trails and downhill ski facilities in the area.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Agriculture, urban and rural residential uses will continue to dominate this area. Timber production is an important secondary use in the northern section. Recreation, tourism and cottaging are other existing recognized uses.Ministry programs will continue to encourage resource production, primarily forest products and fur, from private lands and the limited amount of Crown land. Wildlife management will continue on Crown land. Fisheries management in Lake Nipissing will require a close monitoring of agricultural drainage projects and erosion problems to protect recreation and tourism values. Aggregate resources are limited and these should be kept accessible for extractive uses. Several sites, where protection is the main theme of management, (e.g. heronries, archaeological sites, wetland areas, etc.) are found in this area. These and other such sites, which may be found in the future, will be protected during resource management operations and will be identified as values requiring protector in municipal planning. Angling opportunities for cold water species are limited. As a result, existing cold water fisheries may be accorded higher protection. Important waterfowl concentration areas will be given priority. MNR is responsible for the management of Crown land and natural resources in organized townships. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the North Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
