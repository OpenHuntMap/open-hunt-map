## Land area description

A large relatively remote area containing portions of the Onaping River drainage basin. Well used for a variety of Crown land activities including hunting, fishing, canoeing, snowmobiling, trapping and baitfishing. Important for resource uses including forestry and mining exploration. Contains significant aggregate deposits. The area is also located next to the Sudbury Nickel Irruptive known as one of hate world's richest mineral deposits.The fish and wildlife resources and the rugged terrain have attracted fishermen and hunters to this area for many years.Fisheries resources and the moose populations in some areas have been subjected to over-harvesting, attributed in part, to the relative ease of access provided by roads constructed for logging and mining purposes. This accessibility is regarded as being in conflict with the desire of recreationists and tourist operators to maintain a natural environment recreation experience.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The land use intent is to maintain the area's remote character. Manage recreational and resource sector (forestry, mining) uses consistent with the enhanced management area (remote access) policies. Lake trout lakes will be managed to ensure the protection and rehabilitation of lake trout populations and habitat.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Sudbury District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Remote access enhanced management area are intended to maintain the remote character of selected areas. Typically, these are relatively large areas which provide the public and tourism operators with high-quality remote recreational experiences including hunting, fishing, canoeing, and camping. Given the large size, remoteness, and relative absence of roads, these areas will play a significant role in protecting wilderness values outside the parks and protected areas system.In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of Natural lake trout lakes:­ no additional access will be permitted­ 120 metre Area of Concern will be applied­ forest management activities may be permitted within the Area of Concern subject to concerns for erosion/sedimentation and the protection of lake trout habitat.
