## Land area description

This area includes portions of several watershed units draining northeasterly to the Pickerel and French Rivers. Terrain is moderately broken with shallow soils and bedrock outcrops. There are some areas of deeper soils in glacial valleys and channels now occupied by smaller water courses. A large portion of the area is private land originally settled as farm land, but agricultural uses have diminished. This area encompasses those parts of the Loring deer yard which are within the North Bay District. The portion of Gurd Township included is part of an organized municipality (Nipissing) and three small urban communities are present at Restoule, Arnstein and Port Loring. Areas of high mineral potential occur in Pringle and East Mills Townships.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The focus of Ministry programs in this area is management of the Loring deer yard. Generally, any uses which would destroy important winter habitat will be discouraged.Portions of the area were designated in the NGBRR Plan for mixed developments or uses. The mix of existing urban, rural residential, agriculture, recreation, tourism and cottaging uses is recognized in this document. Expansions of some of these uses onto Crown land which may have a negative impact on the deer yard will be considered on a site specific basis. Resource production (timber, aggregate, etc.) is an important use and should be managed in a manner that complements wildlife management. Land uses in the area included in the Memsagamesing Lake Management Plan will be conducted within the spirit and intention of this document and the detailed policies of the Lake Management Plan.Several sites, where preservation is the main theme of management, (e.g. heronries, cold water fisheries habitats, etc.) are found in this area. Their exact location may be seen in the North Bay District Sensitive Areas Report. These and other such sites, which may be found in the future, will be protected during resource management operations and given special consideration in the Ministry's review of the plans ofother agencies.Disposition of Crown land will only be considered on a case-by-case basis with careful consideration of potential impacts on Ministry programs and under the general disposition policy contained in this document.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in appropriate District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
