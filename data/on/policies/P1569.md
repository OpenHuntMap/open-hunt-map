## Land area description

This highly diverse site is representative of approximately 22 different landform vegetation combinations. The dominant landform types present are: weakly broken ground moraine and lacustrine deposits. Dominant vegetation types include mixed conifer and mixed deciduous forests.The Groundhog River has self sustaining sturgeon fisheries, and brook trout occur in feeder streams.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

Groundhog River Provincial Park was regulated as a provincial park on June 20, 2006.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999).

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning andManagement Policies (1992), the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Groundhog RiverProvincial Park Interim Management Statement (2007). The Interim Management Statement provides directionto ensure custodial management of park resources until a management plan is prepared which includes fullpublic and Aboriginal review of proposals. At that time, significant decisions regarding resource stewardship,operations, development and permitted uses are made. Where a use may be permitted (Maybe or Yes on the table), the long-term management direction will be determined through planning.
