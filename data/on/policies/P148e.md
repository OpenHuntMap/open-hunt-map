## Land area description

Mattawa River Provincial Park is located between the City of North Bay and the Town of Mattawa, stretching approximately 37 kilometres, in Ecodistrict 5E-5. The area contains provincially significant wetlands, marsh communities, unusual silver maple swamp forests growing on deep silty clay and sand deposits, smaller wetlands in the upland section, as well as old growth red and white pine growing on hilly, sandy uplands and pockets of flat deposits. Glaciologists have determined that the Mattawa River served as a drainage channel for the inland, meltwater lakes of 10,000 years ago, and was a much larger river than it is today. Evidence of erosion by the ancient river may be seen along cliff faces and canyon walls, many metres above present flood levels. Its route features several stretches of rapids, an eight-metre waterfall and 14 portages. The Mattawa River was once an important route for voyageurs, trappers and loggers-- and before them for the Aboriginal people who began inhabiting the region more than 6,000 years ago. As such, the river is designated as a Canadian Heritage River in 1988. This area also contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report. Mattawa River Provincial Park is a waterway class park and was originally regulated in 1970. An addition to the park was made in 2006 as a result of Ontario’s Living Legacy.

## Land use intent

Mattawa River Provincial Park will be managed according to Provincial Park policy and Park Management Plan (1988). Mattawa River Provincial Park will protect significant natural, cultural and recreational values. The River was an important Aboriginal, exploration and fur trade route and is a popular canoe route today.The Park will protect natural, cultural and recreational values that contribute to the Canadian Heritage River System.
