## Land area description

This area includes the major rivers and their tributaries north of Highway 11 including 120 metres of shoreline on each side.The Abitibi River north of Gardiner and a portion of the Onakawana River is part of a provincial canoe route to Moosonee. These rivers have in total, approximately 250 kilometres of waterway. Road and rail access is available at various locations. The area has two official access points and a number of unofficial access points. The rivers, excepting the Onakawana, support the following development: 2 hydraulic generating stations, water power leases, gravel pits, rail crossings, cottages and Crown land campsites for canoe routes. Areas of medium mineral potential are located in Pinard and in Pitt Townships and the townships to the north. The remainder of the area is classed as low or unknown mineral potential. Much of the Abitibi River has been harnessed for its hydro-electric generating potential, e.g. Abitibi Canyon. The majority of the land in this area is Crown. The only major concentrations of private land are found in the southern part of this area, around the generating stations and at Fraserdale. The forest resources of the area are managed in four forest management units. One major significant archaeological site has been identified in this area. This site is located at Flood's Landing on the Frederickhouse River.This area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.”

## Land use intent

The primary use of this area will be recreation. A mix of public recreation, cottaging and commercial tourism will be encouraged. Hydraulic power production is also a priority in this area. Municipal planning control, exists in Leitch Township. Activities in this township will be in accordance with this official plan. Structural development will not be permitted in areas subject to shoreline erosion or flooding. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Cochrane District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
