## Land area description

This area covers the Kenorain Creek and Manion Creek watersheds. The Glenorchy Road provides access to the western edge of the area; the Bending Lake Highway to the eastern edge. The area is sparsely developed and is comprised almost entirely of Crown lands. The major existing use of the area is the operation of thirteen tourist establishments located there. Other current uses include angling, hunting, trapping, Crown land recreation and timber harvesting which has just begun in the western reaches of the area. There are scattered locales of high or moderate aggregate potential. Metallic mineral potential is low. The area lies essentially within the Crossroute Forest.Issues which will be taken into account in future management include the impact that forest extraction and increased access will have on remote tourism operations, and the effect that increased access will have on trout fisheries.The following are designated as tourism lakes: Bernadine, Pettit, Manion, Mount, Eric, Sandbeach, Kenorain, Silvertip and Warren Creek.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The role will be to provide a mix of resource extraction, tourism and recreational benefits. It is anticipated that Crown land recreational activities will continue to be high on the Manion/Sandbeach chain. Hunting pressure will likely increase as the western reaches of the area become more accessible. The potential for a commercial fishery on Manion Lake is addressed in the District Fisheries Management Plan. Also, potential exists for an increase in commercial fur production. Road access will be managed for resource extraction, resource management and for the protection of tourism interests. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Fort Frances District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
