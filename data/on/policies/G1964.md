## Land area description

This area is located within the Lake Timiskaming, Wabi River, Montreal River, and Blanche River watershed. The values and uses of this area include agricultural lands, urban and rural communities, and multiple municipal government jurisdictions. This area is also home to the Cobalt Heritage Silver Trail, the Temiskaming Nordic Ski Trails, the Tritowns Ski Village Trails as well as numerous tourism and recreation facilities. This area includes a forest management genetic test areas and an extensive mining landscape.Features of this area include the Devil's Rock lookout and the Dawson Point Limestone Area of Natural and Scientific Interest (ANSI). The Dawson Point ANSI encompasses a large limestone cliff at the north end of Lake Timiskaming. The cliff is composed of fossiliferous Silurian limestone and is the largest exposure of this type of bedrock in the area. This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Inland Ontario Lakes Designated for Lake Trout Management (2006) accessible in the Source of Direction portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The land use intent and resource management direction for this management area pertains to Crown land and other resources under Ontario government jurisdiction which include the protection of fish and wildlife habitats, cultural resources, and natural heritage values such as ANSIs while fostering private land forestry and mining related activities. Municipalities will have jurisdiction over certain resources within their boundaries on private lands.The concerns of this area include the protection of Crown land and resource values and water quality concerns with respect to agricultural and historical mining areas as well the Blanche River wetlands. There is significant rural residential development throughout the management area (e.g., Twin Lakes area), mining hazard on the landscape as well as un-rehabilitated mining properties.The objectives of this area include considering municipal interests in Crown land use planning and provincial interests in municipal planning. The management of Crown and municipal lands are to complement each other.The strategies of this area include developing partnerships between Ministry of Natural Resources and area municipalities in regards to resource issues, promoting various resource policies, and encouraging resource stewardship on private lands.
