## Land area description

Algonquin, the first provincial park in Ontario (1893), protects a variety of natural, cultural, and recreational features and values. Algonquin is biologically diverse with more the 1,000 vascular plant species and more then 200 vertebrates that breed within its boundaries. The Park contains numerous historical and archaeological resources and has inspired more than 40 books, 1,800 scientific papers, a dozen films, a symphony, and the art of Tom Thompson and the Group of Seven. The original park area, consisting of 18 townships, was designated in 1893 as Algonquin National Park under the Algonquin National Park Act. The Park's name was subsequently changed in 1913 to Algonquin Provincial Park. The Park is situated in the Georgian Bay Site Region (5E). This region is an area of rolling uplands and bedrock outcrops with areas of sand and gravel of glacial origin.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Forest management occurs in Algonquin Park under authority of the Algonquin Forestry Authority (AFA). Forest management operations are restricted in Algonquin Park to places, times and areas approved by the MNR within the Recreation/Utilization Zone. They must also subscribe to specify restrictions that protect Park values.
