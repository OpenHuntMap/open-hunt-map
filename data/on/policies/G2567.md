## Land area description

This area consists of 5,260 hectares of land formerly owned and operated by a mining company, Steep Rock Resources Inc. The land was actively open pit mined for approximately 45 years, resulting in numerous large deep pits. Returned to the Crown in 1988, the area is located 4 kilometres north of the Atikokan townsite and is completely within the boundaries of the Township of Atikokan. According to the Township of Atikokan Official Plan, the surrender site is designated as a Resource Development Area, allowing for the location of major industrial development.The area has considerable potential for recreational, industrial, tourism and resource extraction activities. Existing rail and road access, electrical power lines, natural gas pipelines and a considerable area of level land make the area economically attractive.Significant issues include the utilization of the returned lands for the maximum economic benefit of Atikokan, the maintenance of the water control structure (dams, tunnels, channels, etc.) constructed during the development and operation of the mine, public safety and site security on the mine site (in particular the open pit areas), and the retention of 2 PCB storage areas by Steep Rock Resources Inc.

## Land use intent

Detailed direction to Crown land development and uses in the Steep Rock Area will be found in the Steep Rock Property Management Plan. In general, the primary emphasis will be on resource extraction (forestry and mining, including aggregate), industrial development and tourism/recreational activities. Roads will be managed to facilitate the overall land use intent.All Crown land development and uses will have regard for the Official Plan and associated zoning by-laws of the Township. The present municipal zoning for the surrender site is classed M2, industrial, heavy. Zoning amendments, if required, will be the responsibility of the Township. MNR will continue a close working liaison with the Township regarding all activities in the area.The 397 metre contour line (1290 feet A.S.L.) has been identified as the boundary for the flooding area, or the “Anticipated Area of Inundation” (AAI). As well, the steep, unstable walls of the pits are prone to collapse in some areas. These areas are identified and are classed as “Hazards Lands”. Both the AAI and Hazard Lands will be identified and incorporated into the Atikokan Township Official Plan. Supplementary by-laws will control development and land use within these areas. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Atikokan District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
