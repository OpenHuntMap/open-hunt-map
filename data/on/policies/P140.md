## Land area description

Located within portions of Eddy, French, Socko, Clarkson, Lockhart, Garrow, McAuslan, LaSalle, Osborne and Steward townships, this waterway park in Site District 5E-6 has 4 components:1) the rivers (Jocko and Little Jocko) have a combined length of 110km. This includes 70 kilometers west to east from Jocko Lake to the Jocko River mouth where it enters the Ottawa River and the tributary drainage of the Little Jocko which parallels the longer river for 40 kilometers west to east from Mitchell Lake headwaters to the confluence with the Jocko River.2) Osborne Township Patterned Peatland and Dune Complex, a unique combination of a large and diverse wetland area and a large area of sand dunes covered in black spruce and jack pine. This forest is part of an extensive wetland of fens, bogs and swamps, that grows in distinct patterns within the peatland and supports at least 11 vegetation communities. The black spruce, jack pine and lichen habitats in the dune areas are unusual in the Site District.3) Banana Lake White Birch, a significant, relatively undisturbed combination of vegetation and landforms, including low hills and rough terrain of bedrock with shallow sandy deposits. There are 20 vegetation associations found here, including white birch, red and white pine stands, bogs, fens, marshes and floodplain swamps.4) A waterway connection to the Blue Lake End Moraine Conservation Reserve along Pine Mountain Creek.

## Land use intent

The park boundary is initially set at 200 meters from the water's edge. Significant wetlands contiguous with the Rivers and located beyond the 200 meter boundary are included within the park.The Jocko Rivers will be managed as a waterway park providing outstanding recreational landscapes with representative natural features offering a high quality recreational and educational experiences. Outside of the natural heritage areas and wetlands continuous with the Jocko and Little Jocko Rivers, most of the park boundary is defined as 200 meters from the waters edge. Land use and resource use decisions will consider the sensitivity of features. The sensitivity of the Banana Lake White Birch site is related to the susceptibility to fire and the protection of any mature stands in the depressions and steep slopes in the barrens, which could easily be affected if the thin soil supporting the communities were disturbed. Banana Lake also has good turtle habitat and includes headwater areas for the Jocko River.The large area of wind modified eolian sand dunes pattern around the peatlands represents a unique landform vegetation type at the southern limit of distribution. This area provides a linkage between the Nipissing Game Preserve located in the Martin River Enhanced Management Area, and the McConnell Lakes Recreational Area. Enhanced Management Area land use and resource management decisions should consider the high compressible nature of peat sands and the extensive wildlife habitat available for game animals, furbearers, waterfowl and fish. Minimization of disturbance along the many possible ways to access the site is recommended.Any proposed land use or resource management decision will consider its effects on the four enhanced management areas abutting the Jocko River Waterway Park (McConnell Lakes Recreation Area, Martin River, Little Jocko Ice Contact Delta, Garrow Township Ice Contact Delta).The intent is to manage this area as a Provincial Park.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999).

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992), the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Interim Management Statement, where available. The Interim Management Statement provides direction to ensure custodial management of park resources until such time as a park management planning process is conducted, which includes public review of proposals. At that time, significant decisions regarding resource stewardship, operations, development and permitted uses are made.
