## Land area description

This area includes the waters of Batchawana Bay inside a line drawn between Corbeil Point and Rudderhead Point and the western boundary of G1912, Batchawana Bay – Sand Bay. It also includes the waters of Goulais Bay inside a line drawn from Maple Point to Goulais Point. The land uses presently occurring in this area are public recreation (mostly angling, motor boating, and sailing) and commercial fishing. Commercial fishing in this area is presently restricted within 1,000 metres of the mouth of rivers or streams and from Goulais Bay (May 1 - Sept. 30) through legislation and conditions of licences.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The general land use intent for this area is public recreation, with emphasis on the provisions for sport fishing opportunities. MNR will protect the spawning streams located along the shoreline of this area. MNR will protect and encourage the protection of the waterfowl habitat around the Goulais River Delta. All activities in the area will take into consideration known spawning areas and nesting sites. The specific locations can be found in a compendium at the District office. The District will dispose of Crown water lots for public or private use subject to official plan and associated zoning by-laws. Major disposition is also subject to the Ministry of the Environment's review which may result in the requirement of an environmental assessment. Disposition which would result in direct or indirect impacts on any Ministry programs will be discouraged. The shoreline areas, the small protected bays and natural harbours offer attractive recreational boating opportunities (motor boats, yachts, sailboats, canoes). Some of these areas are well known and have been publicized in various publications and consequently are relatively extensively used by cottage owners and tourists. Therefore, in order to maintain the availability of such opportunities, MNR will retain for public use, at least the present area, on Crown islands and Crown shorelines of the Great Lakes. However, no future development for recreation is planned at the present.Islands and lands under the water of this area have been withdrawn from staking. Development proposals and dispositions, including water lots, on the Great Lakes shoreline will be reviewed for conformity with the District’s Shoreline Management Plan.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
