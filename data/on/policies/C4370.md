## Land area description

The regulated Bickford Oak Woods Conservation Reserve is a 31 hectare property located approximately 25 kilometres south of Sarnia, just east of Highway 40 and north of Bickford Line, in the Township of St. Clair, (geographic township of Moore) in the County of Lambton. It contains predominantly forested landscape with scattered wetland pockets that provide habitat for a diversity of Carolinian species and communities.The conservation reserve is located in Ecodistrict 7E2 and in, the Carolinian Life Zone. This zone provides habitat for wildlife species more typically found south of the border and represents the extreme Southwest region of Ontario where the eastern deciduous forest of North America has its most northern limits. The property was selected for protection as it represents the largest Carolinian clay plain forest in Canada and is located on the St. Clair Clay Plain, which is not currently represented within Ontario’s parks and protected areas system. This interior forest habitat is not well represented in Southern Ontario and is vital to the conservation of biological diversity and natural heritage.

## Land use intent

The property will be managed with the long-term goal of protecting natural heritage values and maintaining biodiversity while providing compatible recreational activities. The conservation reserve offers excellent recreational, stewardship and educational opportunities including cross-country skiing, hunting, hiking, and wildlife viewing opportunities. Bickford Oak Woods was regulated as a conservation reserve on June 09, 2008. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Chatham District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Uses and management activities are governed by existing conservation reserve policy and the Bickford Oak Woods Conservation Reserve Management Plan. Any new uses, and commercial activities associated with conservation reserves, will be considered on a case by case basis, and they must pass a test of compatibility to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.

## Addendum

Management of this conservation reserve is carried out within the context of Conservation Reserve policy.
