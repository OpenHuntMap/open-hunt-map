## Land area description

This area includes Lake Abitibi, which covers approximately 91,000 hectares, one-eighth of which is located in the Province of Quebec. It also includes the land area approximately 120 metres measured perpendicularly from the treeline approximately 400 kilometres of shoreline. Sand beaches, extensive wetland areas and an abundance of archaeological and historic sites are also attributes of the lake. All of the Ontario shoreline is Crown except for one private farm lot on the north shore at the Quebec boundary, five private lots at Lowbush and some small parcels of land owned by the Canadian National Railway. There are a number of trapline cabins throughout the area. Access is gained to the Lake Abitibi area by the Canadian National Railway at Lowbush, Mace and Eades and by logging roads on the north shore to Eades (official access point). Indirect access to the lake is also generally gained via the Abitibi River from Twin Falls and via the Ghost River from Highway 101. The Quebec portion of the lake is readily accessible.The water level of the lake is controlled by the dam at Twin Falls. Hazards and limits to recreation on this lake include winds, numerous rocky shoals and the high turbidity of the water. The area north of the lake is currently under a sustainable forest licence. Approximately three-quarters of the area has high or medium mineral potential.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

The priority for the Lake Abitibi area will be a mixture of public recreation, cottaging and commercial tourism. The amount of development on the lake will not exceed the capability of the resources to support this development. Development will also be planned in such a way as to have minimum impact on significant archaeological and historical sites, wetland areas and fish and wildlife habitat.A lake management plan was prepared.The Lake Abitibi area has important wildlife areas, particularly waterfowl production and staging areas (Ghost River Marsh, Lightning Point) and moose winter concentration areas (Rand, Lamplugh and Frecheville Townships). The area is known to contain some archaeological sites, historical sites and cemeteries and has potential for archaeological discovery. Some of the special features include cormorant nesting sites, several major wetland areas, heronries, tern colonies and cultural sites (the Jessup Site, the Jordan Site, Red Pine Narrows and Ghost River Post sites have Regional and even Provincial significance). Some of these special management areas may be made available for modified resource management (e.g. seasonal restrictions) while other areas warrant outright protection.Management plans for the Lake Abitibi area will consider the inter-provincial boundary location of the waterbody. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Cochrane District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
