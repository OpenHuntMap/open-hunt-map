## Land area description

Access to the area is provided by Highway 11, a number of secondary highways and a network of concession roads. There are eleven municipalities either wholly or partially within this area. The majority of the area is patented land. Agriculture is the major land use. Urban areas include Earlton and Englehart as well as a number of smaller communities. Six commercial tourism establishments, one tent and trailer campground, two municipal parks and twenty-three cottages are located in this area. The commercial establishments and cottage lots are concentrated in the Englehart River (Long Lake) area. The Englehart and Blanche Rivers are a designated canoe route. Timber harvesting is mainly from private land sources although some small areas of Crown land are scheduled for harvest. There are a number of small private sawmills and one waferboard plant located in the area.Use of agricultural lands for non-agricultural purposes and potential for flooding and erosion along waterways (e.g. Blanche River, Englehart River and Evanturel Creek) are land use problems within this area.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The intent of this area is to provide suitable lands for current and future agricultural production. The primary use in this area will be agriculture and its related activities. Forestry, mineral aggregate extraction and mining are acceptable uses. Day-use recreation activities are encouraged.Industrial development will be encouraged to locate within or adjacent to existing urban areas and in accordance with existing municipal development controls. In accordance with the Provincial Food Land Criteria, MNR will encourage the use of Class 2, 3 and 4 soil areas for agricultural purposes. The development of structures on hazard lands will be discouraged. The Ministry of Agriculture and Food and the Ministry of Environment will be consulted regarding possible adverse effects on agricultural and domestic water requirements that may result from clearing and drainage of peatland areas (e.g. the Hilliardton Swamp). Urban development allowed. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Kirkland Lake District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
