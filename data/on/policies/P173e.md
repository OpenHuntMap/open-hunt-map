## Land area description

Sturgeon River Provincial Park is a waterway class park that is approximately 70-kilometres in length and encompasses a 200-metre wide setback from the high water shoreline on either bank of the Sturgeon River. Forest access roads and several river crossings receive moderate use by snowmobiles, ATVs, and four-wheel drive vehicles used to access areas north and east of the park. Running south-easterly in direction, this waterway park includes representative bedrock formations such as cliffs south of Paul Lake and a number of waterfalls. The Sturgeon River connects with canoe routes in Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater, Solace and Obabika River provincial parks, as well as routes on adjacent Crown lands.Sturgeon River Provincial Park was regulated as a provincial park in 1989. Several additional parcels further south along the Sturgeon River were regulated as park additions under the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (OLL) in 2000. However, as different policies apply to the park additions, a separate policy report describes the area, land use intent and management direction.

## Land use intent

Sturgeon River Provincial protects a provincially significant recreational river and links to a wilderness park and other waterway class parks and adjoining Crown land canoe routes. The park will be managed for non-motorized wilderness recreation such as canoeing and camping. In order to provide a continuous navigable route between Maskinonge Lake and the Sturgeon River, a further park boundary review will examine the opportunity of extending the Chiniguchi Waterway Provincial Park boundary through the Gawasi Lake to Gamagowong to Ozhway Lake chain to Sturgeon River Provincial Park. Resource sector impacts of this particular park boundary expansion could possibly be offset by compensating minor reductions in the west and northern boundary areas of Sturgeon River Provincial Park (McNish Township).

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992) and the Temagami Area Park Management Plan (2007). Note that some different policies apply to the OLL additions to the park. These are included in the Sturgeon River Addition Interim Management Statement (2006) and summarized on an area-specific policy report. The permitted uses associated with provincial parks are found in approved management direction, which are available on the Ontario Parks website.
