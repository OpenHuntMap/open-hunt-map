## Land area description

This area is accessed by Highway 11and Highway 101. The area is wholly within the Corporation of the Township of Black River-Matheson. The population of this area is approximately 3,000 and is concentrated in Matheson, Ramore, Holtyre and Val Gagne. The majority of the area is patented land. Agriculture is the major land use, although other industries employ more residents. Mineral potential is high to medium. Timber harvesting is mainly from private land although some small areas of Crown land are scheduled for harvesting. Small pockets of good quality aggregate are found along Highway 11 corridor and there are over 10 gravel pits in the area. Two municipal parks, unofficial access points and part of the Watabeag River canoe route are located in the area. The Black River and its tributaries are part of the water storage area controlled by a dam at Iroquois Falls. Use of agricultural land for non-agricultural purposes and potential for flooding and erosion along waterways (e.g. Watabeag River) are problems within this area.

## Land use intent

The intent for this area is to provide suitable lands for current and future agricultural production demands. The primary use will be agriculture and its related activities. Mining, mineral aggregate extraction and forestry are secondary uses. Commercial tourism and general recreation are also secondary uses. The Black River will be managed primarily to provide recreation and tourism opportunities. Industrial development will be encouraged to locate within or adjacent to existing urban areas and in accordance with existing municipal development controls. Water storage for hydroelectric power generation is a significant use of shoreline areas. In accordance with the Provincial Food Land Criteria, the MNR will encourage the use of Class 2, 3 and 4 soil areas for agricultural purposes. The development of structures on hazard lands will be discouraged. Urban development allowed. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Kirkland Lake District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
