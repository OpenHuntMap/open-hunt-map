## Land area description

The Pringle Lake Dedicated Protected Area is located in the Whitefeather Forest, extending northward from the shore of Nungesser Lake. The geographic and landform characteristics of this area are unique. A provincially significant earth science feature is located within the area. It is a series of parallel glacial ridges that stretch out into Pringle and Nungesser Lakes, known as a De Greer Moraine and, to thePikangikum people, as part of the "Backbone of the Land". The watershed divide between the Berens and English River watersheds runs through the Pringle Lake Dedicated Protected Area. From Pringle Lake, waters flow northwards into the Berens watershed; from Nungesser Lake, waters flow southwards into the English River watershed. First Nation people conduct various traditional activities on this land for livelihood and cultural purposes.No roads exist in the Pringle Lake Dedicated Protected Area. Development currently includes a remote tourism outpost camp, several private recreation camps, and a private recreation boat cache. Bear management areas, baitfish blocks, registered traplines, and a wildlife management unit are also associated with the area. The Pringle Lake Dedicated Protected Area could provide opportunities for new eco-cultural tourism, and support scientific research and nature appreciation. It has been documentedthat woodland caribou travel through the Pringle Lake Dedicated Protected Area to use calving islands in Nungesser Lake.

## Land use intent

The intent is to regulate Pringle Lake as a protected area, as a provincial park or conservation reserve, although the specific designation and category have not been determined. Pringle Lake can provide anoutstanding recreational water route and landscape, with representative natural features, historical resources, and high quality recreational and educational experiences. Maintenance of existing tourism,and new eco-cultural tourism, and natural and cultural heritage appreciation are important land use intents. Providing support for the conservation of woodland caribou is also a key factor.Management will also be governed by the Land Use Strategy for the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas, MNR policies, and/or new policies developed through cross-cultural dialogue.

## Permitted uses — preface

The dedicated protected area designation (i.e. Park Class or Conservation Reserve) for Pringle Lake is yet to be determined. Interim direction for this area is provided in the Land Use Strategy for the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas (WFAA Strategy) and in the table below. Additional specific direction will be determined by the final designation assigned to this dedicated protected area through cross-cultural dialogue, and by future resource management planning.In the absence of resource management planning, new proposals for specific activities will be subject to a review by MNR in consultation with Pikangikum First Nation. This review will consider the intent of the area, direction provided in the WFAA Strategy, impacts on resources and values associated with the Pringle Lake Dedicated Protected Area, compatible economic opportunities, and potential for economicbenefit to Pikangikum First Nation.Values associated with the Pringle Lake Dedicated Protected Area include the natural and cultural heritage features of the area, special features, and existing land uses. Maintenance of natural disturbance regimes and remoteness will be given high importance as will the conservation of fish and wildlife species, including woodland caribou, and wolverine.
