## Land area description

This area is located generally to the northwest of Lake Abitibi. It also extends west of the Abitibi, Frederickhouse, Driftwood and Onakawana River Areas. This large area contains many and varied structural developments. There are some outpostcamps, numerous cottages, bridges, powerlines, communications towers, logging camps, etc. The road system varies from third class forest access roads to a major provincial highway. The entire area is generally well accessed. This area has relatively high resource values, mainly in mineral potential and forestry. Two of high mineral potential are located just north of Lake Abitibi. The majority of the wood fiber in this area is committed to support mills at Iroquois Falls, Smooth Rock Falls, Cochrane and Thorold. A large tract of private land, approximately nine townships, is located in the southwestern part of this area. Additionally private land is located along Highway 11 adjacent to the three municipalities. Due to the large number of forest access roads in the area, recreationists have access to fish and game populations. In localized areas these resources are receiving more pressure than they are capable of supporting.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

The land use priorities in this area are forest management and mineral exploration and development. Intensive public and private recreation will not be a priority; however, a limited amount of extensive unstructured public recreation will continue. Municipal planning controls are in effect in portions of this area around the Towns of Iroquois Falls, Smooth Rock Falls and Cochrane. Land use in these areas will conform to these controls. This area also contains scattered but significant fish and wildlife habitat areas, e.g. pickerel spawning sites, osprey nest sites and heronries. Additionally, there are timber plantations that may require protection. Management of these areas may include seasonal restrictions, selective harvesting or outright protection. General development and activity on and near these features may be discouraged. A small portion of this area overlaps with the Burntbush, Two Peaks and Straggler Lakes Areas of the Approved Cochrane District Remote (Wilderness) Tourism Strategy. Management of the overlapped area will be subject to the Approved Cochrane District Remote (Wilderness) Tourism Strategy (1997). Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Cochrane District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
