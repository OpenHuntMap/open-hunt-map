## Land area description

Urban areas include the built-up areas of Sault Ste. Marie, Bruce Mines, Goulais River, Desbarats, Searchmont, Kirby's Corner, Heyden, Karalash Corners, Echo Bay, Hilton Beach and Richard's Landing. The boundaries of the above-noted areas are usually defined in municipal official plans and/or zoning by-laws.Other communities which do not physically occupy a great deal of land area have not been included in this area description and will be dealt with in the areas where they are found.The primary land use presently occurring in this area is urban development. Other existing land uses include agriculture, fishing, aggregate extraction, forest management, wildlife management (deer yards) and outdoor recreation.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The general land use intent in this area will be for urban development with provisions for public recreation. All other activities will be permitted with the exception of Crown cottaging due to the lack of Crown land. Some agricultural lands are also found within this area. MNR will support the retention of these as agricultural lands.Crown water lots will be disposed for public or private use, subject to municipal official plans and associated zoning by-laws.Discussions with municipal authorities and appropriate provincial agencies should occur prior to any disposition. Major disposition is also subject to the Ministry of the Environment's review which may result in the requirement of an environmental assessment. Disposition which would result in direct or indirect negative impacts on any Ministry program will be discouraged.All activities in the area will take into consideration the known historical sites, vegetation sites, and nesting sites. The specific location for each can be found in a compendium at the District office.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
