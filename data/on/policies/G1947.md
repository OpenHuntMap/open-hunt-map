## Land area description

The area abuts 3 provincial parks; Amable Du Fond River, Mattawa River and Additions, and Widdifield as well as the Boom Creek Conservation Reserve. The area is comprised of portions of two groups of watershed units. Those in the western portion straddle the north-south height of land and drain west to Lake Nipissing or east to Trout Lake and the Mattawa River system. The eastern section consists of the lower portion of a group of watershed units draining north from the Algonquin Highlands into the Mattawa and Ottawa Rivers. In the western portion, above the east-west trending escarpment, there is an area of deep glacial sand and gravel deposits with shallow soils interspersed with exposed bedrock and wetlands in much of the southern portion. Terrain in the eastern portion is weakly broken and soils are deep with some bare and shallow areas. These deep soils of the central lowlands are well suited to agriculture and timber production. Within the area is a large population which includes the outskirts of the City of North Bay and several smaller communities. The town of Mattawa, and historic settlement, is situated at the confluence of the Mattawa and Ottawa Rivers. The entire area with the exception of Phelps Township has some form of municipal government. MNR is responsible for the management of Crown land and natural resources in organized and unorganized townships. Rural areas are experiencing development pressures from people seeking rural residential opportunities who are employed in the City of North Bay and other urban areas. Trout Lake, Lake Nosbonsing, and the Mattawa River are important recreational features. The area contains the headwaters of several brook trout streams and includes Trout Lake. The area south of the Mattawa River contribute to deer hunting opportunities for local residents.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Urban and rural residential uses with associated agricultural and recreational activities are predominant and will continue to be in the future. Wildlife management and resource production are existing uses in this area and there is no intent to modify the nature of the area except to encourage marginal agricultural lands to be returned to forest production. A portion of the Mattawa deer wintering area and other small wintering areas extend into this zone and should receive consideration when developing resource management plans and in the Ministry’s review of the plans of other agencies. Trout Lake, the water supply for the City of North Bay and an important cold water fishery, should be protected against any development which may impair water quality. The intent is to manage Trout Lake for lake trout. A number of flood prone areas have been identified in both urbanized and rural areas. Implementation of the Provincial Policy Statement is particularly important in this area. Important aggregate deposits, and potential fish hatchery sites and their associated water supplies should be protected. Several sites, where protection is the main theme of management, heronries, archaeological sites, etc.) are found in this area. These and other such sites, which may be found in the future, will be protected during resource management operations and will be identified as values requiring protection in municipal planning. Any proposed planning on activities adjacent to any provincial park or conservation reserve will consider the management intent and direction of these protected areas.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in North Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
