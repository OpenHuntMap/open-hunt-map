## Land area description

Cultural Landscape Waterways Enhanced Management Area is located along many waterwaysand/or portions of waterways as described in the Land Use Strategy for the Whitefeather Forestand Adjacent Areas. Waterways include the Berens River, Throat River, Nungesser River,Serpent River, Mamakwash Lake, Dowling River and others. In many cases, the CulturalLandscape Waterways Enhanced Management Area complements the Cultural LandscapeWaterway Dedicated Protected Area by providing an additional focus on remoteness and culturalheritage values.The policy map and the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas Land Use Strategy should bereferenced for additional detail on a case-by-case basis.The waterways, which include rivers and lakes along their course, are a defining feature of thislandscape. Waterways are a hub of activity for customary travel and cultural purposes. Sacredsites are found throughout, and First Nation people conduct various traditional activities on thisland for livelihood and cultural purposes. Notable fish and wildlife includes the Berens River lakesturgeon population, woodland caribou and wolverine. Existing development is limited to severalremote tourism base lodges and outposts, a private boat cache, and several locations with an allweather and/or winter road crossing. There is a potential interest in new crossings for roadcorridors to access neighboring First Nations such as Poplar Hill, McDowell Lake, and NorthSpirit Lake. McInnes Lake, identified as a waterway located in the north-central portion of theWhitefeather Forest is notable as a lake trout lake with several remote tourism venues.Existing development in the defined Cultural Landscape Waterway Enhanced Management Areais limited to the community of Pikangikum, several remote tourism base lodges and outposts(three on patent land), boat caches, access points, one campground, and several locations withan all weather and/or winter road crossing. Management boundaries of relevance to the areainclude bear management areas, baitfish blocks, traplines, wild rice harvesting licences, andwildlife management units. There is a proposed hydro corridor in the area, and there is apotential need for new crossings for road corridors to access neighboring First Nations. Thereare also several locations having Provincially Significant Mineral Potential.

## Land use intent

These areas are dedicated to celebrating the importance of waterways to Pikangikum people andto provide for conservation of the ecology of waterways through promotion of compatible activitiessuch as tradtional use, eco-cultural tourism, recreation, scientific research, wildernessappreciation, and travel. Cultural heritage enhanced management areas are also intended toprovide a gradient of use for culturally important waterways by limiting the extent and nature ofdevelopment and access from other land use areas. The intent is to minimize road access withinthis EMA, to support the remoteness objective for waterways. Limited crossing of waterways tofacilitate access will be supported for new opportunities including mining and forestry, and forcommunity access. Forestry will not be pursued as an activity in the cultural heritage EMAextending from the eastern shore of the Flanagan River to the boundary of the Sampson LakeDedicated Protected Area.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Land Use Strategy for the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas proposes the creation ofa new category of EMA for the recognition and protection of cultural, and/or historic values andlandscapes, while allowing a range of resource activities. The area specific cultural heritage EMAand land use policies outlined in this policy report are approved. The creation of a new provinicalEMA category for cultural heritage will be explored.Activities are permitted, but will be subject to conditions to protect the cultural heritage values in,or adjacent to the EMA. Where new access is desired, a strategic access planning approach thatconsiders needs, and guides controls on the planning and use of roads will be applied to providea high degree of remoteness and protect significant values.Management will be in accordance with the Land Use Strategy for the Whitefeather Forest andAdjacent Areas and MNR policies.
