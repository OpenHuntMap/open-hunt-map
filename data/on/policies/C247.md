## Land area description

Byrnes Lake White Birch Conservation Reserve, in Site District 5E-1, protects a provincially significant representation of white birch dominated forest stands on moderately broken, shallow, sandy till uplands, as well as red oak at the northern extent of its range. Additionally, poplar, white pine, red maple, sugar maple, and eastern white cedar can be found in mixedwood forest communities throughout the site. This conservation reserve falls within two tertiary watersheds of the Lake Huron Basin, and contains the headwaters of tributaries flowing into the Little Thessalon River and Big Basswood Lake. Long Lake II, Byrnes, Prospect, Rainbow, Sucker, and Bailey lakes are all encompassed within this site. Crockford's Marsh is located in the east, and smaller muskegs and thicket swamps are found scattered throughout this conservation reserve.Byrnes Lake White Birch Conservation Reserve was regulated on November 22, 2002. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.This conservation reserve is located approximately 65 kilometres (km) east of the City of Sault Ste. Marie and 10 km north of the Town of Thessalon, in Bridgland and Wells townships. Multiple Use Area (G1890), and Winter Deer Habitat (G1893) General Use areas surround the site.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Byrnes Lake White Birch Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on April 2, 2004 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis, and must pass a Test of Compatibility, and comply with requirements under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures, to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
