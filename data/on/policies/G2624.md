## Land area description

The lands and waters lying beyond the urban and rural areas of Thunder Bay City and the recreational focuses of the boundary waterways and Lac des Mille Lacs, Dog, Shebandowan and Northern Light Lakes is the Hinterland. The resources of this large area support local industries and an extensive recreational and tourism industry.Development is sparse for the whole area but pockets of moderate to dense development occur along major rail and highway corridors at places like Shabaqua and Upsala. Other types of development include tourism lodges, highway services such as motels and gas stations, the Trans Canada pipeline, hydro transmission lines and numerous secondary and tertiary resource access roads.The two Indian Reserves at Seine River and Lac des Mille Lacs lie in this area but the policies of this document do not apply to them. Several historic and archaeological sites, sensitive fish and wildlife resources, and interesting earth science features have been recognized throughout the area.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

The watershed of Quetico Provincial Park is an Area of Concern. Resource management and land use activities in the watershed will be carried out in consideration of the protection of values within the park. The major role of this large area is to produce resources that support local industries. A secondary role will be to provide extensive Crown land recreation, cottaging and tourism opportunities for the residents of and travelers.Service corridors such as the Atikokan to Thunder Bay transmission line and the twinning of the Trans Canada pipeline will continue to be developed in this area.Within resource operations and land use development, known cultural and natural features of value will be protected. An effort will be made to provide additional cottages on Crown land in the future if demand warrants and private lots are not available. Lakes which have been identified for their existing tourism potential and for future access control and special management are Clay and Powell, Legris, Survey, Holinshead, Gillard, Whitebirch, McGinness, Moss, Mosher and Bishop Lakes.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Thunder Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).Hinterland General Use Area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Hinterland General Use Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS)at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .

## Addendum

The following are designated as Tourism Lakes: Clay and Powell, Legris, Survey, Holinshead, Gillard, Whitebirch, McGinness, Moss, Mosher and Bishop Lakes. Badour, Bemar, Big McCloud, Bishop, Candide, Clay and Powell, Damon, Dora, East Lily, Gillard, Gosling, Gravel Beach, Greenwood, Harry, Holinshead, Kabatay, Kearns, Kekekuab, Kitchiwatchi, Legris, Leigh, Loganberry, McCausland, McGuiness, Mettawi, Mosher, Moss, Peewatai, Piche, Pyramid, Quorn, Shaver, Stonehouse, Pyramid, Unnamed Lakes: 26, 31, 32, 34, 52, Whitebirch Lakes, and portions of Wawlag & Roaring Rivers are identified as remote tourism lakes. In addition to the provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of these Tourism Lakes: - These water bodies will be managed according to the access and areas of concern policies and the “Timber Management Guidelines for the Protection of Tourism Values”. - Blossom, Cowan, Empire, Flatrock, Flower, Fountain, Garden, Globe, Heaven, Myles, Tease, Unnamed Lake 75, Vivid, Williams Lakes have road accessed tourism potential. - Traditional accesses to the water bodies will be allowed to continue.
