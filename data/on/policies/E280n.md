## Land area description

Batchawana Bay-Carp Lake Raised Delta Enhanced Management Area is located approximately 45 km northwest of the City of Sault Ste. Marie in Palmer Township and Ecodistrict 5E-13. Access to the Enhanced Management Area is provided by a number of forest access roads that branch off of Tribag Mine Road and East Carp River Road. Batchawana Bay-Carp Lake Raised Delta Enhanced Management Area consists of two sections. The largest section is located east of Batchawana River Provincial Park (P282) and encompasses the northern portion of Carp Lake. The smaller section runs parallel to part of the west boundary of Batchawana River Provincial Park.Batchawana Bay-Carp Lake Raised Delta Enhanced Management Area is an area of earth science significance. In this area, a large bluff formed about 5,000 years ago, marking the Nipissing stage. The Nipissing stage was one of the last "prehistoric" lake levels that existed before the current levels and drainage of the Great Lakes were established. Above the bluff, a delta of the much older Minong and post-Minong lake stages of the Superior basin is found, formed when glacial ice stood along its north shore. Below the Nipissing bluff are younger landforms and features that show the downward progress of the shoreline. The Carp Lake site was formed by progressively younger rivers as they emptied into the high lake levels in the Superior basin, creating a multi-layered delta. The delta is now raised above the surrounding land, and overlooks the current shore below. Batchawana Bay-Carp Lake Raised Delta Enhanced Management Area captures part of this significant earth science feature. In conjunction with the features on the patented land south of the site, this Enhanced Management Area is considered to be provincially significant.

## Land use intent

The land use intent of this area is to protect the earth science features found in Batchewana Bay-Carp Lake Raised Delta Enhanced Management Area, while allowing for a range of resource activities. This is an important recreational and resource sector (forestry, mining) area.Land use direction and resource management activities will be compatible with protecting the recreation and natural values of the Batchawana River Provincial Park.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
