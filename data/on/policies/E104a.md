## Land area description

This extensive area is heavily forested, interspersed with innumerable lakes and rivers. White pine forests with high timber values predominate. The area provides habitat for a wide diversity of fish and wildlife species, including several vulnerable or threatened species. There are also a number of locations with unusual or rare plant species. Part of the area is within the provincially important Loring deer yard. The area is very important for a range of back-country recreational activities like fishing, big game hunting (moose, deer, bear), canoeing, kayaking, camping, and nature appreciation. The bear density is relatively high in part of this area, since it is reserved from commercial bear hunting. The area contains many recreation camps and snowmobile trails. It is crossed by a main CNR railway line. The area has First Nations cultural heritage values. The area contains high forest and mineral industry values.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The intent of the area is to provide opportunities for backcountry recreation and tourism, as well as opportunities for resource management, including forest management.Maintain the remote access characteristics of this multiple use area, by continuing to implement the existing direction of the MNR Parry Sound District Land Use Guidelines. The area is comprised of a Restricted Access Area in the southern part of this Enhanced Management Area, and the Parry Sound Wildlands in the north part of this Enhanced Management Area, in which there are restricted access policies as well as special resource management controls. This restricted access land use direction complements the protection of recreational values along the proposed Noganosh Lake and Magnetawan River provincial parks.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Parry Sound District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Remote access enhanced management areas are intended to maintain the remote character of selected areas. Typically, these are relatively large areas which provide the public and tourism operators with high-quality remote recreational experiences including hunting, fishing, canoeing, and camping. Given the large size, remoteness, and relative absence of roads, these areas will play a significant role in protecting wilderness values outside the parks and protected areas system.
