## Land area description

The St. Williams Conservation Reserve (SWCR) is located in the townships of South Walsingham and Charlotteviile, Norfolk County, approximately 20 kilometers southwest of the Town of Simcoe. The SWCR comprises two geographically separate parcels of land. The first parcel contains lands near the leased nursery operation property, referred to as the Nursery Tract, including the Manestar property. These lands are associated with the old provincial Forest Station. The second parcel refers to lands called the Turkey Point Tract, near Turkey Point Provincial Park and Turkey Point marsh.The SWCR represents one of the largest blocks of forest in the Carolinian Life Zone of extreme southern Ontario and are recognized regionally, provincially, nationally as being singularly important for the conservation of biological diversity and natural heritage.The SWCR are on the Norfolk sand Plain, a delta of glacial Lakes Whittlesey and Warren. The Nursery and Manestar Tracts fall mainly within the mid-reaches of the watershed of Dedrick Creek, which rises several kilometers above the tract and flows through the southeast portion where it has been dammed for a nursery irrigation pond. Downstream, Dedrick Creek powers the Backhouse Mill and empties into Long Point’s Inner Bay. The Turkey Point tract drains into a short creek that rises within the property and flows through its core before emptying over the bluff into Turkey Point marsh. This creek supports a fish rearing station within the tract. The tracts are within a landscape of forests and agriculture, and uses characteristic of the Norfolk Sand Plain. Both tracts are important features within the Long Point world Biosphere Reserve.

## Land use intent

The property will be managed with the long-term goal of conserving natural heritage values while permitting compatible recreational activities.Areas such as the St. Williams Conservation Reserve are considered to be sensitive, requiring protection from incompatible activities if their values are to ensure over time. MNR has established conservation reserves as a new tool to offer protection for natural heritage areas on public lands, while permitting many traditional public lands uses to continue.
