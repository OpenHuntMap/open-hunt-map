## Land area description

Galbraith Peatland Conservation Reserve protects a provincially significant wetland complex, which constitutes more than 30% of the site. Prehistoric lake sediments form the base of this complex which is composed of bog, swamp and fen habitats. Eastern white cedar and tamarack populate wetland areas, along with shrub and herb species such as leatherleaf, labrador tea, wild sarsaparilla, and goldthread. Black spruce dominated forest communities are prevalent throughout the central area of the conservation reserve, and poplar and sugar maple species are associated with the bedrock ridge in the northern portion of the site. A small unnamed creek drains the southwest section of the site, into Wanamaker Creek, as part of a tertiary watershed of the Lake Huron Basin. This conservation reserve's cultural uniqueness derives from the mid-1970s to the mid-1990s, when peat was extracted by the Ministry of Natural Resources for horticultural use at the Kirkwood Tree Nursery near the Town of Thessalon.Galbraith Peatland Conservation Reserve was regulated on November 22, 2002. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.Galbraith Peatland Conservation Reserve is located approximately 50 kilometres (km) east of the City of Sault Ste. Marie, and 20 km north of the Town of Bruce Mines, in Galbraith Township. This site is surrounded by the Agricultural Lands General Use Area (G1892).

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Galbraith Peatland Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on April 2, 2004 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis, and must pass a Test of Compatibility, and comply with requirements under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures, to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
