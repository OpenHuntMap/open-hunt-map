## Land area description

This area is bounded by Umfreville Lake and the English River to the south, the South Pakwash Road to the east, the Iriam Lake Road and Woodland Caribou Park to the north and the Ontario-Manitoba border to the west. The majority of the area lies within the Kenora Forest, although the eastern portion forms part of the Whiskey Jack Forest. Access is generally limited to aircraft, although the South Pakwash Road provides primary road access along the area’s eastern boundary. A restricted access road presently exists into Werner Lake from Manitoba. The Werner Lake area is covered by a Restricted Area Order (under the Public Lands Act) to limit development to mining purposes only.The area includes a portion of the Wabaseemoong Traditional Land Use Area and is subject to the 1983 Ontario-Islington Band Agreement.The North English River Area is in the North Kenora Pilot Project Agreement Area, where specific provisions for the protection of the remote resource-based tourism industry are in effect.The following lakes have been designated as tourism lakes and “limited access lakes”: Babe, Dowswell, Fletcher, Homer, Joe, Long, Nancy, Pineneedle, Right, Roger, Rolston/Chaval, Salvesen, Trapline, Treelined, Trout, West Rolston.This area is located within the Woodland Caribou Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.Part of this area is subject to the Range Management Policy in Support of Woodland Caribou Conservation and Recovery (2014). The Range Management Policy is accessible via the “Source of Direction.””

## Land use intent

The primary activities in this area will be remote fly-in tourism and timber harvesting. Lakes with tourist lodges or outpost camps are designated as “limited access lakes”. Access via road to these designated lakes will be controlled during part of the year. Access to these lakes by traditional means is acceptable (e.g. fly-in, canoe, portages, etc).Future access into the area could result from the construction of the Sydney Lake Road. The road strategy for this road will reflect conditions that protect the tourism resources of the area being accessed. Prior to any development proposal within 2 kilometres of the Ontario-Manitoba border being approved, discussions will be held with the Manitoba government to avoid potential conflicts and minimize any negative impacts. The Restricted Area Order for Werner Lake will be maintained.The area contains a significant resource based tourism industry. Management of this area will also be governed by the general policies of the Kenora District Land Use Guidelines, and will follow the provisions of the North Kenora Pilot Project Agreement, the Kenora Forest Management Plan, the Whiskey Jack Forest Management Plan and the 1983 Ontario-Islington Band Agreement.
