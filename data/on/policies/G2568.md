## Land area description

This large area is located north and east of Atikokan, south of Highway 11, east of Atikokan and north of Calm Lake. Two small segments near Pekagoning Lake complete the area. Portions of the Sapawe Forest and the Crossroute Forest comprise this area.Present use includes resource extraction, recreation and commercial tourist development. There are cottages, commercial tourist lodges and commercial outpost camps in the area. Development is sparse.Existing uses include logging, mineral exploration, trapping, commercial bait fishing, aggregate extraction, recreation, tourism and wild rice harvesting. Recreational use of the area by both residents and nonresidents for angling and hunting is intensive. The area contains two hydro line corridors, a natural gas pipeline corridor and a railway.The area is significant for resource extraction, tourism and recreation. The majority of the area is well accessed by resource extraction roads.The following are designated as tourism lakes: McQuat, Keewatin, Light, Pisky, Hematite, Richardson, Gargoyle, Surprise, Finlayson, Bradshaw, Husband, Lizard and North of Finlayson.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The primary use in the area will continue to be timber production. Mineral exploration and development will be encouraged. Recreational levels will be increased through increased cottaging development. An increase in commercial outpost camps and tourist lodges will be encouraged.Access will generally be restricted to roads necessary for resource extraction. Lake trout lakes and tourist lakes will be protected from road access. Lakes having potential for cottage development will be protected from road access until individual lake development plans are prepared.Areas of Concern will be applied as required to lakes with recreational and tourism development, significant fish and wildlife features, major and secondary highways, the Eye River canoe route, to protect rare and endangered species and sensitive areas. The watershed of Quetico Provincial Park is an Area of Concern. Resource management and land use activities in the watershed will be carried out in consideration of the protection of values within the park. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Atikokan District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of Natural lake trout lakes:- lakes shall have a 120 metre Area of Concern- timber harvesting will not be permitted within 120 metres
