## Land area description

The Lady Evelyn River was first designated as a Wild River (now waterway) park in 1973. The park was expanded to its present size and designated as a wilderness class park in 1983. It includes the lakes-of-origin for the endangered Aurora Trout. The park's rugged topography encompasses many high ridges such as Maple Mountain and Ishpatina Ridge, the highest point in Ontario.The park also contains significant geological, biological, and cultural features including: representative bedrock geology formations, a ground moraine, kettled outwash plains, sand dunes, river and lake valley terrain, sacred Aboriginal sites, and remnant stands of old growth red and white pine.Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater offers a variety of wilderness canoeing opportunities with over 235-kilometres of canoe routes which are part of a larger canoe route network of over 2,400-kilometres in the Temagami area. The routes also connect to four adjacent waterway class parks and routes on adjacent Crown land.Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater Provincial Park was regulated as a provincial park in 1983.The park also contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Inland Ontario Lakes Designated for Lake Trout Management (2006), accessible in the Source of Direction portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater Provincial Park will be managed to protect the Temagami planning area's largest natural heritage area by developing ecosystem restoration strategies for a large roadless area in the site region's representative natural heritage setting. The intent is also to sensitively protect Aboriginal cultural heritage landscapes and sacred sites and to manage a high concentration of wilderness canoe routes and hiking areas with wilderness camping and remote commercial tourism opportunities. The park will also provide enhanced protection to the only known lakes with naturally occurring Aurora trout.

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of this provincial park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks: Planning and Management Policies (1992), and the Temagami Area Park Management Plan (2007). The permitted uses associated with provincial parks are found in approved management direction, which are available on the Ontario Parks website.
