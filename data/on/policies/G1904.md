## Land area description

This area is comprised roughly of the central area of St. Joseph Island, excluding the critical areas for aggregate extraction, agriculture and deer yarding areas. The primary land use presently occurring in this area is agriculture. Other land uses include recreation and resource production. The great majority of these lands are under private ownership.This area is located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The general land use intent in this area is agriculture and resource production. Other land use activities will be permitted with the exception of Crown cottaging. MNR will encourage, through the municipal and local planning boards, the maintenance of good agricultural lands. All Crown land development and uses will attempt to conform to the approved official plan and zoning by-law. MNR will recognize an MOE sampling station in Jocelyn Township. MNR will also recognize the significance of the National Park (Old Fort St. Joseph) located in this area as well as the bird sanctuary within this park. Some deer yards are found within this area. In these yarding areas, MNR will encourage their protection and maintenance essentially through proper forest management techniques.MNR will encourage the preservation of the important spawning habitats of Milford Haven and Gawas Creeks. Activities in the area will take into consideration known nesting sites and historical sites. The specific locations can be found in a compendium at the District office. The important water fowl areas adjacent to St. Joseph Island will be protected to minimize external disruptions. These disruptions can be physical, such as aquatic vegetation removal, or qualitative, such as high noise levels from residential subdivisions. Development will be discouraged in these areas. On-shore mining developments may expand underground, outward under the Great Lakes, subject to existing international agreements. Development proposals and dispositions, including water lots, on the Great Lakes shoreline will be reviewed for conformity with the District’s Shoreline Management Plan.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
