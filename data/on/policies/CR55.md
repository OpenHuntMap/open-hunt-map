## Land area description

The Smith Lake Conservation Reserve is located southwest of the Town of Elk Lake within the Montreal River watershed. The values and uses within the conservation reserve include representative old growth red and white pine stands, high quality cold-water fisheries in Smith Lake, and the an acid-stressed Lulu Lake. There are remote fly-in tourism operations on Smith Lake and Lulu Lake as well as low-intensity ATV access to Smith Lake. The conservation reserve also encompasses part of Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater Wilderness Park's watershed.Smith Lake was regulated as a conservation reserve on April 6, 2001.This area also contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Inland Ontario Lakes Designated for Lake Trout Management (2006) accessible in the Source of Direction portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The intent of the Smith Lake Conservation Reserve is to protect the old growth pine ecosystems, quality cold water fisheries, headwaters of Smoothwater Lake, and to encourage compatible remote tourism and recreation activities adjacent to Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater Wilderness Park without resource extraction activities. Land use concerns include allowing natural processes to occur and angling pressure on a heavily used remote tourism lake (Smith Lake), Other concerns include a portion of this watershed that is not a protected area, over-harvesting and acidification concerns for Lulu Lake, increased access to Smith Lake by ATV, and the potential for upgrading of trails to truck access.The management area objectives are to allow natural processes to occur, protect the headwaters of the wilderness park, and to allow identified ATV use to continue, but not expand nor be upgraded to public truck access. Objectives also include restoring the Lulu Lake cold-water fishery and ensuring no groomed snowmobile trails pass through the conservation reserve. The land use strategies are to develop a fisheries strategy to address the over-harvesting on Smith Lake and to plan access on surrounding Crown lands to minimize conflicts.
