## Land area description

The waters and offshore islands of Lake Superior are a tourism and recreational focus for the people of Thunder Bay as well as a major source of commercial fish.Present use is primarily commercial fishing, sport fishing, boating, sailing, camping and hunting. Development on the offshore islands is sparse. Many of these islands offer important bird nesting, feeding and shelter sites, sensitive plant communities and significant earth science features. The lake trout resource of Lake Superior is on the rebound and is expected to contribute to sport angling needs. The Crown land portion of Pie Island is not included in this land use area.This area is located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The intent of this area is to provide a large relatively undeveloped area close to the City of Thunder Bay for low intensity public recreation and tourism opportunities. There will continue to be limited resource extraction and the extraction which does occur will abide by the required 300 metre setback from island shores, will protect sensitive features and be modified to preserve the significant recreational and aesthetic values of this area. The aggregate extraction from the bed of Black Bay which took place many years ago, is not anticipated to reoccur because of alternate available dry land sites. However, should it be required, protection of waterfowl, fisheries and wild rice resources would be paramount in the operating guidelines. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Thunder Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).Lake Superiort General Use Area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Lake Superiort General Use Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS)at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .
