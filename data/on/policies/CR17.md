## Land area description

The White Bear Forest Conservation Reserve is located within the Matabitchuan River/Temagami River watershed. The values and uses within the reserve include a provincially significant old growth white and red pine forest, significant stands of white birch and cedar located on the western shore of Cassels Lake, and hiking trails with a scenic lookout adjacent to the town of Temagami. The Gowganda land formation, which is considered a regionally significant feature, is also well represented in the reserve as it is at the end of its considerable exposure in Ontario. The conservation reserves overall diversity, physical condition, design, and current and potential recreational and interpretive values are also considered significant. Finally, the reserve maintains natural linkages that connect the conservation reserve to the surrounding forested and aquatic landscapes found within theTemagami region.The White Bear Forest was regulated as a conservation reserve on July 4,1997.The White Bear Forest Conservation Reserve also contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Inland Ontario Lakes Designated for Lake Trout Management (2006) accessible in the Source of Direction portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The intent of the White Bear Forest Conservation Reserve is to protect the cultural, natural heritage, and recreational values (old growth and hiking trails) within the reserve, and to identify natural, cultural heritage appreciation, and recreation opportunities in an area which is easily accessible from the town of Temagami. The management area objectives are to allow natural processes to occur in the protected portion of the management area and to maintain backcountry recreation opportunities close to an urban area. Land use management concerns include the potential for conflicting uses of trails (motorized and non-motorized) and the need for natural disturbances to create suitable conditions for pine regeneration. The strategy for this area is to continue to foster the White Bear Forest partnership.

Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing conservation reserve policy, the Temagami Land Use Plan (1997), and the White Bear Forest Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (January 2002). Any new uses and commercial activities associated with conservation reserves will be considered on a case-by-case basis and will be required to pass a test of compatibility to be acceptable.
