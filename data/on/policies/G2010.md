## Land area description

Located North of the lowlands, this is a group of watershed units, comprised of generally moderate to strongly broken uplands draining easterly into the Ottawa River. Many of the weakly broken valleys or plains included are overlain with water and ice-laid glacial deposits of sand, and gravel with pockets of silt and clay. The entire area is unorganized except for Mattawan Township area. There are no existing, designated access points. There are areas of high mineral potential in Butler and Antoine Townships. Timber production from Crown land is a major industrial use.The area abuts several provincial parks: Jocko River, Mattawa River Additions, Ottawa River, Alexander Lake Forest, and Samuel De Champlain.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The primary use proposed is resource production and extraction. Wildlife and fisheries management are important secondary uses (e.g. moose range and deer yard habitat improvement, fish stocking, etc.). Extensive recreational uses such as hunting, fishing, back-country travel, canoeing, camping, cross-country skiing and other day trip activities are existing recognized uses. Some intensive uses, cross-country skiing and downhill ski facilities, are within the area and these will require consideration in subsequent management plans. Land uses adjacent to Brule Lake are also subject to the more detailed policies of the Lake Management Plan. A clean, assured water supply for the existing fish hatchery and potential expansion of the facility is to be a consideration in all land management decisions in French, Butler and to a lesser degree Mulock Townships. Several sites, where protection is the main theme of management, (e.g. heronries, archaeological sites, etc.) are found in this area. These and other such sites, which may be found in the future, will be protected during resource management operations, and will be identified as values for protection in municipal planning. Any proposed planning on activities adjacent to any provincial park will consider the management intent and direction of the protected areas. MNR is responsible for the management of Crown land and natural resources in organized and unorganized townships.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in North Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
