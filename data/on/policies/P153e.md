## Land area description

Restoule Provincial Park is situated 40 kilometres southwest of North Bay, in the Great Lakes-St. Lawrence Forest, in the southern portion of Ecodistrict 5E-5.Vegetation in the park represents a variety of upland, wetland and aquatic communities. Much of the forest consists of mixed sugar maple, red oak, American beech and hemlock mixed forests. The vegetation in the park grows on moderately rolling bedrock with a thin to moderately deep mantle of sandy till interspersed with bedrock ridges and cliffs along Stormy Lake.The park is connected by a popular canoe route between Stormy Lake and French River Provincial Park. This narrow band of shoreline supports shrub thicket communities with upland coniferous forest intermixed with sugar maple, red maple, and yellow birch. A cobble apron characterizes much of the Restoule River shoreline.Restoule, Story and Clear lakes are designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy reportRestoule Provincial Park is a natural environment park and was originally regulated in 1963. An addition was made to the park in 2003 as a result of Ontario’s Living Legacy.

## Land use intent

Restoule Provincial Park is protected by regulation under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act. Ontario Regulation No. 493/85, Schedule 50, delinates the boundary of the park.
