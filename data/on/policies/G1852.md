## Land area description

This area consists of Larder Lake and its shoreline area. (The urban communities of Virginiatown and Larder Lake are not considered to be within this area, see Area 4). Access to the area is provided by Highways 66 and 624. McVittie and Hearst Townships are under the municipal control of the Corporation of the Township of Larder Lake. The Township of McGarry is also organized. The predominant use is recreation. A significant amount of cold water fish production comes from Larder Lake. Larder Lake is part of a designated canoe route. There is municipal waterfront park and campground at Larder Lake. Mineral potential is high and mineral exploration is ongoing. The amount of timber harvesting in the area is minor. The area contains one identified archaeological site and a heron nesting site.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The primary intent is general recreation and particularly day-use opportunities. Any recreational development which is not detrimental to or dependent on the sport fishery is encouraged. Resource extraction may continue but particular emphasis will be placed on the protection of the water quality for the maintenance of the fishery. Industrial development will be encouraged to locate within or adjacent to existing urban areas and in accordance with existing municipal development controls. The Ministry will investigate further mechanisms for protecting the regionally significant cultural resources of Pearl Beach. Urban development allowed. The Ministry will continue its liaison with the Town of Larder lake, the Blanche River-Bayly Watershed and Lake Protection Association, and the Wendigo Cottagers Association regarding the maintenance of an acceptable water level for Larder Lake.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Kirkland Lake District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
