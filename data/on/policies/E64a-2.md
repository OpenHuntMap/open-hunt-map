## Land area description

Enhanced Management Area E64a is located almost entirely within the boundaries of the Leslie M. Frost Natural Resource Centre. To provide clear land use direction, their policies have been combined into this report and apply to that area where the Enhanced Management Area and the Frost Centre overlap.Located in the Haliburton Highlands, this enhanced management area in Sherborne and Stanhope townships is characterized by rugged Shield topography with mixed forest types. Land use and resource values include high forestry and mineral potential values, intensive cottaging on larger lakes, moderate hunting and fishing activities and a popular canoe route with backcountry camping opportunities. This Enhanced Management Area surrounds the Clear Lake Conservation Reserve.The Leslie M. Frost Natural Resources Centre was established by MNR in 1974 as a resource management education centre. Its main goal is to foster a wider public understanding and appreciation of Ontario's natural resources, their management and use. A plan for the Frost Centre was approved in 1980.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The primary intent of this area is to maintain the remote access characteristics while managing for its high value forest resources and backcountry recreation opportunities. In addition, three distinct objectives have been established for the Frost Centre. These relate to education and demonstration, integrated resource management, and innovative and experimental resource management.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999), the Integrated Plan for Land Use and Resource Development (1980) - the Frost Centre plan, and the general policies of the Bracebridge District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Remote access enhanced management areas are intended to maintain the remote character of selected areas. Typically, these are relatively large areas which provide the public and tourism operators with high-quality remote recreational experiences including hunting, fishing, canoeing, and camping. Given the large size, remoteness, and relative absence of roads, these areas will play a significant role in protecting wilderness values outside the parks and protected areas system.
