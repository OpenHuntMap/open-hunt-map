## Land area description

Situated along the southern fringe of the Canadian Shield, Wakami is dotted with areas of southern tree species within the encompassing boreal forest. The highlands in the region act as a watershed between Hudson Bay to the north and the Great Lakes to the south.The area was inhabited by the Ojibwa and Cree for several centuries. The park gets its name from a word meaning 'still and clear waters', referring to elongated Lake Wakami, which lies in its center.Because the park is at the southern edge of the boreal forest, it has some trees more common in the Great Lake - St. Lawrence forest region as well as more typically northern varieties. Yellow birch, sugar maple and white pine stands are 'biological islands' of southern species in an area of unsuitable northern habitat. More common are jack pines and other northern firs, whose habitat stretches northward.Wakami Lake Provincial Park is also rich in animal life.Wakami Lake Provincial Park was regulated as a provincial park in 1973.

## Land use intent

Wakami Lake Provincial Park will be managed according to Provincial Park policy and Interim Management Statement (1999). While currently a recreation class park, Wakami Lake is managed as a natural environment class park in recognition of its significant cultural, earth science and life science features and its capacity to support a wide range of outdoor recreational activities. Wakami Lake park addition (P1640), a significant nature reserve candidate, will contribute to the park's significance. The classification will be reviewed and policies developed through Park Management Planning for Wakami Lake.
