## Land area description

The Lake Country ("Weeskayjahk Ohtahzhoganiing" ,"Weeskayjahk's Stone Bridge") is locatedimmediately north of Woodland Caribou Provincial Park. This landscape includes numerous lakes, suchas the Keeper, Roderick, Cairns, and Wagin water bodies, each with intensive clusters of importantecological and cultural features. The Lake Country is traversed by a network of summer and wintertraditional travel routes which are evidence of its rich cultural heritage. The headwaters of the Keeper River System, which flows northwest into the Berens River, also originate within the Lake Country. First Nation people conduct various traditional activities on this land for livelihood and cultural purposes.No roads exist in the Lake Country and development is sparse. Structures include: remote tourism outpost camps, and remote tourism main base lodges. There are also many registered commercial boat caches within the Lake Country. Bear management areas, bait fish blocks (located south of the 11th baseline), traplines, wild rice harvesting licences, a commercial fishing licence, and a wildlifemanagement unit are also used to manage the area. The area may support opportunities for new ecoculturaltourism, eco-tourism, recreation, and appreciation of boreal ecological and cultural landscape activities.The Lake Country contains valuable habitat for fish and wildlife species. Notable are several clear water lakes which contain healthy lake trout populations, a species which is relatively uncommon in the area. The presence of woodland caribou and wolverine is significant in Lake Country and throughout the Whitefeather Forest. Woodland caribou winter habitat and numerous caribou calving islands have beenidentified through indigenous knowledge, and western science surveys.

## Land use intent

The intent is to regulate the Lake Country as a provincial park or conservation reserve, although the specific designation and category have not been determined. Existing tourism, eco-cultural tourism, and natural and cultural heritage appreciation are important land use intents. The area will continue to provide support for species at risk, such as woodland caribou and wolverine. Lake Country will contribute to the remoteness objective for the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas Planning Area.Management will also be governed by the Land Use Strategy for the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas, MNR policies, and/or new policies developed through cross-cultural dialogue.

## Permitted uses — preface

The dedicated protected area designation (i.e. Park Class or Conservation Reserve) for the Lake Country is yet to be determined. Interim direction for this area is provided in the Land Use Strategy for the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas (WFAA Strategy) and in the table below. Additional specific direction will be determined by the final designation assigned to this dedicated protected area through cross-cultural dialogue and by future resource management planning.In the absence of resource management planning, new proposals for specific activities will be subject to a review by MNR in consultation with Pikangikum First Nation and Poplar Hill First Nation. This review will consider the intent of the area, direction provided in the WFAA Strategy, impacts on resources and values associated with the Lake Country Dedicated Protected Area, compatible economic opportunities,and potential for economic benefit to Pikangikum First Nation and/or Poplar Hill First Nation. Values associated with the Lake Country include the natural and cultural heritage features of the area, special features, and existing land uses. Maintenance of natural disturbance regimes and remoteness will be given high importance as will the conservation of fish and wildlife species, including woodland caribou, and wolverine.
