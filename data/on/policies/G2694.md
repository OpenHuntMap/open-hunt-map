## Land area description

*Coastal portions of this area are also located within the Great Lakes Heritage*This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The primary activities in the area will be forest management, mineral exploration, mining, hydroelectric power generation, tourism and Crown land recreation. On-shore mining developments may expand underground, outward under the Great Lakes, subject to existing international agreements. Lake trout lakes will be managed to protect and/or rehabilitate their respective lake trout populations and habitat. Tourism lakes will be managed to protect their remote quality.Achievement of the land use intent will be dependent on MNR's ability to influence natural resource management activities on the lands managed by Wagner Ontario Forest Management.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Wawa District Land Use Guidelines (1983), including Tourism section C:8 (1992).Integrated Multiple Resources Management General Use Area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Integrated Multiple Resources Management General Use Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS) at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .

## Addendum

TOURISM LAKESThe following lakes are designated as remote tourism lakes: Anaharea, Apisabigo, Bruce, Budworm, Bulldozer, Cigar, Craig, Daisy, Dave, Dayohessarah, Dibben, Easey, East Nameigos, Esnagi, Flagg, Foot, Foster, Fred, Fulcher, Gould, Hambleton, Hayward, Herrick, Hobon, Jackfish, Jembi, Kabinakagami, Kaginagakog, Kawaweagama, Kinniwabi, Line, Little Craig, Lobo, Long, McCrae, McEwen, McGill, McMurty, Mank, Medhurst, Merekeme, Michi, Missing, Mosambik, Nameigos, North McCrea, North Wejinabikon, Oba, Otter (Cheesehead), Pelt, Phil's, Pickle, Pike, Pinei, Pozzo, Roderic, Rule, Shakashi, Shikwamkwa, South Greenhill, Spangle, Strickland, Tikamaganda, Unnamed east of Line, Unnamed south of Line, Unnamed chain south of McMurty, Upper Dingman, Upper Kawaweagama, Wabatongushi*, Wabenung, Wejinabikun, White Owl, and Zola.The following lakes are designated as a remote tourism lodge lakes: Oba, Esnagi, Kabinakagami, and Wabatongushi*. In addition to the provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of these lakes: *The roads across the narrows and near the south end of the lake will not be subject to access controls designated for other commercial outpost camp lakes.- Timber Management near the lakes will be conducted in accordance with the following principles:i) Areas of highest use immediately adjacent to a designated remote lake will be protected by a no-cut reserve in order to provide recreational users with an area free from timber management activities. The suggested extent of the no-cut zone is to within 90 metres of the lake (120 metres for remote lodge lakes) but may be changed on the basis of: - density of forest cover; - slope; - protection of other values and application of provincial guidelines that provide a larger buffer; and - mutual agreement of the principal parties involved.ii) The aesthetics of surrounding shorelands will be maintained and visual evidence of timber management will be minimized by modifying timber management as necessary within immediate view of the lake. The suggested extent of this zone of no visible disturbance is to within 500 metres of the lake, but may be changed on the basis of: - mutual agreement of the principal parties involved; and - protection of other values.iii) A zone of minimal visual disturbance will be established around the zone of no visible disturbance on designated remote lakes. This zone of minimal disturbance will apply only to forests visible from the lake, and normally to a maximum of 1 kilometre from the lake. In the zone, only partial cutting will be permitted and the residual stand must provide a forested appearance (i.e., a minimum of 50% crown cover. Strip cutting and other modified cutting will be permitted in conifer stands provided that a forest appearance is maintained). Surrounding this zone of minimal visual disturbance, a zone will be established in which cutting within view of the lake will be modified. Normally harvesting will be in a strip or block pattern, with blocks of less than 100 hectares, and no more than 25% of the area clear-cut in any 5 year term of a timber management plan. The suggested extent of the zone of modified cutting is to a distance of 2 kilometres from the lake. The width of these outer two zones may be changed on the basis of: - visibility of roads in or behind the zones; and - mutual agreement of the principal parties involved.iv) In order to minimize disturbance of tourism clients, timber harvesting, road construction and log hauling near (normally within 1.5 kilometres) a designated remote lake will take place after the second week of moose hunting season and before the beginning of walleye season. Site preparation in this area may be permitted during the frost-free season but will normally be limited to the hours of 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. from the opening of walleye season to the end of the second week of moose hunting season. These periods may be changed on the basis of: - fish species and corresponding seasons; - whether the lake is used for moose hunting; - sound buffering or sound carrying ability of surrounding lands; - mutual agreement of the principal parties involved; and - timing restrictions will not necessarily apply when the facilities are not in operation.v) Outpost camp and lodge sites will be provided with a no-cut buffer to shield recreational users from the visual evidence of timber management activities and to provide a disturbance free zone. It is recommended that no timber management activities occur within 300 metres of the property boundary of an outpost camp, or within 400 metres of the property boundary of a remote lodge.vi) Timber harvesting, road construction and log hauling operations within immediate hearing distance (normally 2.5 kilometres) of a remote lodge will normally be restricted to the period from the second week of moose hunting season to the beginning of walleye season.vii) In order to reduce the impacts of new access on fish and game populations, and preserve the remote character of designated lakes, roads will be located far enough away that a reasonable effort must be made to access the lake by traditional means (including by watercraft, on foot, by air or by railway). Normally, new primary and secondary forest access roads will be located at least 2 kilometres from remote lodge lakes, and 1 kilometre from outpost camp lakes, and tertiary roads will be located 400 metres away from remote lodge and outpost camp lakes. However, the minimum distances may be modified on the basis of: - how well topography and vegetation protect the lake from noise, the sight of timber management activities, or easy access; - whether the road construction and hauling can be scheduled for periods when the above negative impacts can be avoided; and - topography and the physical ability to construct the road in a given location.viii) Access roads that potentially create access to a designated remote lake will have access restricted. This may require road use control mechanisms such as signs and/or gates. The restricting sign or gate will normally be erected a minimum of 3 kilometres from the designated remote lake.ix) Trails, including snowmachine and all terrain vehicle trails, will generally not be permitted near a remote lake. Some resource access trails such as trappers’ trails may be authorized on a case by case basis, but public access to the remote lake via the trail will be restricted.x) To supplement the above road location and access control strategies, boat caches, except for approved resource caches by licensed trappers, bait fishermen and prospectors, will not be permitted on outpost camp or remote lodge lakes. Some existing commercial caches may be approved and grandfathered in. If that operation changes hands, that commercial cache will no longer be approved. The following lakes are designated as semi-remote or non-remote tourism takes: Shikwamkwa, Matchinameigos, McMurty, Berry, Sonny, Solitaire, and Jeff. In addition to the provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of these lakes: i) Natural resource dependent commercial tourism accommodations (i.e., normally establishments that are more than 500 metres from a provincial highway) will be afforded protection from negative impacts of timber management by: - establishing a no-cut buffer around accommodation facilities or any lake on which a non-highway corridor establishment is located. Normally this no-cut buffer will extend 90 metres from the facility or lake, but the dimension of the buffer may be changed because of the screening or shielding ability of surrounding topography and vegetation. - establishing a minimal disturbance zone within view of any lake on which a non-highway corridor establishment is located. The suggested extent of the buffer is to within 750 metres of the lake, but the width may be changed because of the screening or shielding ability of surrounding topography and vegetation, by mutual agreement of the principal parties involved, or to protect other values. In the zone, only partial cutting will be permitted and the residual stand must provide a forested appearance (i.e., a minimum of 50% crown cover. Strip cutting and other modified cutting will be permitted in conifer stands provided that a forested appearance is maintained). - ensuring that timber harvesting is scheduled for non-peak season, normally from the end of the second week of moose hunting until the beginning of walleye season. (In the Chapleau Game Preserve, the seasonal restriction will extend only until September 15.)ii) Natural resource dependent highway corridor commercial tourism facilities (i.e., normally less than 500 metres from a provincial highway) will be protected with: - a no-cut buffer that shields logging from view of the establishment. Normally this no-cut buffer will be 50 metres in width, but the width may change because of the screening ability of surrounding topography and vegetation, for the protection of other values, or by the mutual agreement of the parties involved. - establishing a zone (normally within 500 metres of the establishment) in which only partial cutting may occur to a minimum of 50% crown cover. Strip cutting and other modified cutting will be permitted in conifer stands provided that a forested appearance is maintained. It is recommended that partial or modified cutting not include more than 30% of the area during any 20 year period.CANOE ROUTESCategory ''A'':A buffer, normally of 90 metres (may be changed based on density of forest cover, slope, protection of other values), and skyline reserve (normally of 500 metres) will be established on the following Category ''A'' canoe routes, including portages. The residual stand in the skyline reserve must provide at least 50% crown cover: - Dog River from Obatanga Provincial Park to the north boundary of Legarde Township; - Oba River from Oba Lake to the south boundary of Ermine Township; - Kabinakagami River from Anahara Creek to Kabinakagami Lake; - Bremner River; - Lower White River from White Lake Provincial Park to Lake Superior; - Nameigos River in Nameigos Township from the old District Boundary to the Kabinakagami River: and - Pukaskwa River.Category ''B'':A 30 metre no-cut buffer will be established on the following Category ''B'' canoe routes, and portages along these routes must be cleared of slash and other debris after road construction across them are completed. Portages must be easily visible at either side of the road and must be leveled within the right of way to provide adequate footing. No disturbances of portages outside of road rights of way will be permitted. The designation of rivers as canoe routes will not necessarily preclude hydro development on them. - White River (excluding section of river described under Category ''A'' canoe routes). - Depew River; - Kwinkwaga River; - Michipicoten River; - Shikwamkwa River; - Magpie River, and - Dog River from the west end of Hammer Lake to the northern boundary of Obatanga Provincial Park.
