## Land area description

Basswood Lake Hemlock Conservation Reserve, in Site District 5E-1, protects eastern hemlock mixedwood forest communities approaching old-growth status. Sugar maple dominated stands can be found along the south shore of Basswood Lake, and smaller stands of white birch, yellow birch, white pine, and red oak are scattered throughout the site.Basswood Lake Hemlock Conservation Reserve was regulated on November 22, 2002. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.Situated approximately 15 kilometres northeast of the Town of Thessalon, in Day Township, this conservation reserve includes four separate parcels of Crown land located along the south shore of Basswood Lake. Public access to this site is gained solely via water because all four parts of it are surrounded by private lands. This conservation reserve is surrounded by the Basswood/Bright System (G1894), Winter Deer Habitat (G1893), and Agricultural Lands (G1892) General Use areas. Basswood Lake Conservation Reserve is located nearby, on the north shore of Basswood Lake.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Basswood Lake Hemlock Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on April 2, 2004 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis, and must pass a Test of Compatibility, and comply with requirements under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures, to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
