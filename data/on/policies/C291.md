## Land area description

Goulais River Beach Ridges Conservation Reserve is located at the south end of the Goulais River in Eco District 5E-13 (formerly classified as Site District 4E-2). It protects an area dominated by ancient beach ridge landforms and a modern river environment of exceptional quality. Important wetlands (bogs, fens, swamps and marshes) of potential provincial significance comprise a majority of the site. Interspersed between wetlands, upland forests have developed on the raised beach ridges. Black spruce is the dominant conifer species in these stands, and is usually associated with white birch, trembling aspen, tamarack, balsam fir and eastern white cedar. Moose, and a variety of birds are frequent occupants of the site's wetlands. Portions of Cranberry Creek, which is recognized as habitat for brook trout and rainbow trout, are found in the western section of the site. Goulais River Beach Ridges Conservation Reserve was regulated on May 21, 2003. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.This protected area is located approximately 20 kilometres north of the City of Sault Ste. Marie, mainly within Fenwick Township, with small portions extending into Aweres and Pennefather townships. Additionally, this conservation reserve is immediately surrounded by the Goulais (G1906), Agricultural (G1905), and Urban Areas (G1898) General Use areas, and located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature sites are recognized for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Goulais River Beach Ridges Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on April 5, 2005 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy. Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis, and must pass a Test of Compatibility, and comply with requirements under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
