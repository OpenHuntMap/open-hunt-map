## Land area description

This area consists of five sub-areas. Each of these areas is centred along a main forest access road. The five sub-areas are: (i) those areas accessed by the Ivanhoe Road in Ivanhoe, Silk, Wigham, and Coppell Townships - It is already accessed and will continue to be accessed, by timber roads; (ii) the Kebskwasheshi River access road, running mainly through Neelands, Lynch, and Bounsall Townships - This road corridor accesses a number of warmwater and coldwater lakes. It has a long history of Crown land recreation use; (iii) the Pineal Lake/Cow River road corridor - This sub-area includes all those locations that are accessible from the road system southwest of Chapleau into the Cow River area. It also includes the lake areas along Highway 101 west of Chapleau that are accessible from both the Esher Road and the Bird Bath Bay access point road (to Nagasin Lake); (iv) the east Gallagher-McNaught-Halsey- Nimitz area. Much of the recreational potential in this area has just recently been opened up by timber harvesting activities. The area has both warm and coldwater lakes and considerable cottaging potential. One outpost camp will be affected by the access that is being brought into this area; and (v) the game preserve access corridor - This area runs straight north from Chapleau along the main roads into the Chapleau Crown Game Preserve. This road allows access to a large number of warmwater lakes.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Although they have been labeled as ''vehicle recreation areas'', these corridors will, in fact, be true multiple resource use areas. MNR will encourage a program of at least minimal road maintenance, to be carried out in co-operation with other agencies and organizations, in order to keep the main access arteries into these areas open for recreation and resource extraction. Recreation will be a priority in shoreline areas and resource extraction will be priority in backshore areas. Resource management plans will contain guidelines for the protection of significant recreation values, archaeological sites, and fish and wildlife habitat.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Chapleau District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of Natural lake trout lakes:­ no additional access will be permitted­ 120 metre Area of Concern will be applied­ forest management activities may be permitted within the Area of Concern subject to concerns for erosion/sedimentation and the protection of lake trout habitat
