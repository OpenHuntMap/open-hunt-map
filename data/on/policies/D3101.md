## Land area description

The Sampson Lake Dedicated Protected Area is located in the northeastern portion of the Whitefeather Forest. It has both cultural and ecological significance to the Pikangikum people who have named it Paahoongwaahzhushk or "large, fanged muskrat-like animal". An outstanding wetland complex of fen, bog, and marsh, this area acts as a water filter and a headwater (water flows north, south, and west) forboth the Severn and Berens watersheds. Sampson Lake itself is situated on the south-central side of this dedicated protected area and is flanked by the northern extent of the Lac Seul Moraine. Sampson Lake is remarkable for its distinct ecological features including extensive sand beaches, and shallow, clear, spring-fed waters that support a remarkable whitefish population. First Nation people conductvarious traditional activities on this land for livelihood and cultural purposes.A winter road traverses the Sampson Lake Dedicated Protected Area and connects with McDowell Lake and North Spirit Lake First Nations to the north. There is one registered commercial boat cache; otherwise this area is undeveloped. Management boundaries that cover the area include all or part of 2 traplines, and one wildlife management unit. Opportunities may exist in the area for new eco-culturaltourism related activities.The Harling Creek Valley is located in the north and northeastern section of this dedicated protected area. This site has previously been highlighted by Ontario as having significant earth and life science features. The Sampson Lake Dedicated Protected Area supports woodland caribou that use the muskeg calving "islands". Some of the other notable wildlife in this area include: sandhill cranes, waterfowl, white pelicans, sharp-tailed grouse, and bald eagles.

## Land use intent

The intent is to regulate the Sampson Lake Dedicated Protected Area as a provincial park or conservation reserve, although the specific designation and category have not been determined. Promoting eco-cultural tourism opportunities in natural and cultural heritage appreciation is an important land use intent. The area will continue to provide support for species at risk, such as woodland caribou and wolverine. This area will also contribute to the remoteness objective for the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas Planning Area.Management will also be governed by the Land Use Strategy for the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas, MNR policies, and/or new policies developed through cross-cultural dialogue.

## Permitted uses — preface

The dedicated protected area designation (i.e. Park Class or Conservation Reserve) for Sampson Lake is yet to be determined. Interim direction for this area is provided in the Land Use Strategy for the Whitefeather Forest and Adjacent Areas (WFAA Strategy) and in the table below. Additional specific direction will be determined by the final designation assigned to this dedicated protected area through cross-cultural dialogue, and by future resource management planning.In the absence of resource management planning, new proposals for specific activities will be subject to a review by MNR in consultation with Pikangikum First Nation. This review will consider the intent of the area, direction provided in the WFAA Strategy, impacts on resources and values associated with the Sampson Lake Dedicated Protected Area, compatible economic opportunities, and potential for economicbenefit to Pikangikum First Nation.Values associated with the Sampson Lake Dedicated Protected Area include the natural and cultural heritage features of the area, special features, and existing land uses. Maintenance of natural disturbance regimes and remoteness will be given high importance as will the conservation of fish and wildlife species, including woodland caribou, and wolverine.
