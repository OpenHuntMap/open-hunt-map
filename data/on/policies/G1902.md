## Land area description

Significant waterfowl habitat areas exist in various locations in the Great Lakes and around the shoreline of a number of smaller inland lakes. In Lake Superior, the significant habitats are located at the eastern end of Batchawana Island and around the mouth of the Goulais River. In Lake Huron, 4 locations also exist: the shoreline of Lake George from Echo Bay to East Neebish Island, along the Southwest shoreline of St. Joseph Island, Portlock Marsh and Stobie Creek. Two locations in the St. Marys River are also important: Little Lake George and part of the shoreline of Squirrel Island. The designated inland lakes include Gibboney, Ottertail, Gordon, Desbarats, Grey Duck, Bocage, Walls, Madill, Round and 5 small lakes (Otter, Caufield, Deet and Twin Lakes on St. Joseph Island). The designated inland sites are Bar River Flats, Desbarats River Delta, the farmland within St. Joseph Township and Court and Richardson Points. All of the above wetland areas are important for waterfowl as staging areas and/or stop-over areas during migration periods.Coastal portions of this area are located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The general land use intent in the waterfowl areas is one of protection. Such designated areas located on Crown land will not be developed. On private land, development that will have a negative impact on waterfowl populations will be actively discouraged. If development is to occur, mitigating measures that will minimize the impact will be proposed. The waterfowl areas will be protected from external disruptions as much as possible. These disruptions can be physical, such as aquatic vegetation removal, or qualitative, such as high noise levels from residential subdivisions. Development will be discouraged in these areas. All activities will take into consideration the known nesting sites. The specific locations can be found in a compendium at the District office. No disposition of Crown land will be permitted in this area. Islands and lands under the waters of Lakes Superior, Huron and the connecting channels have been withdrawn from staking. Development proposals and dispositions, including water lots, on the Great Lakes shoreline will be reviewed for conformity with the District’s Shoreline Management Plan.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Sault Ste. Marie District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
