## Land area description

Luther Marsh Wildlife Management Area is situated in central southwestern Ontario west of the Town of Arthur in Wellington and Dufferin Counties.In 1980, MNRF designated Luther Marsh as an Area of Natural and Scientific Interest (ANSI). Significant natural and earth science features are present. It is also identified as to be a Provincially Significant Wetland and federally designated Important Bird Area.MNRF owns a small portion of the total property area. The Grand River Conservation Authority (GRCA) manage the marsh jointly. The gate house, located at the main entrance, provides an interpretive kiosk, parking and access to a network of roads. The Crown land provides opportunities for a range of recreational activities, such as hunting, hiking, snowshoeing, cross-country skiing, birding, canoeing, picnicking, boating and fishing.The Provincial Wildlife Area is located within the boundaries of the A Place to Grow: Growth Plan for the Greater Golden Horseshoe. These lands contribute to the Natural Heritage System identified under this plan.

## Land use intent

The primary goals for management of the Luther Marsh Provincial Wildlife Management Area are:· to protect the provincially significant wetlands and other significant features and species;· to optimize habitat value and benefits to wildlife;· to accommodate compatible human uses that do not impair significant features; and · to provide low flow augmentation to the Grand River.

## Permitted uses — preface

MNRF will consider the Land Use Intent and Management Direction outlined in this policy report when reviewing applications for permitted activities that require licences, leases, permits, or other forms of approval. Commercial Activities and Land and Resource Management Activities listed as permitted may require authorization under the relevant legislation (e.g., Public Lands Act, Aggregate Resources Act, Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act, etc.). The review of individual applications involves the consideration of a variety of factors and requirements on a site-specific basis, in addition to land use policy.Individuals seeking to carry out any of the permitted activities listed below should contact the local MNRF district office to find out if an authorization is required.Crown lands are managed in a manner consistent with the Constitutional protection afforded to existing Aboriginal and treaty rights.
