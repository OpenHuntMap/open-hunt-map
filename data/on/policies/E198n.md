## Land area description

This earth science area is within site district 5E-4. The Nelson Delta is a diverse landscape of glacial features that show the gradual lowering of prehistoric lake levels and the deposits of glacial meltwater. Up to 11 terraces in the huge sand and gravel deposits are visible, with each indicating a different stage in the lowering of glacial lake Algonquin. This area is important for resource sector use (forestry, aggregates, mining). Private land holdings occur within these areas and municipalities can control land use under the Planning Act. Glaciofluvial outwash deposits, glaciofluvial delta deposits, ice-contact kame moraines and eskers, and glaciolocustine beach deposits were deposited during the Pleistocene Period of continental glaciation.Glaciofluvial outwash deposits, formed by deposition of water carried materials along glaciofluvial spillways and at the terminus of spillways. The Nelson spillway is a major delta which contains large amounts of well stratified sands and gravel.

## Land use intent

The land use intent is to protect the noted natural heritage features while permitting forest management and aggregate extraction, provided that they are consistent with the protection of earth science natural heritage values. Any developmental proposals in the waterfowl nesting areas of the Vermilion River will be subject to a through review to ensure the protection of these important wetland areas.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Land Use Strategy (1999) and the Sudbury District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Natural heritage enhanced management area are intended to protect areas with significant natural values, while allowing a range of resource activities.
