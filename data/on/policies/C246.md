## Land area description

Echo River Hardwoods Conservation Reserve protects a pattern of extensive uplands and deep valleys associated with the Upper Echo River and its tributaries. It features the best example of sugar maple dominated forests, ranging from 40 to 140 years old, growing on a landscape of steep hills with shallow sands and bare bedrock in Site District 4E-3 and 5E-1. Mixedwood stands of old-growth eastern white pine have been identified in the eastern portion of the conservation reserve, and old-growth stands of black spruce, yellow birch, and eastern white cedar are scattered throughout the site. This conservation reserve's red oak mixedwood communities are considered to be provincially significant because they are near the northern limits of their range in Ontario. Crockatt Lake and its associated wetlands, in this conservation reserve, form a small watershed that ultimately feeds into the Thessalon River south of the site. Solar, Gillmor, Kinahan, Gracie, and Cassidy lakes, as well as Cassidy Creek are also included in this site. Echo River Hardwoods Conservation Reserve was regulated on April 6, 2001. It is regulated under the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act.Located approximately 35 kilometres east of the City of Sault Ste. Marie, in Chesley, Gillmor, Kane, Chesley Additional, McMahon, and Morin townships, this conservation reserve is relatively remote and inaccessible. The Ranger-Garden Lakes-Aubinadong (G1909), and Multiple Use (G1890) General Use areas surround this site.

## Land use intent

To protect the natural heritage values on these public lands while permitting compatible land use activities.

## Permitted uses — preface

The Echo River Hardwoods Conservation Reserve Statement of Conservation Interest (SCI) was approved on April 2, 2004 and should be consulted for detailed information on the site's management. Those uses and management activities not listed in the following table are governed by existing Conservation Reserves Policy of the Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act and by the general policies contained in the Ontario's Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Any new uses and commercial activities will be considered on a case by case basis, and must pass a Test of Compatibility, and comply with requirements under the Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves (2004), as well as other applicable Ministry of Natural Resources' (MNR) policies and procedures, to be acceptable. Compatibility is normally determined through a planning process.The following tables aim to present current information. However, with the passage of new legislation, updates to provincial policy and the development of protected areas management direction documents, certain content elements may be out of date.
