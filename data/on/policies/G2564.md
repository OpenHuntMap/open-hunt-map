## Land area description

This area is located to the west of Quetico Park. It is bounded on the south and west by the International Boundary, and on the north by Bullmoose Lake. The Lac la Croix Indian Reserve-25D, is located in the southwest part of the area.In 1980, an extension of the Flanders Road system provided the first access from Ontario to the area. The area is crossed by a single hydro line corridor which supplies electricity to the Lac la Croix Reservation.Present uses are cottaging, boating, canoeing, angling and resource extraction such as commercial fishing, trapping, wild rice harvesting and logging.There are cottages, commercial tourist lodges, a Ministry of Natural Resources' conservation officer station, a Canada Customs station and one commercial outpost camp in this area.There are two concerns in the area. Cottagers are concerned about the effects that may result from road access created by future forestry operations. The second concern is the exclusion of motor powered water craft from the American side of Lac la Croix which has resulted in intensive use of the Canadian waters and increased angling pressure.The following are designated as tourism lakes: Namakan, Sandpoint, Captain Tom, Redhorse, Little Eva and Little Vermillion.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The area is designated for general recreation, including a slight increase in both cottaging and tourism and controlled resource extraction, including wild rice harvesting, trapping, mineral exploration, commercial fishing and maintaining a high deer population.The high quality of the area's aesthetics will be protected. All land uses will protect sensitive areas and historical sites such as the Dawson Trail portage. The application of Area of Concern guidelines will protect recreational, tourism and important fish and wildlife values in the area.MNR will participate in any existing Forest Fire Protection Agreement with the United States Forest Service.The watershed of Quetico Provincial Park is an Area of Concern. Resource management and land use activities in the watershed will be carried out in consideration of the protection of values within the park. An Agreement of Co-existence between the Government of Ontario and Lac la Croix First Nation provides for a role for the First Nation resource management decisions affecting the portion of the area lying south of the Namakan River.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Atikokan District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
