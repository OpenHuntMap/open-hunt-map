## Land area description

The Killarney - Leech Lake Addition is situated between the northern edge of Killarney Provincial Park (P331), and the southern edge of Killarney Lakelands and Headwaters (P187). This 146 hectare area is comprised of lapsed patent mining claims that had continued as a private holding. The Leech Lake area is part of the LaCloche Range, which is characterized by white quartzite ridges. The owner donated this parcel of land to the Province of Ontario, with the intent of adding it to Killarney Provincial Park (wilderness class). However, the donor has retained ownership of the 11 hectare island on Leech Lake, which is not part of the donated land. Killarney Provincial Park is located on Highway 637, approximately 100 kilometres south of Sudbury. The Killarney area, in which Leech Lake is situated, is a vast and beautiful tract of wilderness, with crystal-blue lakes, a spectacular range of white quartzite ridges, pine and hardwood forests and boggy lowlands. Its beauty and magnificent, unspoiled scenery has inspired many artists, including members of the Group of Seven.

## Land use intent

The intent is to proceed to formal regulation via an Order in Council and regulate this area as part of the existing Killarney Provincial Park. Once regulated, these lands will be managed as an addition to the existing park (wilderness class).

## Permitted uses — preface

Management of Killarney Provincial Park is carried out in accordance with Ontario Provincial Parks Planning and Management Policies (OMNR, 1992), Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy, (1999), A Class Environmental Assessment for Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves, 2005, Provincial Parks and Conservation Reserves Act, 2006, and the Killarney Provincial Park Management Plan, 1985. The management of the park provides for day-use, camping and backcountry recreation opportunities, such as hiking and canoeing, while protecting provincially significant natural and cultural values. The preceding direction will be applied as appropriate to the recommended addition to ensure custodial management of park resources, until such time as resource management decisions are made through a planning process. Where a use may be permitted (Maybe or Yes on the table), the long-term management direction will be determined through future planning.
