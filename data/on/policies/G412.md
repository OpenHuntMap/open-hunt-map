## Land area description

This area roughly follows the Rideau River corridor. The area exhibits a high capability (Canada Land Inventory) for recreational opportunities and is identified in the Canada-Ontario Rideau, Trent-Severn Study (CORTS) as being a significant recreation corridor.Agriculture is the predominant use along the river while extensive recreation including fishing, hunting, boating, back-country hiking and cottaging occurs throughout the area. The Rideau Trail, a major hiking trail, follows the corridor. Special features include:- significant wetland areas; Tay River and Rideau River- Perth Wildlife Area (Tay Marsh)- Migratory Bird Sanctuary (Merrickville)- Mill Pond Conservation Area- Crown land open space on the Rideau River at Kilmarnock and Mouth of Tay, significant pickerel spawning areasProblems and issues:There is a high potential for conflict in this area over the regulation of water levels along the Rideau system. Conflicting demands on the resource from different users (e.g. boaters, naturalists) pose management problems. The pressure for development along the waterway causes some concern for the existing resources (land, water, fish and wildlife). Aquatic weeds are prolific in this area and are the subject of much discussion with respect to their regulation or harvesting.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The significance of this area from a recreational point of view is recognized in MNR's program emphasis. Wildlife management will focus on the protection of wetland areas as bullfrog, fur-bearer and waterfowl habitat. Fisheries management will be undertaken to protect the existing sport fishery resource and lake trout resources. General recreation opportunities will be enhanced throughout the area. Discussion: The wetland areas within this area represent the largest concentration of such a resource within the district. Two main areas are identified as significant to the fish and wildlife program; the Tay River wetland comprising some 600 hectares (1,500 acres) and the Rideau River from Rocky Narrows to Merrickville (Smiths Falls Marsh), which covers approximately 250 hectares (600 acres). The Perth Wildlife Area encompasses approximately 910 hectares (2,250 acres) including a portion of the Tay Marsh. This area is managed by the Rideau Valley Conservation Authority. The marsh is susceptible to damage from fluctuating water levels.The Migratory Bird Sanctuary, located at Merrickville, is owned by the federal government and comprises approximately 170 hectares (420 acres). It is used as a staging area by waterfowl. The Mill Pond Conservation Area comprising some 620 hectares (1,530 acres) is owned and managed by the Rideau Valley Conservation Authority. The area provides access to the Rideau system as well as general recreation facilities. The Kilmarnock and Mouth of Tay sites are Crown land holdings totaling 100 hectares (250 acres) on the Rideau River which provide open space in support of the CORTS objectives. Private land forestry is undertaken where possible and in consideration of other existing uses and resources.Specific strategies:- apply special management guidelines for the Crown land holdings on the Rideau River (Kilmarnock and Mouth of Tay) in order to maintain future options for recreational use. These areas provide public open space and access to the Rideau waterway. The Kilmarnock site has been stabilized by reforestation. The Mouth of Tay property is presently leased for agriculture related uses; - identify concerns to municipalities regarding the value of maintaining wetlands for wildlife habitat and wild rice production; and - monitor the harvest of bullfrogs and snapping turtles.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Carleton Place District Land Use Guidelines (1983).
