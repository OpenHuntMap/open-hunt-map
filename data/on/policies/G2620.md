## Land area description

This area follows the land boundary of the City of Thunder Bay and includes the harbour area between the shore and breakwall. It is highly developed with residential, industrial, commercial and service land uses. These uses are expected to expand. Some private land, not yet developed, is being used for agricultural, aggregate and woodlot purposes, but these uses may be phased out with urban expansion. The Fort William Indian Reserve lies in this area. There is very little Crown land in this area and the focus of resource management has been on the fish and wildlife resources along the urban waterways. The major issue is the lack of Crown land available to meet urban resource needs.This area is partially located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The primary role of this area is to meet the needs of this Regional growth centre. Crown resources management will focus on contributing amenities such as recreational opportunities and protection of sensitive resources to the urban landscape. This will include the protection and management of the waterways for fish and wildlife viewing, and sports angling, providing access to Lake Superior, protection of sensitive wildlife habitats such as Mission Island Marsh, offshore nesting sites and significant earth science features. These roles will be fulfilled on Crown land where possible, through land owner agreements on private land; and by encouraging the Municipality and Lakehead Region Conservation Authority (LRCA) to consider these resource concerns on their lands. MNR will provide resource information for inclusion in other agency planning reports to ensure that resource management needs are met. Working with the Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing, the Lakehead Planning Board, the City of Thunder Bay and the Lakehead Region Conservation Authority, MNR will ensure MNR hazard land policy is implemented. Agriculture, aggregate extraction, trapping and forestry will be permitted on Crown land but will be limited because of the lack of resource base and the role of providing recreation opportunities for the City. All Crown land uses including the services such as access roads will attempt to conform to official plans and zoning by-laws. One (1) Area of Natural and Scientific Interest has been identified in this area. Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in Thunder Bay District Land Use Guideline (1983)
