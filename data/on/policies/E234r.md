## Land area description

Large area containing the Mississagi River watershed and Rockly Island Lake. The area possesses a significant fly-in tourism industry and important forestry operations. Although timber operations have accessed the central portion of the area, there remain sizable remote pockets, the largest of which occurs north of the Hinkler Road.In the portion south of the Mississagi River, forest management and Crown land recreation are the major land uses in the area. Scattered throughout are a wide variety of developments and uses including outpost camps, cottages, canoe routes, and angling and hunting areas.North of the Mississagi river, the area is heavily developed for tourism purposes (outpost camps). Portions of this area are accessible by road, and further access proposals will consider the protection of remote tourism facilities. Canoeing, angling, hunting timber harvesting occur on an ongoing basis.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

This is an important remote tourism, recreation and resource sector (forestry, mining) area. Further planning will detail individual zones within this overall area to provide for the needs of remote tourism, remote recreation and resource extraction, including the consideration of the use of vehicular access controls at the west end of the Hinckler Road.Land use direction and resource management activities within this area to be compatible with protecting the recreational and limited access values of Mississagi River provincial park and its recommended additions.South of the Mississagi River, the area is to be managed primarily for foresty, together with Crown land recreation and commercial/private recreation. Due to the complexity of the land use pattern throughout this portion of the Enhanced Management Area, new proposals for development will require a detailed evaluation on a case-by-case basis to ensure compatibility with surrounding uses.North of the Mississagi River, land use will combine forest management with tourism. Outpost operations will be protected as much as possible against conflicting uses. Although the public has limited road access in to this area, MNR will not develop access facilities (access points) on lakes and rivers, and will discourage the public from doing so. Land disposition, except for tourism and forestry uses, will not be permitted, except along Highway 129 where other uses may be considered.Management of this entire area is also governer by the general policies in the Land Use Strategy (1999), the Blind River District Land Use Guidelines (1983) and the Espanola District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

Recreation enhanced management areas have been applied to areas with high recreational use of significant recreation values for activities such as angling, hunting, motorized and pedestrian trail use, and canoeing. The enhanced management area can be used for a wider range of recreation values and management intents, ranging from areas where relatively substantial recreation development is permitted, to areas providing low-density, high quality recreation in a natural setting.Where the Recreation enhanced management area has been identified to protect remote recreation values, all activities need to be carried out in such a way as to maintain or enhance the remote recreation qualities.In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of Natural lake trout lakes:­ no additional access will be permitted­ Area of Concern will be established
