## Land area description

Halfway Lake Provincial Park is located approximately 90 kilometres northwest of Sudbury, along Highway 144. This natural environment class park encompasses 5,113 hectares.Reminders of glacial activity can be seen in the high, rolling hills, the valleys partly filled with glacial soils and the uplands with their eskers, kames and moraines -- different formations of gravel and silt that were left behind when the glacial receded. East of Antrim Lake in the park is a pre-glacial boulder field, where massive rounded rocks lie in a jumble.Park forests are dominated by a few giant white pines that have survived logging and forest fires over the years. The most common tree in the park is the Jack pine. The lowland areas with their moister soils host black spruce, tamarack and balsam fir, while the dryer areas of the uplands and sandy plains have many white birch, maple and trembling aspen trees. The park also contains sphagnum bogs with abundant plant life.Recreation activities include camping, hiking, swimming and fishing.Halfway Lake Provincial Park was regulated as a provincial park in 1980.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

Halfway Lake Provincial Park will be managed according to Provincial Park policy and the park management plan (2002). The park's natural, cultural and recreational values will be protected while providing day-use and camping opportunities associated with the natural environment.
