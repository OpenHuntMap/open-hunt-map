## Land area description

This area includes most of the land area within the Marathon municipal boundary, excluding Lake Superior. Two provincial parks and part of the Lake Superior Shoreline Enhanced Management Area are located in the municipality. The area has developed as a timber harvesting and processing center. The major industry is forestry which employs a large percentage of the area's population. The area forms very small parts of the Big Pic Forest, Black River Forest and the Pic River Ojibway Forest.This area is located within the Great Lakes Heritage Coast Signature Site, one of 9 such areas featured in the Ontario’s Living Legacy Land Use Strategy (1999). Signature Sites are identified for their range of natural and recreational values and their potential to contribute to future recreation and tourism.

## Land use intent

The primary intent of this area is to provide for mixed uses such as permanent residences, industry, commercial and public services, public open space recreation, and resource extraction to allow for the continued growth of the community, subject to the official plan and zoning by-laws.Future permanent residential, commercial, tourism and service development will be encouraged within or adjacent to existing built up areas. Resource extraction activities and intensive recreational facilities within 300 meters of the shoreline of Lake Superior will be permitted only under controllyed conditions and within specific guidelines. Mileage 9 on the Pic River has been identified by Ontario Hydro as a secondary (alternate) potential hydro development site and will be protected accordingly. Any development on Crown land near the Marathon Municipal Airport must comply with Ministry of Transportation and Communications airport requirements.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Terrace Bay District Land Use Guidelines (1983).Marathon General Use Area is located in an area in which one or more First Nations have asserted Aboriginal rights and title, including an unextinguished Aboriginal right to exclusive use and occupancy of the land. The First Nations’ claims are the subject of litigation and negotiations. Dispositions in Marathon General Use Area may be subject to heightened Crown consultation and accommodation obligations.This may mean a greater degree of engagement with First Nation communities asserting rights and title prior to a decision regarding potential authorizations or dispositions of public lands or resources. For a map of areas affected by these claims, please go to the Notice of Caution on the Mining Lands Administration System (MLAS) at https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mines/lands/withreop/notices_e.html .
