## Land area description

There are four parts to this planning area. The four sub-areas are:(i) Kapuskasing Lake, which is accessible via either float plane or the railway at Elsas. It has three tourist lodges and connects together the north ends of the two branches of the Chapleau-Nemegosenda Provincial Waterway Park;(ii) the upper reaches of the Chapleau-Nemegosenda canoe route. Because it crosses private land, the south end of this canoe route was never incorporated into the adjacent provincial park. Nonetheless, it still receives heavy recreational use, both from canoeists and, on Chapleau, Henderson, and Borden Lakes, from power boaters. Both Borden and Chapleau Lakes have tourist lodge facilities and considerable cottage development. Nemegosenda Lake has two lodges;(iii) Horwood, Hoodoo, and Groundhog Lakes and the lower reaches of the Woman River at the south end of Horwood Lake - This is a popular general recreation area, especially for people from the city of Timmins. This sub-area has five lodges, two outpost camps, and over 50 cottages. General day-use recreation is prominent among land use activities in this area. Two canoe routes enter the area via the Woman River at the south end of Horwood Lake. Horwood and Hoodoo Lakes are also water storage reservoirs for Spruce Falls Power and Paper Co. hydraulic generating stations downstream on the Groundhog River;(iv) The Snake River is a popular canoeing waterway which also contains tourist outpost camps.This area contains lake(s) designated for lake trout management. For a current list of designated lakes associated with this area, refer to the Lake Trout Lake Search Index accessible via the ‘Source of Direction’ portion of this policy report.

## Land use intent

The primary use of these waterway areas, including 120 metres of shoreline, will be recreation. Further road access may be developed into the Harwood-Hoodoo-Groundhog Lake area, and upper Chapleau-Nemegosenda area, primarily to serve cottaging properties. Road access into and across the other areas will be discouraged but not prohibited. Alternatives to road crossings will be considered. Where a crossing is found necessary, it will be designed to enhance recreational values. Structural development will not be permitted in areas subject to flooding. Resource management plans will contain guidelines for the protection of significant recreation values, historical sites and fish and wildlife habitats.Management of this area is also governed by the general policies contained in the Chapleau District Land Use Guidelines (1983).

## Permitted uses — preface

In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of Natural lake trout lakes:­ no additional access will be permitted­ 120 metre Area of Concern will be applied­ forest management activities may be permitted within the Area of Concern subject to concerns for erosion/sedimentation and the protection of lake trout habitat.

## Addendum

The following lakes are designated as tourism lakes: Kapuskasing Lake, First Lake, Lebell, Spanish & Snake River Confluence. In addition to provincial and regional policies, the following local policies will apply to the management of these lakes: - Tourism will be promoted as a primary land use objective. Road access restrictions and other policies may be applied to protect these lakes from increased access.
